Why this matters

Nearly half of all hurricane deaths happen after the storm, during the rescue window, when help is too slow and too scarce.

  • Helicopters cost $10,000+ per flight hour, cover one zone at a time, and rely on a spotter whose focus lapses after about 20 minutes.
  • During Katrina, 40+ helicopters flew nonstop and still could not cover everything.
  • Hurricane Helene, Sept 2024: 108 deaths statewide. Towns in western NC went silent for 72+ hours.

HADES04 / 09
How it works

Three steps: Deploy, Detect, Coordinate.

01 / DEPLOY
Fly the sweep
A drone flies a wide-area sweep, streaming live video to a ground-station laptop over a digital video link.
02 / DETECT
Find the people
A YOLO11s model flags people on rooftops, near submerged vehicles, and in open water. Temporal aggregation across frames keeps real survivors and filters one-frame false positives.
03 / COORDINATE
Place them on a map
Our pipeline turns each detection into GPS coordinates using onboard sensors only. No LiDAR. No RTK. Locations stream to a live survivor map.
HADES05 / 09
Responsible AI

The stakes are literally life and death, so ethics is a design requirement, not an afterthought.

  • Sensitivity over silence. A missed survivor is worse than a false alarm, so we tune to flag more. The AI surfaces information; a human coordinator always makes the final call.
  • Auditing for bias. Disaster datasets underrepresent some skin tones, clothing, and lighting. We are auditing and supplementing our training data, guided by Dr. Sakaguchi.
  • No false certainty. Every detection ships with two separate confidence scores and an honest uncertainty radius. We never paint a false-precision pin.
  • Scoped deployment. Designed to operate only in officially designated disaster zones, alongside authorized agencies, never as a civilian surveillance tool.
