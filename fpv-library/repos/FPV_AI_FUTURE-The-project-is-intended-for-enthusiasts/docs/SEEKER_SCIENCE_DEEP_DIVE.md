## 1. IR Signature Phenomenology & Radiometry

### 1.1 Blackbody, graybody, and the radiometric primitives

All passive IR seeking begins with Planck's law for spectral radiant exitance from a blackbody at temperature T:

```
M_λ(λ,T) = (2π h c²) / [ λ⁵ ( exp(hc/λkT) − 1 ) ]   [W·m⁻²·µm⁻¹]
```

with h=6.626e-34 J·s, c=2.998e8 m/s, k=1.381e-23 J/K. Two textbook corollaries set the design space. **Wien's displacement law**, λ_peak·T ≈ 2898 µm·K, puts the spectral peak of a near-ambient scene (T ≈ 290 K) at ≈10 µm — squarely in the LWIR band — versus ≈3.6 µm (MWIR) only for sources near 800 K. A drone is an ambient-class emitter, so *the bulk of its self-emitted photon flux lives in LWIR*; this is the first reason an uncooled LWIR seeker is the physically correct choice for cold targets. **Stefan-Boltzmann**, M = εσT⁴ (σ=5.67e-8 W·m⁻²·K⁻⁴), governs total flux and, by differentiation, the contrast sensitivity that actually matters for detection:

```
dM/dT = 4εσT³  ≈ 4 × (5.67e-8) × 290³ ≈ 5.5 W·m⁻²·K⁻¹  (broadband, ε=1, T=290 K)
```

So a 1 K target-to-background ΔT produces only ~5.5 W/m² of broadband contrast — and the in-band (8–14 µm) fraction is the relevant part. The exponential Planck factor means contrast scales much more steeply in MWIR (dM/M per K is ~4×/T-weighted higher near 4 µm for ambient scenes), but the *absolute* in-band radiance is far larger in LWIR for cold bodies. This is the central LWIR-vs-MWIR trade for our mission, returned to in §1.3.

**Emissivity (ε)** is the multiplicative correction that turns a blackbody into a graybody: M = εσT⁴, with 0≤ε≤1 and, by Kirchhoff, ε(λ)=α(λ) (absorptivity) in thermal equilibrium. Drone-relevant materials are mostly high-ε in LWIR: painted/anodized surfaces, carbon-fibre, plastics and PCB all sit ε≈0.85–0.95; bare aluminium and polished metal are the dangerous low-ε exceptions (ε≈0.05–0.20), which both *suppress* self-emission and *specularly reflect* the cold sky, occasionally inverting a warm part into an apparent cold spot. Our seeker must treat the airframe as a **spatially mixed-emissivity object**, not a uniform graybody — relevant because 8-bit AGC will quantize away the subtle ε structure that a 16-bit radiometric core might preserve.

### 1.2 The signal chain: from ΔT to NETD-limited SNR

The quantity an uncooled microbolometer measures is band-integrated radiance contrast, expressed as an **apparent ΔT** referred to the focal plane. Sensitivity is set by NETD (Noise-Equivalent Temperature Difference): the scene ΔT that produces a signal equal to the temporal noise of one detector element. Modern uncooled VOx microbolometers achieve NETD ≈ 30–50 mK at f/1, 17 µm pitch, 60 Hz; the FT640-class core is a ~50 mK part — confirmed as the typical commodity figure for 8–14 µm uncooled FPAs ([OEMCameras LWIR](https://www.oemcameras.com/spectralband/lwir8m-14m/), [movitherm VOx FPA](https://movitherm.com/feeds/category/uncooled-vox-microbolometer)). NETD scales as:

```
NETD ∝ (F/#)² / ( √(A_d · τ_int) · (dM/dT)_band )
```

i.e. it degrades with the square of the f-number, improves with the root of detector area and integration time, and improves with in-band thermal contrast slope. The single most consequential clause for *us* is the (F/#)² term: every aperture stop roughly quadruples NETD. A bench 50 mK figure is quoted at f/1; real interceptor optics at f/1.0–1.2 are near-best-case, but any vignetting or warm-optic self-emission inflates effective NETD.

The detection SNR for a resolved-ish target is, to first order:

```
SNR ≈ (ΔT_apparent · fill_factor) / NETD_effective
```

where ΔT_apparent already folds in atmospheric transmission and the target/background contrast (§1.3–1.5), and fill_factor accounts for sub-pixel targets diluting the signal over the pixel solid angle. **This is the chain that 8-bit AGC corrupts most.** A radiometric Y16 core delivers calibrated ΔT directly; our CVBS path delivers an 8-bit, hardware-AGC, contrast-stretched *image* in which the mapping from DN to ΔT is unknown, scene-dependent, and time-varying. A 50 mK NETD sensor feeding an 8-bit AGC that auto-spans, say, a 20 K scene dynamic range yields ~78 mK per DN — *coarser than the sensor's own noise floor*, so the AGC, not the bolometer, becomes the discrimination bottleneck. We recover SNR not from radiometry but from **spatial structure, temporal persistence, and kinematic consistency** — the only invariants AGC cannot destroy.

### 1.3 LWIR (8–14 µm) vs MWIR (3–5 µm): why LWIR for us

The atmosphere is opaque across most of the thermal IR; only two windows pass: **MWIR 3–5 µm** and **LWIR 8–14 µm**, separated by the strong CO₂/H₂O absorption around 5–8 µm. The decisive facts for a low-altitude, short-range, cold-target counter-UAS engagement:

- **Photon abundance for cold bodies.** For an ambient (~290 K) drone, LWIR carries the dominant in-band exitance (peak at ~10 µm), while MWIR sees only the exponential tail. A cold target in MWIR is contrast-starved unless it has a genuinely hot feature (>400–500 K). Drones have no such feature (§1.4), so LWIR wins on raw signal.
- **Humidity tolerance at sea level.** Water-vapour continuum absorption hurts both windows, but LWIR generally passes humid, low-altitude air with less attenuation than MWIR for the short paths we care about ([LightPath LWIR vs MWIR](https://www.lightpath.com/insights/mid-wave-infrared-vs-long-wave-infrared-imaging-comparison)). The often-cited "MWIR is better in heat/humidity" result ([ScienceDirect, Beier & Gemperlein](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1350449508000376)) applies to *long* maritime/tactical ranges where MWIR's lower scattering and hotter-target contrast dominate; at our **~100–200 m** terminal ranges, transmission is ≳0.7–0.9 in either band and is not the limiting factor.
- **Cost, SWaP, instant-on.** Uncooled LWIR microbolometers need no cryocooler, draw ~1 W, and are instant-on — mandatory for an attritable FPV interceptor. MWIR sensitivity generally requires cooling.

The honest counterpoint: MWIR offers steeper per-Kelvin contrast (dM/M is higher in the Wien-tail), finer diffraction-limited resolution at equal aperture (λ smaller ⇒ smaller Airy disk), and lower path radiance. For a *long-range* CUAS sensor MWIR can win. For *our* short-range, cold-target, attritable, uncooled, passive design, LWIR is the correct and essentially forced choice.

### 1.4 The drone thermal signature: weak, small, cool, plume-less

This is the crux and the hardest physics for us. A small multirotor/fixed-wing drone is a **weak, small-area, low-ΔT, structurally-mixed** thermal source with **no hot exhaust plume** — the opposite of the jet/rocket/flare signatures military IR seekers were historically built to chase. Component-by-component plausible signature:

- **Brushless motor windings / stator:** the warmest steady feature. Copper I²R and core losses warm the stator to ΔT ≈ +15 to +40 K above ambient under sustained load; emitting area per motor is only ~3–7 cm² of partly-occluded stator, and rotation/airflow convectively cool it. Apparent ΔT at the seeker is much less than the surface ΔT once mixed with cooler airframe within a pixel.
- **ESCs / power electronics:** localized hot spots, ΔT ≈ +10 to +30 K, but tiny (≲1–2 cm²) and often internal/shadowed.
- **LiPo battery:** large thermal mass, modest self-heating under high discharge, ΔT ≈ +5 to +15 K, area ~tens of cm² — the largest-area warm feature but the coolest and slow to respond.
- **Body / airframe solar loading:** in daylight, dark composite surfaces can sit +5 to +20 K over air via absorbed solar flux — a *background-competitive* contrast that can dominate or even swamp the motor signature, and which flips sign at night.
- **Aerodynamic heating:** negligible at FPV/drone speeds (<60 m/s); no meaningful kinetic heating.

Order-of-magnitude **integrated apparent ΔT** of the whole airframe against sky is realistically only ~**2–8 K** by day and often **<2 K** at night, over a total emitting area of order **0.01–0.05 m²**. Critically, *there is no high-T, high-contrast plume*: motor temperatures (~310–340 K) are far below jet-exhaust (>800 K) or flare (~1800 K) regimes, so the steep MWIR contrast advantage never engages and IRCM-style spectral discrimination is unavailable. The signature is *just-above-clutter*, and 8-bit AGC erodes the very few Kelvin that exist. This is why our doctrine leans on **kinematics + temporal coherence + blob structure**, not absolute radiometric ΔT, for discrimination — a mitigation, never a solution.

### 1.5 Background radiance, cold-sky, and contrast inversion

Apparent contrast is target-minus-background, and the background is rarely simple:

- **Clear cold sky** is the friendly background. Zenith effective sky temperature in LWIR can be **−30 to −60 °C** (≈215–245 K) in dry conditions; near the horizon it warms toward ambient as the optical path lengthens through warm low atmosphere. Against cold sky, even a +3 K drone is a **strong positive** blob — this is the regime our DETECT estimate assumes.
- **Clouds** radiate near ambient (ε≈1, T≈air), so a drone against cloud has *near-zero* ΔT and can vanish — a known LWIR CUAS failure mode.
- **Solar-loaded ground / horizon clutter** is the hostile background: sun-heated rooftops, asphalt, foliage at +10 to +40 K create high-amplitude, structured clutter that exceeds the target's own contrast. A drone seen *against ground* in daylight can suffer **contrast inversion** — the airframe is cooler than the hot ground behind it and appears as a *cold* (negative) blob, breaking polarity assumptions in naive bright-blob detectors.

For a strapdown, look-up-biased terminal engagement we are *mostly* in the favorable sky-background regime, but the **look-down / horizon-crossing / against-cloud** geometries are where SNR collapses. Our detector must be **polarity-agnostic** (accept dark-on-light and light-on-dark) and lean on temporal/kinematic gating precisely because background contrast can invert mid-track.

### 1.6 Johnson / NVThermIP and honest range derivation

The **Johnson criteria** (1958, still the workhorse) reduce discrimination to "cycles across the critical target dimension," with N50 (cycles for 50% task probability): **detection ≈ 1.0, recognition ≈ 4.0, identification ≈ 8.0** cycles (1-D, per ([Wikipedia: Johnson's criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson%27s_criteria), [OSTI history](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1222446))). Modern **NVThermIP/TTP (Targeting Task Performance)** supersedes pure Johnson by folding in MTF, NETD-limited contrast, and the actual ΔT via the target-transfer probability function ([NATO MP-SCI-180](https://publications.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Meeting%20Proceedings/RTO-MP-SCI-180/MP-SCI-180-09.pdf)) — more accurate for sampled imagers, but Johnson is adequate for an order-of-magnitude envelope.

**Our geometry (FT640 V2):** FOV ≈ 48.7° × 38.6°, array 640×512. Instantaneous FOV (IFOV) per pixel:

```
IFOV_h = 48.7° / 640 ≈ 0.0761°/px = 1.33 mrad/px
IFOV_v = 38.6° / 512 ≈ 0.0754°/px = 1.32 mrad/px
```

so **~1.33 mrad/pixel**. Take a target critical dimension d (rotor-tip span / wingspan). One Johnson "cycle" = one line-pair = **2 pixels**. The number of resolved cycles at range R is:

```
N_cycles(R) = d / (R · IFOV) / 2     [cycles across the target]
```

Setting N_cycles = N50 and solving for range:

```
R_task = d / (2 · N50 · IFOV)
```

**Detection (N50≈1.0):** for a small quadrotor critical dimension **d ≈ 0.30 m**:

```
R_detect = 0.30 / (2 · 1.0 · 1.33e-3) ≈ 113 m
```

For a larger target **d ≈ 0.50 m**: R_detect ≈ 188 m. This **brackets our stated ~115–190 m DETECT envelope** directly from optics + IFOV + the 1-cycle criterion. Note this is the *resolution-limited* (geometric) ceiling; the *contrast-limited* range can be shorter when ΔT is small (§1.4) or the background is hostile (§1.5) — at night against cloud, contrast can cap detection well inside 100 m even though geometry permits 150 m.

**Identification (N50≈8.0):** same d=0.30 m:

```
R_ID = 0.30 / (2 · 8.0 · 1.33e-3) ≈ 14 m
```

and for d=0.50 m, R_ID ≈ 23.5 m — **bracketing our stated ~18–30 m ID envelope**. (Recognition, N50≈4, falls at ~28–47 m, between the two.) The 8× geometric penalty from detect to ID is exactly why **LOBL is doctrinally mandatory**: we cannot *identify* autonomously at the ranges where we first *detect*, so identity must be established by the operator pre-launch, and the seeker's job downstream is *track-maintenance on an already-classified blob*, not autonomous ID. The 8-bit AGC further means even the geometric R_ID is optimistic — the contrast structure needed to confirm identity is partly quantized away, which is the radiometric justification for our "AI writes only to lock-quality / engage-permission, never to centroid/LOS" invariant: the imagery is too impoverished to trust for autonomous identity decisions.

### Implications for our seeker

- **LWIR is forced, not chosen.** For ambient-temperature drones at <200 m, LWIR maximizes in-band photon flux (Wien peak ~10 µm) and tolerates humid low-altitude air; MWIR's contrast advantage only pays off against hot plumes we will never see. No regret.
- **AGC, not NETD, is our sensitivity floor.** A 50 mK bolometer behind an 8-bit hardware AGC delivers ~tens-of-mK-per-DN over typical scenes — coarser than the sensor's intrinsic noise. Treat ΔT as *uncalibrated*; never build a threshold or classifier that assumes radiometric DN→temperature stability.
- **Detector must be polarity-agnostic and contrast-robust.** Background can invert (drone-vs-hot-ground in daylight, drone-vs-cloud near-zero ΔT). Bright-blob-only logic will drop track at horizon crossings and against cloud — accept both polarities and lean on temporal/kinematic gating.
- **Range envelope is geometry-consistent but contrast-fragile.** ~115–190 m detect / ~18–30 m ID follow honestly from 1.33 mrad IFOV + Johnson N50. But the *contrast-limited* range at night/against-cloud can sit well inside the geometric ceiling — quote the envelope with the background regime attached.
- **LOBL is radiometrically mandatory.** The 8× detect→ID penalty means we physically cannot ID autonomously where we detect; operator pre-launch classification is load-bearing, and the seeker maintains track on a pre-vetted blob. This is the radiometric root of the "AI → engage-permission only, never centroid/LOS" invariant.

### Open questions / frontier

- **Empirical drone ΔT under our exact AGC chain.** The 2–8 K (day) / <2 K (night) figures are order-of-magnitude. We need bench/flight characterization of *apparent post-AGC contrast* vs aspect, load, and battery state for our specific target set — the AGC transfer function makes published radiometric numbers only loosely predictive.
- **Solar-loading sign and magnitude.** Daytime body solar loading may rival or exceed the motor signature and can dominate the blob centroid; we lack a model for how AGC renders a solar-warmed airframe vs a motor hot-spot, which matters for centroid stability.
- **Against-cloud / contrast-inversion detection floor.** What is our true contrast-limited detection range when sky background warms to ambient? This, not geometry, likely sets the worst-case engageable envelope and is currently unquantified.
- **Can blob *structure* substitute for radiometric discrimination?** With ε-structure and ΔT largely quantized away, how much classifier signal survives in shape/temporal-flicker (rotor modulation, motor hot-spot geometry)? ## 2. Uncooled Microbolometer Detector & Optics

This chapter establishes the physical floor under everything the seeker does. Every centroid, every λ-dot estimate, every lock-quality score is a function of photons that were converted to heat in a 12-µm membrane, low-pass-filtered by that membrane's thermal mass, contrast-stretched by a hardware AGC we do not control, and squeezed through an analog NTSC line. You cannot out-algorithm the detector. You can only understand it well enough to stop asking it for information it physically did not capture.

### 2.1 The microbolometer as a thermal-mass low-pass filter

An uncooled microbolometer pixel is a thermally isolated absorber (a-Si or VOx membrane on legs) whose electrical resistance is read out as a proxy for its temperature rise ΔT under incident LWIR flux. It is *not* a photon counter; it is a calorimeter. The governing equation is a single first-order thermal balance:

```
C dΔT/dt + G·ΔT = η·ΔΦ(t)
```

where C is the pixel heat capacity [J/K], G the thermal conductance to the substrate [W/K], η the absorptance, and ΔΦ the incident-power change. Two parameters fall straight out:

- **Thermal time constant** τ_th = C/G. For modern small-pitch a-Si/VOx arrays this is **~4–7 ms** for 17–35 µm pixels (ULIS/Lynred and others report 4 ms at 45 µm, 7 ms at 25–35 µm) [ULIS/ScienceDirect]. Cheaper commercial cores like the FT640 class sit at the slow end; **8–12 ms is a defensible working number** for our part.
- **Responsivity** R_v ∝ (temperature coefficient of resistance, TCR) × bias / G. Lowering G raises responsivity *and* raises τ_th — they trade against each other. You cannot make a bolometer both fast and sensitive; that is the central design tension uncooled detectors live with.

**Motion blur is the consequence we cannot escape.** The detector's temporal response is a one-pole filter with bandwidth f_3dB = 1/(2π·τ_th). For τ_th = 10 ms, f_3dB ≈ 16 Hz. Any scene feature whose image moves across a pixel faster than ~τ_th smears. Quantify it for our geometry: IFOV ≈ FOV/N_pixels. The FT640 V2 is 640×512 over ~48.7°×38.6°, so

```
IFOV ≈ 48.7° / 640 ≈ 0.076° ≈ 1.33 mrad/pixel.
```

A target crossing at angular rate ω blurs by ω·τ_th radians = (ω·τ_th)/IFOV pixels. A drone crossing at 15 m/s at 100 m subtends ω = 0.15 rad/s; over 10 ms that is 1.5 mrad ≈ **~1.1 pixels of smear** — tolerable. But our own *body* rotation, strapdown, is the dominant term: a 40°/s yaw transient (0.70 rad/s, well within an FPV airframe's authority) smears **~5 pixels**. This is why ego-rate management and gyro de-rotation matter at the detector level, not just the tracker level: blur is imposed *before* digitization and is unrecoverable. The detector itself sets a ceiling on how aggressively the airframe may slew while still presenting a sharp target. For our <0.84 g, θ_max 40° envelope this is mostly benign, but fast acquisition snaps and wind-gust transients are exactly where blur eats sub-pixel centroid accuracy.

### 2.2 NETD and its drivers

NETD (noise-equivalent temperature difference) is the scene temperature change that produces a signal equal to the temporal noise — the SNR=1 sensitivity floor. The standard definition:

```
NETD = (temporal noise voltage) / (dΔV/dT_scene)
     = V_noise / (R_v · (dΦ/dT_scene)·A_d·τ_opt / (4·F² + 1))
```

The single most important scaling law for *us* lives in that denominator: **NETD ∝ (4F² + 1)** — sensitivity scales with the square of the f-number. Halving F (f/1.4 → f/1.0) roughly **doubles** flux on the pixel and nearly halves NETD. This is why every datasheet NETD ("~50 mK class" for our part) is quoted **at f/1, 300 K, 30–60 Hz** [Lynred/ULIS]. If FT640-class optics run slower than f/1, our effective field NETD is worse than the headline number — and nobody hands us the field NETD because the analog path (next section) destroys the radiometric reference needed to measure it.

The noise voltage V_noise is the quadrature sum of:

- **Thermal (phonon) fluctuation noise** — the fundamental floor, √(4 k_B T² G·Δf). Reducing G to chase responsivity *raises* τ_th but *lowers* this noise; this is the deepest physics limit and is why uncooled NETD has a hard wall around the single-digit-mK regime regardless of readout cleverness.
- **Johnson/thermal resistor noise** √(4 k_B T R Δf).
- **1/f noise** — dominant at low frequencies, scales with bias current; it is the reason long-integration averaging gives diminishing returns and the reason **slow DC drift** appears between NUC events.
- **Photon shot noise** from the scene/body radiation exchange.

For a CUAS target this is the brutal part of the radiometry. A small drone's motor/ESC/battery hotspots may run only **2–10 K above a sky or terrain background** in LWIR; against a ~50 mK NETD that is a raw thermal SNR of 40–200 *per pixel under ideal radiometry* — comfortable. But at our DETECT range of 115–190 m the target spans only a **handful of pixels** (Section 2.6), the AGC has thrown away the absolute scale, atmospheric transmission has attenuated the differential, and the contrast against a cluttered ground or a sun-warmed roofline can collapse to single-digit apparent ΔT. NETD is necessary but nowhere near sufficient; **clutter contrast, not detector noise, is our binding constraint** beyond ~150 m.

### 2.3 Fixed-pattern noise, NUC, and the FFC shutter event

No two bolometer pixels share gain and offset. Raw FPN (spatial non-uniformity) is typically **larger than the temporal NETD** — tens to hundreds of mK pixel-to-pixel — and would swamp a small target entirely. **Non-uniformity correction (NUC)** applies per-pixel gain/offset: corrected = gain_i·(raw_i − offset_i). The offset term drifts with the focal-plane and ambient temperature (the membrane sees its own warming housing), so an uncooled core periodically closes an internal **flat-field-correction (FFC) shutter** across the array, images a uniform surface, and re-zeros the offsets [Lynred NUC literature; MDPI Sensors 16(7):1121].

For a strapdown terminal interceptor this is a real operational hazard, not a footnote:

- **FFC blackout**: the shutter event freezes/repeats the last frame for **~0.3–1 frame** (tens to ~hundreds of ms on cheap cores). During a ~1.0–1.4 s terminal "ten-tau" wall, a 200–500 ms blind window is a *large fraction of the whole engagement*. The tracker must coast (IMM predict-only) across it, and lock-quality must *know* an FFC happened so it does not interpret the frozen/garbage frame as a track loss or a confirming detection.
- **Residual NUC drift**: between shutter events, slowly growing offset gradients appear as **smooth low-spatial-frequency "ghosting"** and edge halos. This is precisely the signal structure a naive absolute-threshold detector mistakes for a warm blob — a structured false target that walks across the frame.

Mitigation in our doctrine: FFC events should be *flagged into the pipeline* (a frame-validity bit), the IMM coasts on prediction, and any AI/lock-quality model treats post-FFC frames as low-confidence. Per doctrine, none of this touches the centroid or LOS — it gates engage-permission only.

### 2.4 The 8-bit analog-CVBS + hardware-AGC path: our defining handicap

This is the chapter's load-bearing point. A radiometric core (Y16) hands you a **16-bit, per-pixel, calibrated apparent temperature** — every pixel value maps to a physical scene temperature, frame to frame, stably. **We do not have that.** The FT640 V2 emits **analog CVBS**: the core's internal hardware AGC maps the scene's instantaneous min/max into an 8-bit display-optimized video signal, that signal is **analog-encoded onto an NTSC/PAL waveform**, run down a wire, and **re-digitized by a CVBS→USB grabber** on the Pi 5. The consequences are categorical, not incremental:

1. **Absolute radiometry is gone.** Pixel value 180 in this frame and pixel value 180 in the next frame can correspond to *different scene temperatures*, because the AGC re-derived its mapping each frame from that frame's content. There is no temperature anchor.
2. **Per-frame contrast stretch (AGC pumping).** When a hot object enters or leaves the FOV — sun glint, a vehicle, the target itself growing as range closes — the AGC re-normalizes and the *entire frame's apparent brightness shifts*. A static background pixel visibly "breathes." This **AGC pumping is a first-class clutter source**: it injects global, target-correlated intensity transients that a temporal-difference detector will flag.
3. **Absolute-temperature CFAR is forbidden.** Classic IR detection sets a threshold at "background + k·σ in calibrated temperature." With no calibrated temperature and a per-frame-varying transfer curve, **any fixed DN threshold is meaningless.** We are *forced* into **relative / percentile / local-contrast thresholding** — the target must be detected as an outlier *relative to its immediate spatial-temporal neighborhood within the same frame* (local CFAR, top-hat morphology, percentile-rank, matched local contrast). This is not a preference; it is dictated by the missing radiometry. Our seeker's CFAR/MTI design choices are downstream consequences of this exact loss.
4. **8-bit dynamic range + analog noise.** Quantizing to 256 levels after AGC throws away the deep well a 14-bit ADC would preserve; analog CVBS adds its own line noise, sync jitter, chroma-subcarrier crosstalk into luma, and grabber re-sampling artifacts. Effective usable bits are often **<7**. Single-band 8-bit AGC is exactly why our discrimination is *weaker* and must be propped up by **kinematics + temporal persistence + structure**, never by absolute-temperature signatures — mitigated, never solved.

The honest framing: the analog path converts a metrology instrument into a *display device*, and we are doing science on the display output. Everything the tracker believes about "how bright/hot" the target is, is contaminated by an automatic gain loop optimizing for a human eye that isn't there.

### 2.5 Optics: f-number, MTF, diffraction, and the blur budget

The optical front end sets how many of those scarce target photons reach how few pixels, and how sharply.

- **f-number vs sensitivity** — already shown via NETD ∝ (4F²+1). Fast optics (low F) are mandatory for uncooled work; this is why LWIR lenses are physically large (Ge/chalcogenide, F≈1).
- **Diffraction limit.** The Airy radius (first null) is θ_diff = 1.22 λ/D. At λ=10 µm with a, say, ~9 mm aperture (F≈1, focal length ~9–10 mm for our FOV), θ_diff ≈ 1.22·10e-6/9e-3 ≈ **1.36 mrad** — almost exactly **one IFOV**. LWIR is fundamentally diffraction-soft: at these wavelengths the blur spot is comparable to the pixel, so we operate near the **point where diffraction and sampling meet** (roughly Nyquist-matched). We cannot resolve our way out of this with software; the diffraction PSF is baked into every frame.
- **MTF chain.** System MTF = MTF_optics × MTF_detector(pixel aperture, ~sinc) × MTF_motion(blur) × MTF_AGC/analog. Each term <1; they multiply. The detector's pixel-aperture and the analog channel's limited bandwidth both roll off high spatial frequencies, so a point target is *always* spread over ≥~1.5–2 pixels even in perfect focus. This is **bad for ID/recognition** (fine structure is gone) but **good for sub-pixel centroiding** (see 2.6).
- **Defocus** adds a roughly disk-shaped blur of angular diameter ≈ (defocus/F)/focal-length. A strapdown, fixed-focus interceptor is set to a hyperfocal-style compromise; the target is mildly soft across the engagement, which we accept.

### 2.6 IFOV, angular resolution, and the sub-pixel-centroid lever

Pixel pitch p and focal length f set IFOV = p/f ≈ 1.33 mrad here. This drives two range-band facts via Johnson/NVThermIP criteria, which specify *cycles across the target* needed for a task at 50% probability: **DETECT ≈ 1–1.5 cycles, RECOGNIZE ≈ 4, IDENTIFY ≈ 6–8 cycles** [Johnson criterion / NVThermIP, NVESD]. A cycle = 2 pixels.

For a ~0.3 m drone dimension at range Δ, pixels-on-target = 0.3/(IFOV·Δ). At Δ=150 m: 0.3/(1.33e-3·150) ≈ **1.5 pixels** — barely a detection blob, consistent with the stated **DETECT 115–190 m**. For ID at 6 cycles (12 px) you need 0.3/(1.33e-3·12) ≈ **19 m** — consistent with the stated **ID 18–30 m**. These numbers are not pessimism; they are the geometry. The detector/optics *cannot* identify a drone at 100 m; that is **fundamentally impossible** with this IFOV, not merely hard.

The redeeming lever: even a 1–3 pixel blob's **centroid can be located to ~0.1 pixel** when SNR is adequate, because the PSF spreads energy across neighbors and a weighted/intensity centroid interpolates the peak. At 1.33 mrad/px, 0.1 px ≈ **0.13 mrad of angular precision** — and *angle* is the one quantity our passive monocular seeker *can* observe. The whole guidance philosophy (null λ-dot, never scale by range) is an admission that we trade away range/identity and **bank entirely on sub-pixel angular precision**, which the spread-out PSF actually *helps* deliver. This is the rare place where a detector limitation (blur) works in our favor.

### 2.7 The latency stack

End-to-end sensor latency is the sum of: detector integration/settling (~τ_th, **8–12 ms**, with the read happening within a 60 Hz / ~16.7 ms frame) + AGC computation (a frame or a fraction) + analog NTSC encode + line transport + **CVBS→USB grabber capture and deinterlace** (often ~1 field, ~8–16 ms) + our **~17 ms/frame Pi 5 pipeline**. Realistically the *seen-to-acted* delay is **~50–80 ms**, dominated by the analog/grabber stages we did not choose and cannot easily shrink. Against a 1.0–1.4 s terminal wall, 60 ms is ~5% of the whole engagement and **one-to-two IMM cycles of pure dead time** — the tracker must explicitly compensate (predict-ahead by the measured latency), and guidance corrections must be planned with this transport delay as a known phase lag, or the λ-dot nuller will chase stale geometry near impact.

## 3. Dim-Point-Target Detection Theory

### 3.1 The unresolved-target problem and why it dominates our engagement geometry

A point target is, by definition, an object whose angular extent is smaller than the diffraction-plus-detector blur of the optics, so that its image on the focal-plane array (FPA) is *not* a resolved silhouette but a single sample of the system point-spread function (PSF) scaled by the target's collected irradiance. For our seeker this is not a corner case — it is the *common* case across most of the engagement.

The geometry sets the regime. The FT640 V2 has a 640×512 array over ~48.7°×38.6°, giving an instantaneous field of view (IFOV) of

    IFOV ≈ (48.7° / 640) ≈ 0.076°/px ≈ 1.33 mrad/px.

A small multirotor of characteristic dimension d_char ≈ 0.3 m subtends one pixel at

    R_1px = d_char / IFOV ≈ 0.3 / 1.33e-3 ≈ 225 m,

and the diffraction limit at λ = 10 µm for an aperture D ≈ 8–10 mm is λ/D ≈ 1.0–1.25 mrad — i.e. the optical blur is *itself* roughly one pixel. So from the maximum useful detect range (~115–190 m, where the target is 1–2 px) inward to roughly the "recognition" band, the drone is an **unresolved or barely-resolved point source**. Only inside the ID band (~18–30 m, where d_char spans 8–15 px) does it become a structured blob. The detection chain must therefore be optimized for the *point* problem; the structured-blob problem is a luxury reserved for the last second.

This is also why the Johnson/NVThermIP machinery is the correct ranging-down framework: detection needs ~0.75–1.0 cycle across the critical dimension, recognition ~3–4, identification ~6–8 cycles (the classic Johnson N50 values, K≈4.05 in the modern Fλ/d formulation) ([OSTI Johnson-criteria history](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1222446); [Fλ/d model, Univ. Arizona](https://repository.arizona.edu/bitstream/handle/10150/660578/023104_1.pdf)). With ~1.33 mrad pixels a single "cycle" = 2 px, so detect (~1.5 px on target) ↔ ~190 m and ID (~12–16 px) ↔ ~20–30 m, exactly matching the stated honest envelope. The point-target detector is what buys us the outer 80% of that bracket.

### 3.2 Matched filtering, the PSF-matched spatial filter, and pre-whitening

The optimal linear detector for a known signature in additive noise is the matched filter. If the target image is s(x,y) = A·h(x,y) where h is the (unit-energy) PSF and the noise/clutter has spatial autocorrelation R_n (power spectral density S_n(f)), the detection statistic that maximizes output SNR is the **pre-whitened matched filter**:

    g = h_w ⊛ I,   with   H_w(f) = H*(f) / S_n(f),

i.e. whiten the data by S_n^{-1/2}, then correlate with the (whitened) PSF. Output SNR equals the whitened signal energy:

    SNR² = ∫∫ |H(f)|² / S_n(f) df.

Two consequences matter for us:

1. **Spatial whitening = clutter suppression.** Natural LWIR backgrounds (sky gradients, cloud edges, sun-warmed terrain, building thermals) have a steeply red (1/f-like) spatial PSD: most clutter power is at low spatial frequency, while a point target is *broadband and centred on the PSF passband*. So S_n^{-1}(f) is a high-pass operation. Every clutter-suppression filter in §3.3 is, to first order, an approximation to this whitening high-pass followed by a PSF-shaped match.

2. **The PSF-matched kernel.** When clutter is locally white (post-whitening, or in benign sky), the optimum reduces to correlation with the PSF itself — a small Gaussian of σ ≈ 0.4–0.6 px for a near-Nyquist system. A 3×3 or 5×5 Gaussian correlation is therefore close to optimal *and* cheap, which matters on a single A76 core at ~17 ms/frame. The catch unique to us: the **8-bit hardware AGC** makes S_n(f) and even h(·) scene-dependent and time-varying — AGC re-maps the histogram every frame, so the "known signature" amplitude A is not stationary. We never see true radiometric energy (no Y16), so the matched filter can only be matched in *shape*, never in absolute *level*. This is the first place where the analog/8-bit choice quietly costs us optimality.

### 3.3 The clutter-suppression filter family

All of these are practical surrogates for the pre-whitened matched filter, trading optimality for robustness, locality and speed.

**White top-hat morphology.** WTH(I) = I − (I ∘ B) (image minus its morphological opening by structuring element B). The opening estimates the smooth background that "fits under" a bright peak too small to admit B; subtracting it leaves bright structures smaller than B — exactly point targets. It is a nonlinear high-pass tuned to a size, robust to slow gradients, and O(N) with a small SE. Failure mode: bright clutter edges *narrower* than B (cloud rims, hot wires) survive and generate false peaks. This is our coarse first stage.

**Max-median / max-mean (Deshpande).** Compute directional medians along 4 orientations through the test pixel and take the max (or the median of means); subtract from the centre. Medians along background-edge directions reject ridge/edge clutter that the isotropic top-hat passes — strong against linear structure (horizon, building edges, contrails). Deshpande's max-median is the canonical reference ([Deshpande et al., SPIE 3809, 1999]).

**Local Contrast Measure (LCM) and its descendants.** Human-vision-inspired: partition a sliding window into a central patch T and surrounding patches B_i; the contrast at a cell is

    C_LCM = min_i ( L_T² / m_i ),   where L_T = max gray in T, m_i = mean of patch i.

This *enhances* a bright central patch (square law on the target) while *suppressing* backgrounds where T ≈ B (ratio → 1). Variants:
- **MPCM** (Wei et al., *Pattern Recognition* 58:216, 2016) — uses the product of opposing patch differences, **MPCM = (m_T − m_i)·(m_T − m_{i+4})** summed/maxed over opposing pairs and over multiple patch scales, so it enhances *both* bright and dark targets, is sign-aware, and is parallelizable for real-time use ([Wei et al. 2016, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0031320316300358); [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016PatRe..58..216W)).
- **RLCM** (relative LCM), **WLDM** (weighted local difference measure), and multiscale relative-LCM extend robustness to size and to dim targets at the cost of more windows ([MRLCM, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323134353)).
- **DoG / band-pass.** Difference-of-Gaussians I⊛(G_{σ1}−G_{σ2}) is the linearized matched-filter-with-whitening: the narrow Gaussian is the PSF match, the wide one estimates background. Cheap, separable, but less edge-robust than max-median or MPCM.

For our system MPCM is attractive because the (m_T − m_i) *difference* form is partly invariant to the AGC's affine remap of gray levels — a per-frame gain/offset on I cancels in the difference up to the gain factor — which is exactly the kind of invariance an 8-bit AGC pipeline needs. That is the principled reason MPCM belongs in our stack alongside the top-hat.

### 3.4 CFAR theory and the threshold

After enhancement we have a contrast/residual map; we need a threshold that holds the **false-alarm rate constant** as the background level drifts (AGC, scene change, sun angle). That is CFAR. The detection rule is

    cell-under-test > T,   T = α · Ẑ,

where Ẑ is a background-level estimate from a guard-separated training window and α is set to meet a target Pfa.

- **CA-CFAR** (cell-averaging): Ẑ = mean of N reference cells. For exponential (square-law Gaussian) clutter, α = N·(Pfa^{−1/N} − 1). The −2 dB to −3 dB "CFAR loss" relative to a known-level detector shrinks as N grows.
- **GO-/SO-CFAR** (greatest/smallest-of): split the window, take max (clutter-edge robust, suppresses false alarms at transitions) or min (preserves detection near interfering targets).
- **OS-CFAR** (ordered-statistics): Ẑ = the k-th order statistic (e.g. 0.75-quantile) of the sorted reference cells. Robust to *interfering targets and outliers in the training set* — critical for us because a multi-drone scene or a hot clutter speck would bias a mean. OS-CFAR's price is a modest extra CFAR loss and a sort ([OS-CFAR in Weibull, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/236222921)).

**Clutter PDFs and α.** The multiplier depends *strongly* on tail heaviness:
- Gaussian/exponential (benign sky): α modest, e.g. for Pfa = 10⁻⁴ and N = 24, α ≈ 24·(10^{4/24}−1) ≈ 14.
- **Lognormal / Weibull / K** (terrain, cloud edges, sea): heavy-tailed, so the same Pfa needs a much larger α. For Weibull F(x)=1−exp(−(x/b)^c), small shape c (spiky) blows up the required multiplier — this is the well-documented sea/land-clutter problem ([CA-CFAR in Weibull clutter](https://www.academia.edu/121531790/); [K-distribution CFAR, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12031068/)). Assuming Gaussian tails when the real background is Weibull is the classic way to silently raise Pfa by orders of magnitude.

Our **region-adaptive CFAR** is the right structural choice: estimate the local PDF (or at least a robust scale) per region — sky vs. horizon vs. ground are radically different clutter populations, and a global threshold would be either blind over terrain or false-alarm-flooded at the horizon. Using **percentile + MAD** as the scale estimator is effectively a robust, distribution-light OS-CFAR: MAD ≈ 0.6745·σ for Gaussian but degrades gracefully under heavy tails and ignores the target/outlier cells, so the threshold doesn't self-inflate when the very target we want is sitting in the training window.

### 3.5 ROC, the SNR→Pd law, and where we operate

For a matched filter in Gaussian noise the single-look detection probability is

    Pd = Q( Q⁻¹(Pfa) − SNR ),

with SNR the *amplitude* signal-to-noise ratio at the filter output. Characteristic numbers: to get Pd = 0.9 at Pfa = 10⁻⁶ you need SNR ≈ 4.75 + 3.1 ≈ **~7.8 (≈ 18 dB)** per look; relax to Pfa = 10⁻⁴ and Pd = 0.5 and you need SNR ≈ 3.7. Every ~1 just-noticeable step in SNR moves Pd across most of its dynamic range, so detection is brutally threshold-like in SNR.

What SNR do we actually have? NETD ≈ 50 mK is the *temperature* noise floor; the relevant quantity is the apparent ΔT of the drone against its background divided by NETD, *after* AGC quantization. A sun-warmed or motor-warm drone might present ΔT ≈ 1–5 K → raw ΔT/NETD ≈ 20–100, *but* the 8-bit AGC spreads the full scene across 256 levels, so a 5 K target in a 30 K scene span occupies ≈ 5/30·256 ≈ 43 LSB — healthy — whereas a 0.5 K target in a high-dynamic-range scene may collapse to 1–4 LSB, i.e. **AGC-quantization-limited, not NETD-limited**. This is the central honest limit of the analog/8-bit path: our effective NETD at the *algorithm* input is often the AGC LSB, not 50 mK. Temporal integration helps: coherent/incoherent stacking of M frames gains √M in SNR (≈ +7 dB over 5 frames at 17 ms/frame ≈ 85 ms), which is the legitimate, doctrine-safe way we claw back the loss — kinematics + temporal + structure, never a learned centroid.

### 3.6 Sub-pixel centroiding and the Cramér–Rao bound

Once detected, the LOS feeding the IMM is the centroid, and centroid error maps *directly* into az/el state noise. Two estimators:

- **Intensity-weighted moments (centre of gravity):** x̂ = Σ I_i x_i / Σ I_i. Simple, but biased by the background pedestal and by signal truncation at the ROI edge — pedestal/background must be subtracted (the §3.3 filters do this) or the estimate creeps toward window centre.
- **Gaussian-PSF fit (ML):** fit A·exp(−|r−r₀|²/2σ²)+b. This *is* the maximum-likelihood centroider for a Gaussian PSF and attains the CRLB; it is what closes on the bound where simple CoG does not ([Optica, unbiased centroiding near CRLB](https://opg.optica.org/josaa/abstract.cfm?uri=josaa-41-2-195)).

The **Cramér–Rao lower bound** on centroid standard deviation scales as

    σ_centroid ≳ σ_PSF / SNR     (signal/shot-limited),
    σ_centroid ≳ (σ_PSF / SNR) · √(something growing with background)   (background-limited),

i.e. precision goes as 1/SNR when source-limited and worsens toward √(B)/F when background-dominated ([Astrometry CRLB, arXiv:1304.6361](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.6361)). With σ_PSF ≈ 0.5 px, hitting the **<0.1 px** target requires SNR ≳ 5 — *exactly* the detection-threshold SNR — so sub-pixel centroiding and reliable detection demand the *same* operating point. There is no regime where we detect robustly but centroid poorly, nor vice-versa, which is convenient for system design. Two caveats specific to us: (a) the CRLB assumes an *unbiased* estimator and a *known* PSF — AGC nonlinearity makes the effective PSF amplitude scene-dependent, so realized variance sits *above* CRLB; (b) at <0.1 px the dominant error is often *systematic* (pixel-phase/S-curve bias of the CoG), removable only with a Gaussian-fit or a calibrated bias correction, not with more SNR.

### 3.7 Track-Before-Detect for sub-threshold targets

When per-frame SNR is below the §3.5 threshold (dim target at 150–190 m, or a momentary AGC collapse), single-frame detection *fundamentally* cannot deliver Pd — Pd ≈ Pfa. The escape is **Track-Before-Detect (TBD)**: defer the thresholding decision and integrate the (sub-threshold) signal *along hypothesized trajectories* over M frames, declaring only after accumulation. Variants:

- **Dynamic-programming TBD** (Barniv/Arnold): a Viterbi-like search over the state grid, accumulating a value function along motion-consistent paths; near-optimal, deterministic cost, but the grid is expensive.
- **Recursive Bayesian / particle TBD**: propagate a posterior over (position, velocity, intensity) with the raw (un-thresholded) frame as measurement likelihood; particles concentrate on true tracks. Handles maneuver and unknown intensity, at heavy compute.
- **Multi-frame hypothesis accumulation** (a practical middle ground): a 3-D matched filter in (x,y,t) tuned to plausible image-plane velocities.

TBD's theoretical payoff: it converts the single-look SNR-7.8 wall into an M-look problem, recovering ~10·log₁₀(M) dB and pushing usable detection ~1.5–2× further in range for a given Pd. **Our constraints sharply bound which TBD is admissible.** One A76 core at 17 ms/frame cannot run a full particle-TBD posterior; and — doctrinally — TBD must remain a *detection/lock-quality* function, never something that writes the centroid or LOS. A lightweight, motion-gated multi-frame accumulator (a few image-plane velocity hypotheses consistent with the IMM's predicted az/el-rate, accumulating top-hat/MPCM residuals over ~5 frames) is the realistic, doctrine-safe form: it raises lock-quality / engage-permission, and only a *supra-threshold* peak is ever handed to the centroider and tracker.

### 3.8 How the pieces compose in our stack

Our pipeline — **white top-hat → percentile/MAD region-CFAR → MPCM → (motion-gated temporal accumulation) → robust centroid → IMM** — is a coherent, near-textbook realization of point-target detection theory under our hard constraints:

- top-hat = nonlinear, size-tuned high-pass ≈ the whitening prefilter;
- percentile/MAD region-CFAR = robust, distribution-light, region-adaptive OS-CFAR holding Pfa constant across sky/horizon/ground despite AGC drift;
- MPCM = AGC-affine-robust contrast enhancement that is the closest practical surrogate to a multiscale matched filter and the one discrimination lever we have in a single 8-bit band;
- temporal accumulation = the √M SNR recovery that buys outer range;
- Gaussian-fit-grade centroid = the only estimator that reaches <0.1 px and is *bias*-clean enough for the angular IMM.

The architecture's honesty is that none of this manufactures the missing radiometric information; it manages it.

### Implications for our seeker
- **Operating point is single-valued.** Robust detection (SNR ≳ 7.8 for Pd 0.9 @ Pfa 1e-6) and <0.1 px centroiding (σ_PSF/SNR) demand the *same* SNR ≳ 5–8, so we should size the whole chain to one threshold and stop treating detection and centroid accuracy as separate budgets.
- **The real noise floor is the AGC LSB, not 50 mK NETD.** In high-dynamic-range scenes a dim drone can collapse to 1–4 LSB; design and test against AGC-quantization-limited SNR, and prefer difference-form operators (MPCM, top-hat) that are partly invariant to the per-frame affine remap.
- **Use OS/MAD, never mean, for the CFAR scale** — region-adaptive percentile/MAD prevents the target and clutter spikes from inflating their own threshold, and degrades gracefully when the background is Weibull/K rather than Gaussian.
- **Centroid with a Gaussian-PSF ML fit, not bare center-of-gravity**, and apply a pixel-phase (S-curve) bias correction; at <0.1 px the limit is systematic bias, not SNR, and CoG will not get there.
- **TBD is allowed only as a lock-quality lever.** A motion-gated, IMM-rate-consistent multi-frame accumulator over ~5 frames (~85 ms) recovers ~+7 dB and extends usable range, but must never write centroid/LOS — consistent with the "AI writes only to engage-permission" invariant.

### Open questions / frontier
- **Empirical clutter PDF of *our* scenes.** We assume Weibull/K-like tails at the horizon, but we have not measured the post-AGC residual PDF for FT640 imagery across sky/horizon/ground/sun-angle; the correct region-CFAR α's are unknown until we do.
- **AGC forward model.** Can we estimate the per-frame AGC gain/offset (from frame statistics or a reference patch) and partially invert it to recover quasi-radiometric residuals, restoring matched-filter optimality without a Y16 sensor?
- **Affordable near-optimal TBD on one A76 core at 17 ms.** What is the best Pd-vs-compute frontier for a motion-gated DP/accumulator TBD that stays inside the frame budget and the doctrine boundary?
- **CRLB realizability under AGC.** How far above the ideal CRLB does our realized centroid variance actually sit once AGC nonlinearity and 8-bit quantization are included — and does that gap ever threaten the <0.1 px LOS-rate noise the λ̇-nuller can tolerate?
- **Single-band discrimination ceiling.** With one 8-bit LWIR band, what is the information-theoretic limit on rejecting birds/thermals/decoys using only kinematics + temporal + structure, and where exactly does that ceiling fall inside our 115–190 m detect bracket?
- **MPCM scale/parameter adaptation** to the changing on-array target size (1 px at 190 m → 15 px at 20 m) without a learned component touching the centroid — i.e. a deterministic, range-free multiscale schedule driven only by the IMM's angular-rate state.

## 4. Clutter Suppression, Ego-Motion & MTI

Terminal homing on a small drone is, at the signal level, a search for a few-pixel warm blob embedded in a *moving, structured, heavy-tailed* background. Every frame our FT640 hands us is the superposition of three things: the target signature, a fixed-pattern/AGC artifact field, and a *scene* that is itself sliding across the focal plane because the interceptor is translating and rotating at tens of degrees per second. Clutter suppression is the problem of separating target motion from this ego-induced background motion; Moving-Target Indication (MTI) is the dominant tool. This section develops the physics of the clutter regimes, the geometry of ego-motion-induced image flow, the registration/warp-and-difference pipeline, and the *parallax wall* that fundamentally bounds what monocular MTI can ever achieve — then ties it to our specific gyro-de-rotation + ORB-RANSAC + N-of-M architecture.

### 4.1 Two regimes: look-up vs look-down

The single most important scene descriptor is the elevation of the LOS relative to the horizon, because it switches the clutter statistics by orders of magnitude.

**Look-up (sky background).** Above the horizon the background is cold and smooth. A clear LWIR sky has apparent temperature far below ambient — effective sky radiometric temperatures of −40 °C to −60 °C are routine at zenith in the 8–14 µm window, rising toward ambient near the horizon (the sky-temperature gradient ∝ airmass ≈ sec(zenith angle)). A 300 K drone against a 230 K sky yields apparent ΔT of tens of kelvin, so the *target contrast* problem is easy. The clutter is dominated by:
- **Cloud edges.** A cumulus edge is a near-step in radiance with a spatial high-frequency content that *looks exactly like a point target after a high-pass filter*. The literature on IR small-target detection repeatedly identifies high-brightness cloud edges and their boundaries as the dominant false-alarm source in sky scenes ([Kim 2014, region-adaptive clutter rejection](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4168490/); [Bae 2018, false-alarm-aware multiscale](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1350449517302013)). Crucially, cloud edges are *moving* in the image both from ego-motion and from their own advection (wind, 5–15 m/s), so they survive naive frame differencing.
- **Birds.** A bird at range has the same few-pixel circularly-symmetric blob signature and a comparable apparent ΔT to a drone; birds are explicitly called out as having "similar specifications to small infrared targets." This is a discrimination problem our single 8-bit AGC band cannot solve radiometrically — it must be deferred to kinematics and structure (Section ties below).
- **The Sun and solar-heated structure.** The Sun in LWIR is overwhelmingly bright; direct or near-direct solar ingress saturates the AGC and can pull the whole frame's dynamic range, suppressing target contrast for tens of frames.

**Look-down (ground clutter).** Below the horizon the background is warm, textured, and *three-dimensional* — buildings, trees, terrain, roads, thermal cross-over patches. Two qualitatively new problems appear:
- **Clutter amplitude and heavy tails.** Ground-clutter radiance is not Gaussian; its single-look amplitude distribution is heavy-tailed (Weibull/log-normal/K-distributed), so a CFAR threshold set for Gaussian statistics over-generates false alarms by orders of magnitude. The number of clutter exceedances grows super-linearly as the threshold is lowered. This is the same statistical pathology that forces region-adaptive CFAR in sea/ground IRST.
- **Sun-glint and solar-glint-on-water.** Specular returns from water, glass, wet surfaces, and vehicle bodies produce point-like, circularly-symmetric, high-intensity blobs that are *geometrically indistinguishable from a target in a single frame* — the literature notes sun-glint "has a similar shape (circular symmetry) to small targets and a high intensity value." Glints also *twinkle*: they appear and disappear as the specular condition is met and broken by ego-motion and water-surface slope statistics, which defeats simple temporal persistence and *mimics a maneuvering mover*.

For our seeker the operational implication is stark: **the honest detect/ID envelope (DETECT 115–190 m, ID 18–30 m) is a look-up, near-head-on figure.** Look-down engagements move us into the heavy-tailed regime where the same algorithm produces a far higher false-alarm rate at fixed threshold, and where parallax (4.4) is maximal. Our default-deny doctrine is the correct response: when the background regime degrades, the right move is to *raise the lock-quality bar and PASS*, not to push detection sensitivity.

### 4.2 Ego-motion: how interceptor motion paints the focal plane

Image motion of a static scene point under camera motion decomposes exactly into a **rotational** part (independent of scene depth) and a **translational** part (inversely proportional to depth). For a calibrated camera with focal length f (pixels), a point at image coordinates (x, y) and scene depth Z, with body angular rate ω = (ωx, ωy, ωz) and translational velocity V = (Vx, Vy, Vz), the optical-flow velocity (u, v) is the classic Longuet-Higgins–Prazdny field:

```
u = -f·ωy + ωz·y + (ωx·x·y)/f - (ωy·x²)/f      ← rotational (depth-free)
    + (-f·Vx + x·Vz)/Z                          ← translational (∝ 1/Z)
v = +f·ωx - ωz·x + (ωy·x·y)/f - (ωx·y²)/f
    + (-f·Vy + y·Vz)/Z
```

Two facts dominate everything that follows:

1. **Rotation is depth-independent; translation is not.** The rotational terms depend only on ω and pixel position — they are a *global parametric warp* with no scene knowledge required. The translational terms carry the 1/Z factor — they are the *parallax* and cannot be removed without depth.

2. **Rotation dominates by a large factor for a strapdown interceptor.** With our ~48.7° × 38.6° FOV over ~640 px, f ≈ 640/(2·tan(24.35°)) ≈ 705 px. A modest body rate of ω = 30 °/s = 0.52 rad/s produces a *full-field* image shift of f·ω ≈ 705 × 0.52 / (frame rate). At our measured ~17 ms/frame (~58 fps if grabber-limited, but CVBS field rate ~50/60 Hz), per-frame rotational shift is on the order of f·ω·Δt ≈ 705 × 0.52 × 0.017 ≈ **6 px/frame**, and far more during a guidance correction. By contrast, the translational/parallax displacement of a background object at Z = 100 m with a 5 m/s lateral closing component over one frame is f·V·Δt/Z ≈ 705 × 5 × 0.017 / 100 ≈ **0.6 px/frame**. So ~90% of the background motion is rotational and *removable in closed form*; the residual ~10% is the parallax problem we cannot fully kill.

This is the central design lever: **kill the big, easy, depth-free rotational component with the gyro; then fight the small, hard, depth-dependent residual statistically.**

### 4.3 Image-domain registration and its failure modes

Three families of registration, in increasing robustness-on-sky:

**(a) Feature + RANSAC homography (ORB/AKAZE → RANSAC).** Detect corners, match descriptors, fit a global homography H with RANSAC to reject outliers (independently-moving objects, mismatches). A homography is *exact* only for a planar scene or a pure rotation — which is precisely the look-up case, making it theoretically ideal for sky-background MTI. The failure mode is **low texture**: ORB needs corners, and clear sky has none. With < ~20–30 inliers the homography is ill-conditioned and RANSAC will happily fit a model to noise or, worse, to the moving target plus a cloud edge. The literature is explicit that "low-texture regions like sky areas present difficulties for traditional feature detection," and that the aperture problem compounds this ([ORB+LK registration](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN104751465A/en)). For us this means ORB-RANSAC is strong exactly when the scene has a horizon or cloud structure in frame, and *degrades precisely in the clean-sky case* — which is, perversely, also the case where the target is easiest to see. The mitigation is to *gate* on inlier count and condition number and fall back to gyro-only when the fit is weak.

**(b) KLT / Lucas-Kanade optical flow.** Track sparse points by minimizing brightness-constancy residual; estimate a parametric warp from the tracks. Same Achilles heel — the **aperture problem**: on a textureless or 1-D-textured patch (a clean cloud edge gives only the normal-flow component), the flow is unobservable along the edge direction, and the brightness-constancy assumption is broken outright by our hardware AGC, which *changes the gain frame-to-frame* (illumination/gain non-constancy is a known KLT failure). For an 8-bit AGC CVBS stream, global brightness constancy is violated by construction whenever the AGC re-normalizes — a strong argument against pure photometric registration for us.

**(c) Gyro-aided / gyro-only de-rotation.** Integrate the body-rate gyro to get the inter-frame rotation R, and warp the previous frame by the induced homography H = K·R·K⁻¹ (pure-rotation homography, the conjugation of the rotation by the camera intrinsics K). This is *depth-free, texture-free, and AGC-immune* — it needs no scene content at all. It is exactly the right primitive for the clean-sky case that breaks (a) and (b). Published gyro-only video stabilization for long-range EO surveillance achieves sub-pixel to low-single-digit-pixel residuals when the gyro is well-calibrated and time-synchronized ([Albrektsen 2021, gyroscope-based stabilization](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8473288/)). Its limits are: (i) it removes *only* rotation — translation/parallax remain by definition; (ii) residual error is set by **gyro bias drift, scale-factor error, and timing/sync skew** between the IMU and the CVBS field. The dominant error is usually *not* white noise (MEMS angle-random-walk is small over one frame) but **time-sync skew**: a τ_sync ms misalignment against a body rate ω produces a registration error of f·ω·τ_sync. At ω = 30 °/s and f = 705 px, even 2 ms of sync error is f·ω·τ ≈ 705 × 0.52 × 0.002 ≈ **0.7 px** of bias — and during a hard guidance correction (ω can spike far higher) this blows up linearly. Sync and scale-factor calibration, not noise, are the things to fund.

**The right architecture for us is hybrid and gated:** gyro de-rotation as the always-available coarse prior, *refined* by ORB-RANSAC when sufficient inliers exist (horizon/cloud/ground in frame), with the photometric/KLT path distrusted because of AGC. This matches our stated stack.

### 4.4 MTI by warp-and-difference, and the parallax wall

The MTI primitive is: register frame t−1 to frame t with warp W, then difference D = I_t − W(I_{t−1}); true movers produce a residual signature where the background cancels. Two failure mechanisms:

- **Registration error** produces *edge ghosts*: a mis-registration of δ pixels against a background gradient ∇I leaves residual ≈ δ·|∇I|. High-contrast edges (cloud boundaries, horizon, building rooflines) are exactly where the largest false residuals appear — the diff image is dominated by mis-registered structure. This is why differencing must be **registration-error-aware**, thresholding relative to local gradient, not a global constant.

- **Parallax** is the fundamental one. Even with *perfect* rotation removal, the translational term (−f·Vx + x·Vz)/Z is non-zero for any 3-D background and *cannot be cancelled by any single global warp*, because the warp would need a different displacement at every depth Z. So a single-homography warp-and-difference over a 3-D ground scene generates **false movers at every depth discontinuity** — the tops of buildings, tree lines, terrain ridges. The aerial-MTI literature states this plainly: "regions on the ground occluded by 3-D structures will gradually change… relative movement of image structure will lead to false alarms," and single-motion-model methods "cannot always overcome the parallax error problem," producing high false-alarm rates "at buildings and trees" ([Teutsch/Krüger; Multi-Model aerial MTI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4431192/)). The parallax false-mover amplitude scales as f·V_perp·Δt·(1/Z_near − 1/Z_far): it is **worst at low altitude, look-down, high crossing speed, and large depth spread** — i.e., exactly the terminal phase of a ground-background engagement.

**Mitigations that do not require depth (and that we should lean on):**

1. **Multi-interval / Δt-intersection.** A *true* mover has a velocity; its image displacement scales linearly with the differencing interval Δt. A *parallax false mover* depends on geometry, not on Δt in the same way, and a registration ghost is largely Δt-independent. Computing MTI at two or more intervals and keeping only detections whose displacement is **consistent across intervals** (intersects under the true-motion hypothesis) rejects much parallax and most ghosts. This is the multi-frame / accumulative-differencing idea (AMFD) from aerial MTI.

2. **Trajectory continuity / N-of-M.** A real mover traces a smooth, kinematically-feasible track; parallax ghosts and glints flicker incoherently. Requiring a detection to persist and move consistently over N of the last M frames is a powerful, depth-free discriminant. With per-frame false-alarm probability p_fa, an N-of-M rule drops the effective rate to ≈ Σ_{k≥N} C(M,k) p_fa^k (1−p_fa)^{M−k}; for p_fa = 0.1, a 3-of-5 rule gives ≈ 8.6×10⁻³ — nearly two orders of magnitude of suppression at the cost of N−1 frames of latency (~N×17 ms ≈ 50 ms for 3-of-5; well inside our 1.0–1.4 s ten-tau wall). The cost is **latency vs. clutter rejection**, and against a *maneuvering* target the continuity model itself can reject the truth — which is why our honest envelope is < ~0.28 g.

### 4.5 The fundamental limit: monocular MTI vs. true 3-D

What is *impossible* vs. merely *hard*:

- **Impossible (information-theoretic):** From a single moving monocular camera you cannot separate a *slow background parallax mover* from a *slow real mover* in the general case, because translational image flow is f·V/Z — a **velocity-depth ambiguity**. A near, slow background feature and a far, fast real target can produce identical image flow. This is the image-domain face of our system-level range-unobservability: monocular flow gives you *bearing-rate*, never *range* or *closing speed*, so any MTI decision is fundamentally a bearing-domain decision. A stereo or structured-3-D system breaks this by measuring Z directly (disparity → depth → exact parallax prediction → clean residual); we have no such instrument and never will on this platform.

- **Hard but tractable:** Suppressing rotation (gyro), suppressing planar/sky background (homography), and suppressing *incoherent* parallax/glint/ghost false movers (multi-interval + N-of-M continuity). These exploit the *statistical* difference between coherent target tracks and incoherent clutter, and they work — at a false-alarm rate that is bounded below by the parallax floor of the scene, not by our algorithm cleverness.

The honest framing: monocular MTI gets us from "millions of clutter exceedances" to "a handful of candidate tracks," but it cannot reach the zero-false-mover floor of a true 3-D sensor. The residual is paid down by *fusing* MTI with the angular IMM tracker (a candidate that produces a smooth modified-polar [az, el, aż, el̇] track is trusted; one that doesn't is dropped) and by our doctrine of pushing all uncertainty toward PASS.

### Implications for our seeker
- **Gyro de-rotation must be the always-on primitive; ORB-RANSAC is a gated refinement.** Sky (clean look-up) is where ORB *fails* and gyro *wins*; horizon/look-down is where ORB earns its keep. Switch on live inlier-count + condition-number gates, never run feature-only.
- **Time-sync and scale-factor between IMU and CVBS field are the dominant de-rotation error — not gyro noise.** Budget for it: at 30 °/s, 2 ms of skew ≈ 0.7 px of registration bias, ghosting every high-contrast edge. Calibrate and timestamp at the field, not the frame.
- **Distrust photometric/KLT registration because of the hardware AGC.** Frame-to-frame gain re-normalization breaks brightness constancy by construction; lean on geometric (gyro/feature) warps and difference *relative to local gradient*, not a global threshold.
- **Make look-down a lock-quality demerit, not just a detection challenge.** Heavy-tailed clutter + maximal parallax = elevated p_fa at fixed threshold. Per doctrine, raise the bar and PASS; do not raise detector sensitivity to compensate.
- **N-of-M continuity + multi-interval Δt-intersection are our parallax/glint backstop, and they fit the ten-tau budget.** 3-of-5 buys ~2 orders of FA suppression at ~50 ms latency. MTI writes only to candidate/lock-quality; the IMM angular track, never the centroid, is the final arbiter.

### Open questions / frontier
- **Quantify our actual parallax floor in representative look-down terminal geometry.** What residual false-mover rate survives gyro + single-homography + 3-of-5 at 50–150 m over urban/tree clutter? This is empirical and unmeasured for our optics/altitudes.
- **Plane-plus-parallax or layered/multi-homography MTI on a single A76 core within 17 ms** — can we afford a second motion layer to model the dominant ground plane and flag off-plane residuals, or does it blow the frame budget?
- **Glint/twinkle rejection without killing genuine target scintillation.** Specular twinkle and a glinting/rotating drone airframe can look alike at our resolution; is there a temporal-spectral signature (flicker statistics) that separates them in a single 8-bit band?
- **Gyro-MTI cross-calibration online.** Can registration residual itself be used to estimate and correct gyro scale-factor/sync drift in flight (self-calibrating de-rotation), closing the loop without bench calibration?
- **Bird vs. drone discrimination is unsolved in-band.** With no radiometry and few pixels, how much does *kinematic* signature (wingbeat micro-motion, track smoothness, achievable accelerations) buy us, and can it be made robust enough to gate engage-permission?
- **Where exactly is the maneuver wall for our continuity backstop?** The 0.28 g envelope and the N-of-M continuity model interact: a tighter continuity gate rejects more clutter but lowers the maneuver tolerance. The Pareto front between FA-rate and maneuvering-target retention is uncharacterized.

## 5. Angle-Only Tracking & Observability

### 5.1 The problem and what "passive monocular" costs us

A bearings-only tracker (BOT) — equivalently angle-only tracking (AOT) in 3-D, or target-motion analysis (TMA) in the sonar literature — estimates a relative target state from a time series of line-of-sight (LOS) angles alone. For our Block-3 interceptor the measurement at frame *k* is the 2-vector

```
z_k = [az_k, el_k]ᵀ + v_k ,   v_k ~ N(0, R),   R = diag(σ_az², σ_el²)
```

extracted as the de-rotated centroid bearing of the drone blob. There is **no range channel, no Doppler, no second aperture, no gimbal-parallax baseline**. The relative geometry is

```
az = atan2(y, x),   el = atan2(z, √(x²+y²))
```

where (x,y,z) is the target position in the body/inertial frame relative to the seeker. The map from the 3-D relative position to the 2-D angle pair throws away exactly one degree of freedom: range *r = ‖(x,y,z)‖*. Scaling the entire relative geometry by any λ > 0 — target *and* its velocity, at constant observer velocity — produces an **identical** angle history. This scale invariance is the geometric root of range unobservability and it is not a filter deficiency; it is a property of the observation manifold.

### 5.2 Observability: why range is unobservable for a constant-velocity observer

Take the standard relative-Cartesian state `x = [Δx, Δy, Δvx, Δvy]ᵀ` (2-D for clarity; the 3-D argument is identical with an extra elevation row). With both target and observer moving at constant velocity the dynamics are linear, `ẋ = Fx`, and the only nonlinearity is the bearing map `h(x) = atan2(Δy, Δx)`. Linearizing, the measurement gradient is

```
H_k = ∂β/∂x = (1/r_k²)·[ −Δy_k,  Δx_k,  0,  0 ]
```

**Nonlinear observability** is governed by the rank of the stacked Lie-derivative / observability matrix `O = [H₀; H₁F; H₂F²; …]`. For a **non-maneuvering (constant-velocity) observer**, Nardone & Aidala (1981) proved the result by reducing the bearing evolution to a third-order nonlinear ODE: the system is **unobservable** — the state cannot be uniquely determined from any finite or infinite bearing history. The intuition is the scale invariance above: there is a one-parameter family of target trajectories (the whole "range ray") that fit the bearings exactly. The **necessary and sufficient observability condition** is that the **observer must out-maneuver the target in a sense that changes the bearing geometry** — concretely, the observer's acceleration must have a component that is not collinear with the LOS, i.e. the relative trajectory must not remain a straight line in the bearing-rate sense. A constant-velocity observer against a constant-velocity target has bearing rate that is itself a smooth monotone function and yields a **rank-deficient** observability matrix (rank 3 of 4 in 2-D: the inverse-range direction is the null space) ([Nardone & Aidala, *IEEE T-AC* 1981](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Observability-Criteria-for-Bearings-Only-Target-Nardone-Aidala/3e40ae620c1a3619efb1c84a4fb228727b5f28d4)).

**Fisher / Gramian view.** The same fact is the singularity of the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM). For a bearing sequence with per-sample variance σ²,

```
FIM  J = Σ_k (1/σ²) H_kᵀ H_k = Σ_k (1/(σ² r_k⁴))·[Δy²  −ΔxΔy …;  …]
```

Because every `H_k` is, up to scale `1/r_k²`, the *unit LOS-normal* projected onto the same low-dimensional bearing manifold, the `H_kᵀH_k` terms for a straight-line relative geometry all live in the same subspace; their sum is rank-deficient along the range/range-rate direction. The Cramér-Rao lower bound `CRLB = J⁻¹` therefore has an **eigenvalue → ∞** along inverse-range: the variance of any unbiased range estimate is unbounded. This is the formal statement that **range CRLB → ∞** for a constant-velocity observer — exactly the condition we declared in our doctrine. In continuous time the corresponding object is the **observability Gramian**

```
W_o(t₀,t₁) = ∫ Φᵀ(τ,t₀) Hᵀ(τ) R⁻¹ H(τ) Φ(τ,t₀) dτ
```

(Φ the state-transition matrix). Observability ⇔ `W_o` nonsingular; the **smallest eigenvalue of W_o sets the achievable range accuracy**, and for our geometry that eigenvalue is ~0.

**Observer maneuver restores *partial* observability.** When the observer accelerates with a non-LOS component, successive `H_k` rows span a higher-dimensional subspace, `O` (and `W_o`, and the FIM) become full rank, and range becomes weakly observable. The *quality* scales with how much the maneuver bends the bearing history: practically, range error scales roughly as `σ_r/r ∝ σ_β / (b·Δβ_geom)` where `Δβ_geom` is the *extra* bearing change induced by the leg change and `b` the maneuver baseline as a fraction of range. Two consequences for us:

- The maneuver must be **lateral** (cross-LOS). A maneuver *along* the LOS — pure acceleration toward the target — produces *zero* extra bearing information; the system stays unobservable even though the observer is "maneuvering." This is the classic trap noted in the BOT literature ([Nardone & Aidala 1981](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Observability-Criteria-for-Bearings-Only-Target-Nardone-Aidala/3e40ae620c1a3619efb1c84a4fb228727b5f28d4)).
- **Our seeker deliberately does the opposite.** A LOS-rate *nuller* drives the engagement toward a collision triangle, where bearing rate → 0 and the relative trajectory is a straight line — the **maximally unobservable** geometry. We are not failing to estimate range; we are flying the trajectory that destroys range information *on purpose*, because that same straight-line, zero-λ̇ condition is the kinematic definition of intercept. This is the deep reason the doctrine forbids range from ever scaling the guidance gain: there is no consistent range to scale by, and the optimal-intercept trajectory is precisely the one along which range is least observable.

### 5.3 Coordinate choice: Modified Polar / Modified Spherical Coordinates

The single most important *engineering* lesson of BOT is that **coordinate choice decides whether the filter survives**. A Cartesian EKF on `[Δx,Δy,Δvx,Δvy]` is notoriously unstable for BOT: the observable subspace (bearing, bearing-rate) and the unobservable subspace (range, range-rate) are *mixed* across all four Cartesian states, so the EKF's covariance update tries to inject information into the unobservable directions. The result is **premature covariance collapse / ill-conditioning** — the filter becomes overconfident along range, the linearization point drifts, and the estimate diverges (the well-documented Cartesian-EKF instability).

Aidala & Hammel (1983) solved this with **Modified Polar Coordinates (MPC)**. The MPC state in 2-D is

```
y = [ β,  β̇,  ṙ/r,  1/r ]ᵀ
```

— bearing, bearing-rate, range-rate-over-range, and **inverse range**. The 3-D generalization, **Modified Spherical Coordinates (MSC)**, carries `[az, el, az-rate, el-rate, ṙ/r, 1/r]`. The decisive property: **MPC/MSC automatically decouples the observable from the unobservable component.** The first three (2-D) / five (3-D) states are observable from bearings *without* observer maneuver; the **entire unobservability is isolated in the single state `1/r`** ([Aidala & Hammel, *IEEE T-AC* 1983](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Utilization-of-modified-polar-coordinates-for-Aidala-Hammel/b7a06ada714a420c6678f9b251968ce000d3c8d8)). Because the measurement is *linear* in the MPC state (`z = [β] = H y` with `H = [1,0,0,0]`), the measurement update touches only observable directions, the covariance does **not** collapse into the `1/r` direction, and the filter is **stable and asymptotically unbiased** without an observer maneuver. Comparative studies confirm EKF-MSC and UKF-MSC give the best accuracy at modest cost, the UKF-MSC marginally ahead ([Mallick et al., angle-only filtering in MSC/LSC](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224251968_Angle-only_filtering_in_3D_using_modified_spherical_and_log_spherical_coordinates)).

**Our state is a deliberately reduced MSC.** Our IMM runs a **4-state modified-polar angular state `[az, el, az_rate, el_rate]`** — i.e. we keep *only* the observable block of MSC and **drop `ṙ/r` and `1/r` entirely**. This is the honest engineering move: rather than carry an inverse-range state we cannot inform (and risk it poisoning the angular estimate through cross-covariance), we **delete the unobservable subspace from the model**. The filter then estimates exactly what the data support — two angles and two angular rates — and nothing it cannot. Gyro de-rotation supplies the ego-motion (`λ̇_inertial = λ̇_measured − ω_body` projected to the LOS frame) so that the angular rates are *target-relative*, which is what the LOS-rate nuller consumes. We pay one price: without `1/r` we cannot form the kinematic coupling that links angular acceleration to lateral target acceleration, so a maneuvering target's `az̈/el̈` must be absorbed by process noise / model switching rather than by a range-scaled term. That is acceptable because the guidance never needs range, only λ̇.

### 5.4 Filter families for BOT, and why we land where we do

- **Cartesian EKF** — simplest, but unstable for BOT (above). Avoid.
- **MPC/MSC EKF (Aidala-Hammel)** — stable, cheap, asymptotically unbiased; the reference design. Our choice (reduced form).
- **UKF in MSC** — better handling of the bearing-map nonlinearity at high angular rates, no Jacobians; ~2–3× the cost of an EKF (2L+1 sigma points, L≈4–6). Marginal accuracy gain over EKF-MSC in published comparisons; a candidate if our near-terminal angular rates make the EKF linearization bite.
- **Particle filter / range-parameterized EKF** — the only family that represents the *full non-Gaussian, multimodal range posterior* (the posterior over range is genuinely heavy-tailed early in track). PFs win at long range / poor observability but cost 10²–10⁴ particles. **For us this is over-engineering**: we have explicitly declined to estimate range, so there is no multimodal range posterior to track; the angular posterior is near-Gaussian. A PF would burn our ~17 ms/frame Pi-5 budget to estimate a quantity we discard.
- **Pseudo-linear / instrumental-variable (IV) estimators** — recast bearings into a linear-least-squares form (`pseudo-linear estimator`, PLE). Cheap and closed-form but **biased** (the regressor correlates with the noise); IV and bias-compensated PLE fix this. Useful for fast LOBL initialization of the angular state, not for the maneuvering terminal phase.

**IMM for jinking targets.** A single motion model cannot cover a drone that both cruises and jinks. The **Interacting Multiple Model (IMM)** filter (Blom & Bar-Shalom 1988) runs a bank of models as a Markov-switching system with transition matrix `Π = [π_ij]`, mixing the model-conditioned estimates each cycle, and is the standard for maneuvering targets ([Blom & Bar-Shalom](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/289204656_The_interacting_multiple_model_algorithm_for_accurate_state_estimation_of_maneuvering_targets)). The canonical bank is:

- **CV (constant velocity)** in angle space — low process noise, the nominal cruise/collision-triangle model.
- **Singer** — acceleration as a first-order Gauss-Markov process, autocorrelation time τ_m and variance σ_m², covering "mild, persistent" maneuver. The Singer covariance `Q(τ_m, σ_m²)` is the textbook tunable.
- **Coordinated-turn (CT)** — constant-turn-rate model for sustained banked turns; for our angular state this manifests as a near-constant `az_rate`/`el_rate` curl. Essential for a fixed-wing target in a circling pattern.

The IMM's per-model likelihoods auto-weight which regime the target is in; the mixed output is what the nuller sees. Cost is ~(number of models) × single-filter cost — for 3 models at 4 states this is trivially inside 17 ms.

### 5.5 Consistency, gating, and our "model-wrong" alarm

A BOT filter that *looks* converged can be silently diverging, so **consistency monitoring is not optional — it is the safety interlock.**

- **NIS (Normalized Innovation Squared)** — `ε_z = ν_kᵀ S_k⁻¹ ν_k`, with innovation `ν_k = z_k − ẑ_k` and innovation covariance `S_k = H P H ᵀ + R`. Under a correct, consistent filter `ε_z ~ χ²(n_z)`, here `n_z = 2` (az,el). The expected value is `E[ε_z] = 2`; a time-averaged NIS outside the two-sided 95% χ² band (for 2 dof, single-sample bounds ≈ [0.05, 7.4]) flags **filter–measurement inconsistency**. Crucially, NIS needs **no ground truth**, so it runs live on the interceptor.
- **NEES (Normalized Estimation Error Squared)** — `ε_x = (x−x̂)ᵀ P⁻¹ (x−x̂) ~ χ²(n_x)`, normalized to ≈1 per state. NEES needs truth, so it is a **bench/Monte-Carlo tuning metric**, not a flight metric — we use it offline to verify our IMM is neither optimistic (NEES ≫ n) nor conservative (NEES ≪ n).
- **Mahalanobis gating** — the validation gate `ν_kᵀ S_k⁻¹ ν_k ≤ γ` accepts a detection only if it falls inside the **covariance-sized** ellipse; γ from the χ²(2) inverse CDF (e.g. γ ≈ 9.21 for 99%, 5.99 for 95%). The gate is *adaptive*: it widens when P is large (early/uncertain) and tightens as the track sharpens. This is exactly our **covariance-sized gate**.

**Our NIS model-wrong alarm.** Because our IMM lacks a range/maneuver-coupling state, a hard jink shows up first as a **run of high NIS** — the innovations leave the χ²(2) gate because the CV/Singer prediction can't follow. We use that signal two ways: (1) the IMM raises its CT/Singer model probability (recovery), and (2) sustained NIS excursion is a **"model-wrong" lock-quality alarm** routed *only* to engage-permission — consistent with the doctrine that AI/quality signals write to lock-quality / engage-permission and **never** to the centroid/LOS/tracker. A persistent inconsistency on a range-unobservable, single-band seeker is, correctly, treated as grounds to **ABORT (pass)**, because a divergent angular track on a wrong-model target is exactly the precondition for a WRONG HIT — our hard-fail.

**Data association.** With a single dominant blob per frame, association is mostly **nearest-neighbor (NN) inside the Mahalanobis gate**. When clutter appears (sun glint, a second drone, hot background object passing through the gate), the principled upgrades are **PDA** (probabilistic data association — soft, probability-weighted combination of all gated returns, single target), **JPDA** (joint PDA — multi-target, resolves competing gates), and **MHT** (multiple-hypothesis tracking — defers the association decision across frames, most powerful and most expensive). For our compute budget and single-target terminal mission, **gated NN with a PDA fallback** under multi-return clutter is the right operating point; JPDA/MHT are over-budget on a single A76 and unnecessary against one drone, but PDA's clutter robustness is cheap insurance against glint-induced gate captures that would otherwise drag the centroid — the one failure mode that could turn an ABORT into a wrong hit.

### Implications for our seeker
- **Keep the reduced 4-state modified-polar IMM and never restore `1/r`/`ṙ/r`.** Carrying an unobservable inverse-range state buys nothing the guidance uses and risks cross-covariance poisoning the angular estimate; deleting it is the Aidala-Hammel decoupling taken to its honest limit.
- **Our LOS-rate-nulling trajectory is maximally range-unobservable by design** (straight-line, λ̇→0 collision geometry). This *validates* the no-range-gain doctrine: there is no consistent range to scale by on the intercept manifold, so range must never enter the guidance loop.
- **Run NIS live as the primary in-flight consistency interlock** (χ²(2), E=2); use a covariance-sized Mahalanobis gate (γ from χ²(2)). Reserve NEES for bench Monte-Carlo tuning only — it needs truth we don't have in flight.
- **Sustained NIS excursion = "model-wrong" → engage-permission ABORT, never a tracker correction.** This is the doctrinally-clean coupling: quality signals gate the *decision*, not the *centroid/LOS*.
- **Tune the IMM bank as CV + Singer(τ_m,σ_m²) + coordinated-turn**, with process noise sized to absorb the angular acceleration we can no longer explain kinematically (no range to couple maneuver through).
- **Default to gated nearest-neighbor with a PDA fallback under clutter** (glint, second drone); JPDA/MHT are over-budget for single-target terminal and a PF is wasted compute since we discard the range posterior.

### Open questions / frontier
- **Does a brief, bounded LOBL-phase lateral weave buy enough range observability to be worth it?** A small cross-LOS maneuver *before* committing to the collision triangle would make `1/r` weakly observable (full-rank Gramian) — but it costs lateral g-budget and time before the ten-tau wall, and conflicts with the nuller. Quantifying the observability-vs-energy trade is open.
- **EKF-MSC vs UKF-MSC at our terminal angular rates.** Near impact, az/el rates can spike; whether the EKF linearization of `atan2` stays valid or we need a UKF (still inside 17 ms?) needs a bench NEES study at representative end-game geometries.
- **NIS thresholds under 8-bit-AGC centroid noise that is non-stationary and non-Gaussian.** AGC re-stretch events and bloom make `R` time-varying and heavy-tailed; the χ²(2) gate assumes Gaussian innovations. How much does AGC-induced non-Gaussianity inflate false "model-wrong" alarms (false ABORTs)?
- **Adaptive `R` from AGC/scene state** — can we estimate the instantaneous centroid covariance from blob morphology and AGC histogram so the gate breathes correctly, without ever letting that signal touch the centroid itself?
- **PDA glint robustness vs the "default-deny" cost asymmetry.** PDA softens association but also softens the decision to reject a clutter return; tuning PDA's detection probability `P_D` and clutter density `λ` so that glint never captures the gate *and* we don't suppress a real second-drone re-acquire is an unsolved operating-point question.
- **Provable monotone link between low observability-Gramian eigenvalue and wrong-hit probability.** We assert that the unobservable geometry is safe because guidance ignores range — but a formal bound tying `λ_min(W_o)`, NIS statistics, and P(wrong-hit) would let us set the ABORT threshold from first principles rather than by Monte-Carlo tuning.

## 6. Target Discrimination & Classification

### 6.1 The decision being made, and why it is not the tracker's job

Discrimination is the act of converting raw imagery and kinematics into a single posterior — *is this object a hostile drone I am permitted to terminate?* In our architecture this question is doctrinally walled off from the loop that actually flies the missile. The IMM tracker (4-state modified-polar angular state `[az, el, az_rate, el_rate]`) and the λ̇-nuller consume a centroid and an LOS rate; they do **not** consume a class label. Classification feeds **engage-permission only**. This is not a stylistic choice — it follows from the asymmetric cost structure (§6.7) and from the fact that a misclassification that perturbs the centroid would corrupt guidance on *every* frame, whereas a misclassification gated into permission can only ever cause an abort (a PASS) or, in the worst case, a wrong hit (a HARD FAIL) that the permission logic exists specifically to prevent.

So this section is about estimating a likelihood ratio Λ = p(features | hostile-drone) / p(features | {bird, clutter, decoy, friendly}) and thresholding it under an asymmetric loss — nothing more, nothing less.

### 6.2 The discriminant inventory

Discriminants fall into three physical families. Each carries information; each has a hard ceiling set by our sensor.

**(A) IR-radiometric / structural.** The *thermal hot-spot pattern*: a multirotor concentrates heat at its motor/ESC cluster — brushless motors run 40–80 °C above ambient under load, against a sky background that in LWIR is effectively very cold (apparent sky temperatures of −20 to −50 °C are routine). The motor cluster therefore presents a high-apparent-ΔT constellation of N point sources (N = rotor count) with a roughly fixed geometric arrangement in the body frame. A bird presents a single, lower-contrast, soft-edged blob (feather insulation keeps surface ΔT to a few K over a ~38–42 °C core), and clutter (sun-warmed rooftops, exhaust plumes, hot ground) presents extended, low-frequency structure. This is the single most discriminating *static* cue we have, and it is also the most fragile (see §6.8 — AGC and 8-bit quantization compress exactly this constellation).

**(B) Thermal micro-Doppler (blade/rotor modulation).** A spinning rotor is a warm rotating structure; as a blade sweeps through the pixel footprint it periodically modulates the apparent temperature — the "blade flash." This is the IR analogue of radar micro-Doppler, where rotor flashes and HERM lines give ~99.9–100% drone-vs-bird separability in Ku/W-band CNN studies ([Nature Sci. Rep. 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-35880-9)). The catch is bandwidth (§6.4): radar samples the rotor at MHz; we sample at video rate.

**(C) Kinematic.** Drone flight is *smooth and regular* — bounded jerk, near-constant-velocity arcs, station-keeping. Bird flight is *flap-glide*: quasi-periodic body-bob at flap frequency (4–10 Hz), looser trajectory, larger heading variance. Sequence-based temporal models exploiting exactly this have raised bird-classification F1 by up to ~73% over single-frame appearance ([Drone-Warfare EO/IR](https://drone-warfare.com/counter-uas/eo-ir-detection/)). For us this is doubly valuable because **kinematics survive AGC** — the IMM already estimates az/el rates that are immune to the radiometric crushing that destroys (A) and (B).

### 6.3 Information at the sensor: the bit-depth and resolution ceiling

Start with the irreducible limit. Our IFOV is θ_FOV / N_px. With a 48.7° × 38.6° FOV over a (typical CVBS-grabbed) ~640×512 effective grid, IFOV ≈ 48.7° / 640 ≈ 1.33 mrad/px. A drone of physical extent L at range R subtends

  n_px ≈ L / (R · IFOV).

For L = 0.30 m (a 7–10" quad's hot core), at R = 100 m: n_px ≈ 0.30 / (100 · 1.33e-3) ≈ 2.3 px. At R = 30 m: ≈ 7.5 px. At R = 18 m: ≈ 12.5 px. This is the brutal reality: at *detection* range the target is **2–3 px**; only inside ID range does it become a 30–48 px chip. The honest envelope stated for our seeker — DETECT 115–190 m, ID 18–30 m — is exactly the Johnson/NVThermIP arithmetic.

**Johnson criteria, applied.** The N50 framework says you need a target's critical dimension to span N50 resolvable cycles for 50% task probability: classically ~1 cycle (2 px) to detect, ~3–4 to recognize, ~6–8 to identify; modern MWIR small-craft ID figures land around N50 ≈ 2.8–4.0 cycles ([NATO STO MP-SCI-180-09](https://publications.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Meeting%20Proceedings/RTO-MP-SCI-180/MP-SCI-180-09.pdf); [OSTI Johnson history](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1222446)). At 12–25 px on target we have ~6–12 cycles — enough to *recognize* a multirotor planform and resolve the motor constellation, marginal to *identify* a specific airframe. Below ~8 px we are in pure detection: a hot blob with kinematics, no usable internal structure.

**Bit-depth.** Here is the part unique to us. We do **not** have a 16-bit radiometric Y16 stream. We have **analog CVBS, 8-bit, hardware AGC**. The information-theoretic consequence is exact: per pixel the sensor delivers at most log2(256) = 8 bits, but the *usable* radiometric bits are far fewer because AGC continuously rescales the scene to fill the 8-bit range. Effective radiometric resolution after AGC in a high-dynamic-range scene (hot ground + cold sky + a tiny hot target) is often **4–6 effective bits** on the target, because most of the 256 levels are spent on the background span. The 50 mK NETD ([sensitivity class typical of fast uncooled bolometers](https://www.dias-infrared.co.uk/pdf/p020.pdf)) is therefore *not* the limiting quantization in our pipeline — the AGC quantizer is. We have thrown away the radiometric axis that would let us measure absolute motor ΔT and replaced it with relative, scene-dependent contrast.

### 6.4 Can we see blade flash? A bandwidth calculation

Rotor mechanical RPM for small quads is ~5,000–30,000 rpm → f_rot ≈ 83–500 Hz. Blade-pass frequency f_bp = f_rot · N_blades; a 2-blade prop at 10,000 rpm gives f_bp ≈ 333 Hz. Our pipeline runs at CVBS field/frame rate with a measured ~17 ms/frame ≈ **~50–60 fps**. By Nyquist we can unambiguously resolve modulation only up to ~25–30 Hz. Therefore:

- **f_bp (hundreds of Hz) is grossly undersampled.** We will alias, not measure, the true blade-pass tone. We cannot do clean radar-style micro-Doppler.
- What *is* sometimes observable is a **lower-frequency beat / strobe**: aliasing of f_bp against frame rate can produce an apparent slow flicker, and bulk motor/ESC thermal pulsing and airframe body-bob live in the few-Hz band that we *can* sample. Bird flap (4–10 Hz) is squarely in our observable band.
- Additional killers: **motion blur** (a blade tip moving at tens of m/s smears across many pixels in a 17 ms integration-equivalent window, washing out the flash), the **bolometer thermal time constant** itself (τ ≈ 7–40 ms for fast/standard uncooled detectors; [ULIS/Lynred](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1350449506000727), [DIAS](https://www.dias-infrared.co.uk/pdf/p020.pdf)) acts as a one-pole low-pass with corner ~1/(2πτ) ≈ 4–23 Hz — the detector *physically integrates away* high-frequency thermal flicker before the ADC ever sees it.

**Verdict:** true thermal micro-Doppler blade-flash at fundamental f_bp is **fundamentally unavailable** to this sensor (a sampling + τ limit, not an engineering shortfall). A residual *aliased/sub-harmonic flicker* and the few-Hz kinematic micro-motion are **merely hard** — sometimes present, never reliable. We must treat blade flash as a weak, opportunistic likelihood term, not a primary discriminant.

### 6.5 The appearance channel: a CNN on a 32–48 px 8-bit chip

State-of-the-art IR small-target work (SCTransNet, MiM-ISTD, ISTD-YOLO, the LAT-BirdDrone hybrid trajectory net; see the [awesome-IRST list](https://github.com/Tianfang-Zhang/awesome-infrared-small-targets) and [LAT-BirdDrone](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12769828/)) makes two things clear: (1) single-frame appearance on tiny IR chips saturates at modest accuracy because CNNs "are inherently local and often fail to capture long-range context" needed to separate target from clutter ([IRST review, PMC11207645](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11207645/)); (2) the big wins come from **temporal/trajectory** fusion, not bigger backbones.

The information argument is decisive. A 40×40, 8-bit chip carries ≤ 40·40·8 = 12,800 raw bits, but after AGC, blur, and the fact that the object occupies a small central support, the *task-relevant* entropy is a few tens of bits at most — comparable to a handful of well-chosen scalar features (extent, aspect, peak-contrast ratio, hot-spot count, symmetry). Beyond that floor, a deeper net is fitting noise. This is why we treat any CNN as **one weak log-likelihood contributor**, calibrated and abstaining, never an oracle.

Doctrinally critical: per our invariant, a learned component **writes only to lock-quality / engage-permission, never to centroid/LOS/tracker.** The CNN therefore lives entirely inside the permission posterior. It must be **calibrated** (temperature-scaled so its softmax is a true probability), and it must have an explicit **reject/abstain band** — when the chip is < ~8 px, or AGC has saturated, or confidence sits in the ambiguous middle, the correct output is "I don't know," which under default-deny becomes ABORT.

### 6.6 Combining evidence: log-likelihood-ratio stacking

Treat each discriminant family as an independent (or weakly-coupled) sensor producing a log-likelihood ratio, and accumulate over time. With features f_k from frame t:

  LLR_total = Σ_k log[ p(f_k | drone) / p(f_k | ¬drone) ] + (temporal recursion)

Recursively, a log-odds (binary Bayes) filter:

  L_t = L_{t−1} + Σ_k ℓ_k(f_t) , P(drone)_t = σ(L_t)

where ℓ_k are *calibrated, capped* per-feature log-LRs (cap each |ℓ_k| ≤ ℓ_max so no single noisy frame can swing permission — this is the discrimination analogue of innovation gating in the tracker). Independence is the key lie we must respect: motor-cluster contrast, hot-spot count, and CNN appearance are all radiometric and therefore **correlated** — they co-fail under AGC saturation. We must either decorrelate (whiten) or, more safely, **down-weight the entire radiometric block to the strength of one feature** and let the *kinematic* block (IMM-derived rates, jerk, flap-band spectral energy) carry independent weight, because kinematics fail under different conditions than radiometry. The robust posterior is a fusion of two weakly-correlated subsystems, not five strongly-correlated ones.

### 6.7 Asymmetric-cost decision theory: why the threshold is not 0.5

Bayesian decision theory: choose ENGAGE iff expected cost of engaging < expected cost of aborting:

  P(drone | f) · C_hit-on-drone + (1−P) · C_hit-on-nondrone < P · C_abort-on-drone + (1−P) · C_abort-on-nondrone.

Set the costs to our doctrine: a **wrong hit is catastrophic** (C_hit-on-nondrone ≫ everything; engaging a bird, a friendly, or empty sky is a HARD FAIL), while an **abort is a PASS** (C_abort ≈ small, recoverable — there may be another shot or another interceptor). Solving for the engage threshold:

  P(drone) > τ* = [C_hit-on-nondrone − C_abort-on-nondrone] / [ (C_hit-on-nondrone − C_abort-on-nondrone) + (C_abort-on-drone − C_hit-on-drone) ].

With C_hit-on-nondrone enormous, τ* → 1. Concretely, if a wrong hit is ~100× worse than a missed drone, τ* ≈ 0.99. **The permission threshold is deliberately near-unity** — we demand near-certainty, accept many missed/aborted real drones, and structurally forbid false engagements. This is the formal statement of "default-deny on doubt." It also dictates the operating point on the ROC: we sit far up the low-false-positive tail, paying in P_d to crush P_fa.

### 6.8 The single-8-bit-band discrimination ceiling — what is impossible vs merely hard

Spectral discrimination (two-color / MWIR-vs-LWIR ratioing) is a classical and powerful way to separate hot engine-like sources from cold birds and gray-body clutter. **We have one band and no radiometric calibration.** Therefore:

- **Fundamentally impossible for us:** absolute temperature measurement; spectral/two-color material discrimination; clean blade-pass micro-Doppler at fundamental frequency; range/closing-speed-aided sizing (range is unobservable — we cannot convert px-extent to physical-extent, so "it's drone-sized" is *not* available to us). Anything requiring the radiometric or spectral axis we threw away is out.
- **Merely hard (mitigable, never solved):** drone-vs-bird at the honest envelope, by *fusing* (i) hot-spot constellation structure when ≥ ~12 px, (ii) flap-band (4–10 Hz) kinematic spectral energy vs smooth-arc regularity/jerk, (iii) temporal trajectory consistency over many frames, (iv) a calibrated, abstaining CNN, and (v) the *aspect/extent* trend (a quad's apparent extent and hot-spot count are roughly aspect-stable; a bird's pulse with wingbeat). The single 8-bit AGC band degrades every *radiometric* leg of this stack; it does not touch the *kinematic* leg, which is why our discrimination posterior must lean on kinematics + time + structure and use radiometry only as confirmation. This is the precise sense in which the ceiling is "mitigated, never solved."

A decoy (a flare, a heated balloon, a chaff-like hot source, or a deliberate drone-shaped thermal lure) is the adversarial worst case: it is *designed* to satisfy the radiometric leg. Our only defenses are the **kinematic** leg (a flare falls ballistically; a balloon drifts with wind; neither flies a powered, station-keeping or intercept-relevant trajectory) and the near-unity threshold that makes the adversary's job — forcing P > 0.99 across *both* weakly-correlated subsystems — much harder than spoofing one.

### Implications for our seeker

## 7. Terminal Guidance Law Science

### 7.1 The proportional-navigation family and its governing equation

Terminal homing is, at its core, the problem of producing a lateral acceleration command from observed line-of-sight (LOS) geometry such that the missile arrives at the target with small miss distance. The dominant family of solutions is proportional navigation (PN), whose canonical form is

```
a_cmd = N · Vc · λ̇      (commanded acceleration ⟂ LOS)
```

where `λ̇` is the inertial LOS angular rate, `Vc = −Ṙ` is the closing velocity (range-rate magnitude), and `N` is the dimensionless navigation ratio (effective navigation constant `N_eff`, typically 3–5). The physical content is profound and simple: the missile turns at a rate proportional to the rotation of the LOS. The variants differ in what they assume is measured and what they assume the target does:

- **True PN (TPN):** acceleration applied perpendicular to the instantaneous LOS, `a = N·Vc·λ̇`. The textbook law.
- **Pure PN (PPN):** acceleration perpendicular to the *missile velocity vector* rather than the LOS, `a = N·Vm·λ̇`. Easier to mechanize on a body-fixed airframe; slightly different stability and capture region.
- **Augmented PN (APN):** adds a target-acceleration feedforward term, `a = N·Vc·λ̇ + (N/2)·a_T`, where `a_T` is the (estimated) target acceleration normal to the LOS. APN is the linear-quadratic (LQ) optimal law against a constant (step) target maneuver for a missile with no control-effort penalty and zero lag (confirmed in the literature: optimal `N' = 3`, with the target term weighted by `N'/2` [[Optimality of Augmented Ideal PN, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/303671258_Optimality_of_Augmented_Ideal_Proportional_Navigation_for_Maneuvering_Target_Interception); [NPTEL modern guidance notes](https://archive.nptel.ac.in/content/storage2/courses/101108056/module5/lecture10.pdf)]).
- **Optimal Guidance Law (OGL):** the full LQ solution including airframe/autopilot lag `τ` and a control-weight penalty; reduces to APN as lag → 0 and reduces to TPN when `a_T → 0`. OGL schedules `N_eff` as a function of `t_go/τ`, rising above 3 near intercept.

The unifying result from optimal control: minimize `J = ½·∫ a²dt` subject to the linearized intercept kinematics, and the optimal command is

```
a_cmd = (N/t_go²) · ZEM
```

where **ZEM** is the *zero-effort miss* — the miss that would occur if neither vehicle accelerated further from now on — and `t_go` is time-to-go. For the constant-`Vc`, small-angle engagement, `ZEM = y + ẏ·t_go (+ ½·a_T·t_go²)` with `y` the relative cross-LOS displacement; substituting recovers `a = N·Vc·λ̇ (+ (N/2)a_T)`. **This is the master equation of terminal guidance, and it is the lens through which our seeker's choices must be read: every practical law is an attempt to estimate and null ZEM.**

### 7.2 Why N_eff matters, and why APN is "optimal"

The navigation ratio sets the trade between *responsiveness* and *noise/saturation*. In the deterministic linear problem, miss distance from an initial heading error `decays as (t_go)^(N−1)` — larger `N` collapses miss faster, but the *acceleration demand* near intercept scales as `t_go^(N−2)`, so for `N>2` the command is finite while for `N=2` it is flat. Real autopilot lag `τ` forces a floor: `N_eff` effectively rises through the engagement and the loop becomes a high-gain differentiator of a noisy bearing channel in the last fraction of a second. APN's optimality is narrow but real — it is exactly LQ-optimal *only* for a step `a_T`, *only* with perfect `a_T` knowledge, *only* with no lag and no control penalty. Against a randomly weaving target with unknown `a_T`, the feedforward term can be worse than useless because the `a_T` estimate is itself derived from a noisy, range-coupled filter.

### 7.3 The central problem for us: range and Vc are unobservable

Every law above contains `Vc` (and OGL/APN contain `t_go` and `a_T`). For a **passive, monocular, strapdown** seeker these are not merely hard to measure — they are *fundamentally unobservable on a non-maneuvering collision course*. The angles-only measurement model gives bearing `λ = atan2(y, x)`; range `R` enters only through the *product* `R·λ̇`, so the Fisher information matrix for `(R, Ṙ)` is singular and the Cramér–Rao lower bound on range diverges (CRLB → ∞) unless the observer maneuvers to create parallax. This is the classic bearings-only observability result, and it is sharpest precisely on the collision triangle, where `λ̇ → 0` and the measurement carries *no* range information [[Observability Analysis of Advanced Guidance Laws with Bearing-Only Measurement](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/269265692_Observability_Analysis_of_Advanced_Guidance_Laws_With_Bearing-Only_Measurement); [Practical guidance for homing missiles with bearings-only measurements, IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/481284/)]. Our IMM state is therefore correctly *modified-polar / angular only* — `[az, el, az_rate, el_rate]` — with no range state pretending to exist. This is the honest acknowledgment of the math, not a limitation of the implementation.

There is a deep and uncomfortable tension here, well documented in the literature: PN's job is to drive `λ̇ → 0`, but `λ̇` (and observer maneuver) is exactly what *would* excite range observability. "When the missile and target are on a collision course, the information content of bearings-only measurements may not be sufficient to excite the filter under the PN strategy of nullifying the LOS rate" [IEEE, above]. Observability-enhanced / biased-PN laws deliberately inject `λ̇` bias to estimate range — but **we explicitly reject that path** under our doctrine (range never scales the gain; no S-maneuver weave that costs maneuver headroom we cannot spare). We surrender to unobservability rather than fight it.

### 7.4 The λ̇-nuller as the doctrinally correct law

Set the unobservable quantities aside and ask: what survives? The collision-triangle condition itself. Two bodies are on a collision course **iff the inertial LOS does not rotate** — `λ̇ = 0` — *regardless of range, closing speed, or absolute scale*. This is a purely angular, range-free invariant. Our guidance is therefore a **bearing-rate / LOS-rate nuller**: drive `λ̇ → 0` in azimuth and elevation, using the gyro-de-rotated angular state. Mechanically this is PN with `Vc` removed from the loop and folded into a *fixed scheduled gain*:

```
a_cmd = K · λ̇,     K ≡ N · Vc_sched   (Vc_sched is a constant, NOT a measurement)
```

The critical doctrinal point: **`Vc` is scheduled, never measured, and never scales the gain from range.** Scheduling `Vc` is legitimate because for a near-head-on intercept of a slow drone by a fast FPV, `Vc ≈ |Vm| + |Vt·cosψ|` is dominated by our own (roughly known) airspeed and varies only modestly over the terminal window — so a constant `K` mis-tunes `N_eff` by a bounded factor (perhaps ±30–50%) rather than the orders of magnitude a bad range estimate would inject. A wrong `Vc_sched` does not change *whether* `λ̇ = 0` is the collision condition; it only changes the *loop bandwidth* with which we approach it. That is the whole trick: nulling `λ̇` is correct independent of `Vc`; only the rate of convergence depends on `K`.

**What this forfeits, honestly:** without `Vc`, `t_go`, or `ZEM` we cannot (a) compute the OGL `N_eff` schedule that optimally trades miss vs. effort near intercept; (b) implement APN's `a_T` feedforward optimally, because the `(N/2)a_T·... ` term and the ZEM formula both require `t_go = R/Vc`; (c) trigger guidance events on true `t_go` — we must proxy it with apparent angular size, expansion rate (`τ_optical = θ/θ̇`, a *time-to-contact* surrogate from looming, itself noisy and AGC-corrupted at our 8-bit depth), or a fixed wall-clock budget. We trade provable optimality for provable *robustness to the thing we cannot know*.

### 7.5 Maneuvering targets, ZEM, and the abort geometry

Against a maneuvering target the pure λ̇-nuller is a *regulator without feedforward*: it reacts to the `λ̇` that the target's maneuver induces, always one lag behind. A target pulling constant `a_T` builds a steady-state LOS-rate error that a finite-gain nuller cannot fully null; the residual miss scales roughly as `m ∝ a_T·τ²·f(N)` — i.e., linearly in target g and quadratically in total loop lag. For our drone targets this is tolerable *only* in the stated envelope: near-head-on, target lateral `< ~0.28 g`. Above that, two things fail together — the induced `λ̇` exceeds what our **≤0.84 g lateral budget** (`θ_max ≈ 40°` cone) can null, and the required correction must happen before the geometry runs out.

The **capture cone** is the set of initial heading errors from which the airframe can null `λ̇` before intercept given its g-limit. Its half-angle scales as

```
θ_capture ≈ √( 2·a_max·R / Vc² )   (order-of-magnitude, constant-g turn into the collision triangle)
```

so capture region *shrinks* with closing speed squared and *grows* with available g and range. With `a_max = 0.84g ≈ 8.2 m/s²`, this is a tight cone — it is why we are LOBL (lock-on-before-launch): we launch already near the collision triangle so the required `Δλ` is small. A **high-crossing / over-g geometry** — a fast crossing target whose `λ̇` demands more than 0.84 g — is not a guidance problem to be solved harder; it is an **abort**. This is the asymmetric-cost doctrine made geometric: ABORT is a PASS, a WRONG HIT is a HARD FAIL. The g-budget is a hard ceiling, and the speed loop is coupled to it: lower commanded airspeed lowers `Vc`, which (for the same target crossing velocity) *raises* `θ_capture` and lowers the g needed to null a given `λ̇` — so **slowing down buys maneuver headroom**. This is the correct response to a marginal geometry, opposite to a range-scaled PN law that would demand more g at shorter range.

### 7.6 Noise, glint, scintillation, and the high-gain terminal loop

The λ̇-nuller is a *differentiator* — it acts on the time-derivative of bearing, which amplifies high-frequency measurement noise by `∝ ω`. Near intercept this is brutal: as `R → 0`, a fixed *linear* centroid jitter `δy` maps to an angular error `δλ = δy/R` that *blows up*, so the very last moments have the worst angular SNR exactly when the loop gain is highest. The dominant noise sources for us:

- **Centroid/glint noise:** the target is not a point; its apparent thermal centroid wanders across the drone body (motor pods, battery, airframe) as aspect changes. This **glint** injects `λ̇` with no kinematic meaning. On an uncooled 8-bit AGC sensor the centroid is further corrupted because the AGC remaps the histogram frame-to-frame — a hot motor saturating the band can pull the centroid and shift it as range closes. Our mitigations (temporal smoothing of the IMM, structure/area gating, refusing the `λ̇` spikes that look like glint not kinematics) reduce but never eliminate this.
- **Scintillation / atmospheric:** path turbulence adds angle-of-arrival jitter scaling as `σ_AoA ∝ Cn²^(1/2)·L^(1/2)/D` (Cn² turbulence structure constant, path `L`, aperture `D`). For our short terminal ranges (DETECT ~115–190 m, ID ~18–30 m) and small aperture this is sub-pixel at LWIR — real but not dominant relative to glint and AGC effects.
- **Time-correlated vs. white:** glint is *time-correlated* (colored), which defeats simple averaging and is exactly the regime where high-`N_eff` loops ring. This is a further argument *against* pushing `N_eff` high near intercept.

The classical defense is to *reduce loop gain near intercept* (taper `N_eff`), but OGL's optimal taper requires `t_go` — which we don't have. We instead rely on a **fixed, conservatively-tuned `K`** plus temporal filtering, accepting larger nominal miss for bounded noise-driven miss.

### 7.7 The "ten-tau" terminal wall

Any guidance loop with effective time constant `τ_loop` (autopilot + filter + processing latency) needs roughly **10–15 time constants to settle** a heading correction to small residual. Our measured pipeline is ~17 ms/frame on the Pi-5 single A76 thread; folded with the airframe response this gives a `τ_loop` of order ~0.1 s, so settling a correction demands on the order of **~1.0–1.4 s of usable terminal time** — the **"ten-tau wall."** Inside this window, *no new correction can converge*: the loop is committed. This is the hard deadline behind every other choice. It means corrections must be *made* before the wall, which means the capture geometry must be acceptable *early*, which means LOBL + a tight envelope + early abort. It also means the engage/abort decision is effectively *frozen* at `t_wall` — any AI/learned component must deliver its verdict (lock-quality, engage-permission) *before* the wall, and by doctrine it writes only there, never into the centroid/LOS/tracker.

### Implications for our seeker

- **The λ̇-nuller is not a degraded PN; it is the correct law for our observability class.** Nulling `λ̇` drives the collision triangle independent of range/`Vc`; only convergence *rate* depends on the scheduled gain `K = N·Vc_sched`, so a mis-scheduled `Vc` costs bandwidth, not collision correctness — bounded, unlike a range error.

- **Respect the ten-tau wall as a frozen-decision deadline.** All engage/abort and lock-quality verdicts (including any AI component, which writes only to permission) must land before ~1.0–1.4 s to-intercept; after the wall the loop is committed.
- **Treat glint as colored λ̇ noise, not Gaussian.** On 8-bit AGC the centroid wanders with aspect and histogram remap; reject kinematically-implausible `λ̇` spikes rather than trusting a high-gain differentiator near `R → 0`.

## 8. Strapdown Seeker Architecture & Signal Chain

### 8.1 The gimbaled-vs-strapdown trade and why CUAS rounds go body-fixed

A homing seeker has exactly one job at the physical layer: deliver a clean estimate of the inertial line-of-sight (LOS) rate, conventionally written λ̇ ("lambda-dot"), to the guidance law. Everything in the signal chain exists to protect that one number. The two architectural families differ in *where* the LOS-rate is mechanically and computationally formed.

A **gimbaled (stabilized) seeker** mounts the detector on a 2-axis (or 2-DOF roll/pitch) gimbal carrying its own rate gyros. The gimbal servo slaves the optical boresight to the target and is rate-stabilized against airframe motion, so the gimbal-mounted gyro reads the *inertial* LOS rate directly (to within servo bandwidth and bearing-friction error). The detector sees a target that is mechanically near-stationary in its own frame; the body of the missile can pitch, yaw and roll underneath the optics largely unfelt. This is the classical air-to-air architecture (AIM-9, AIM-120 active seekers, IIR seekers on later Sidewinders). The gimbal buys three things: (1) a *mechanically* isolated LOS-rate measurement, (2) a wide *acquisition* field of regard decoupled from a narrow *instantaneous* FOV (you can stare narrow-and-sensitive while still tracking large off-boresight angles), and (3) tolerance to large airframe attitude excursions in the terminal phase.

A **strapdown (body-fixed) seeker** hard-mounts the detector to the airframe. There is no servo, no gimbal gyro, no slip-ring, no bearing. The target moves freely across the focal-plane array (FPA) as both the target maneuvers *and* the body rotates. The instantaneous FOV **is** the field of regard — you can only track what is inside the lens cone. The inertial LOS rate is no longer measured mechanically; it must be *reconstructed* by software from image-plane motion plus an inertial measurement of body rate. This is the central feature, and the central liability, of the architecture (see [Maley, "Line-of-Sight Rate Estimation and Linearizing Control of an Imaging Seeker"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3332355) and the strapdown LOS-reconstruction literature, e.g. [Lin et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/296354345)).

For a Block-3 CUAS FPV interceptor the choice is forced, not chosen, by SWaP-C. A pitch/yaw gimbal of useful bandwidth (≥ 20–30 Hz to track a maneuvering quadcopter near impact) costs tens to low-hundreds of grams, draws watts, adds cost comparable to the entire airframe, introduces a single mechanical point of failure under launch shock, and consumes volume the round does not have. Our **Foxeer FT640 V2** is a ~few-gram analog module; the entire interceptor economics depend on it staying that way. So we go strapdown — and we pay the strapdown price in *signal processing burden and error budget*, which is exactly what the rest of this section is about.

The price, itemized: (i) λ̇ becomes a computed quantity sensitive to gyro scale-factor, misalignment, and time-sync; (ii) field of regard collapses to the ~**48.7° × 38.6°** lens cone, so loss-of-track on a hard target break is unrecoverable without re-acquisition; (iii) terminal airframe attitude excursions directly translate into target motion across an 8-bit AGC frame, where they can desynchronize the AGC and the tracker; (iv) any structural compliance between detector and IMU corrupts λ̇ directly (Section 8.4). The countervailing benefit — and it is real — is that a *body-fixed* seeker on a *bearing-rate-nulling* guidance law is architecturally honest about the fact that we have no range: we are not trying to do anything a gimbal would buy us (we never command large off-boresight terminal geometry; our envelope is near-head-on, < ~0.28 g targets).

### 8.2 The strapdown λ̇-extraction problem (the governing equations)

Let the target image on the FPA at pixel offset (u, v) from boresight, with focal length f (in pixels). The bearing angles to the target in the body frame are, to small-angle,

  λ_az ≈ u / f,  λ_el ≈ v / f.

The **measured** image-plane angular velocity of the target is the time derivative of those pixel-bearings. The fundamental strapdown identity is:

  **λ̇_measured(image) = λ̇_inertial(true LOS rate) − ω_body⊥**

i.e. *what you see the target do on the focal plane = what the LOS is actually doing in inertial space, MINUS the apparent motion the target acquires purely because the camera (body) rotated under it.* Written per axis with the body rate components resolved into the camera frame:

  λ̇_az,inertial = (d/dt)(u/f) + (ω_yaw − ω_roll·(v/f) + …)
  λ̇_el,inertial = (d/dt)(v/f) + (ω_pitch + ω_roll·(u/f) + …)

The key structural facts:

- **Yaw and pitch body rates couple at unity gain** into apparent az/el image motion. A 1 rad/s body yaw produces 1 rad/s of apparent azimuth LOS rate. For our envelope this term *dwarfs* the true LOS rate: terminal true λ̇ on a near-head-on engagement is order 10⁻²–10⁻¹ rad/s, while airframe body rates from the 0.84 g lateral envelope and atmospheric buffeting are order 10⁰ rad/s. **The thing we must measure is one-to-two orders of magnitude smaller than the thing we must subtract.** This is the whole ballgame.
- **Roll body rate couples multiplicatively** with off-boresight position (the (v/f), (u/f) terms). A target at the FOV edge (u/f ≈ 0.4 rad) under a 1 rad/s roll picks up ~0.4 rad/s of cross-axis apparent motion. On a strapdown round that is not roll-stabilized this is a first-order error source; it is why centroid offset from boresight is itself a risk multiplier.

**Gyro de-rotation (ego-compensation)** is the operation that recovers λ̇_inertial: we read body rate ω from the IMU and *add it back* (subtract the −ω⊥ term) to the differentiated pixel-bearing. In our system this is precisely the de-rotation feeding the IMM's 4-state modified-polar angular state [az, el, az_rate, el_rate]: the image gives (az, el); the gyro-compensated differentiation gives (az_rate, el_rate) as *inertial* LOS rates rather than image-plane rates. The IMM then arbitrates between motion hypotheses on those de-rotated rates.

### 8.3 Parasitic terms, scale-factor, misalignment, time-sync — the error budget

Because de-rotation *subtracts a large number from a comparable large number to recover a small one*, every error in the subtrahend lands at full strength on λ̇. This is the "**disturbance-rejection-rate parasitic loop**" of the strapdown literature ([CN103954179A](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN103954179A/en); [Song et al., disturbance-rejection parasitic loop](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/290671090)). Quantitatively:

- **Gyro scale-factor error.** If the gyro reports k·ω instead of ω, the de-rotation leaves a residual (k−1)·ω in the LOS-rate estimate. With body rate ω ≈ 1 rad/s and a cheap MEMS gyro scale-factor error of 1% (typical for an uncalibrated BMI-class part; 0.1–0.3% achievable with temperature calibration), the residual is **0.01 rad/s** — already the same order as the true terminal λ̇ we are trying to null. The strapdown stability literature finds that even sub-percent scale-factor mismatch opens a *parasitic feedback loop* that can destabilize the homing loop ([Stability Analysis of Strapdown Seeker Scale Factor Error and LOS Rate, PDF](http://img3.custompublish.com/getfile.php/1067021.753.ayrucxbwxb/Or%2030.pdf)). This is a hard, structural reason our doctrine *must not* run a high-gain range-scaled PN law: the parasitic loop gain would multiply against an already-marginal λ̇.
- **Boresight / IMU misalignment.** A fixed angular misalignment θ_m between the camera optical axis and the gyro sense axes leaks body rate into the wrong LOS-rate channel: residual ≈ θ_m·ω. A 0.5° (8.7 mrad) misalignment with ω = 1 rad/s injects ~0.0087 rad/s of cross-coupled false λ̇ — again target-signal-sized. Misalignment is partly calibratable (a bench boresight) but drifts with thermal cycling and launch shock; it is *mitigated, never solved*.
- **Time synchronization (cam ↔ IMU).** This is the most insidious term. De-rotation subtracts ω(t) sampled by the IMU from image motion sampled at a *different* instant. A timestamp skew Δt means we subtract ω(t−Δt) from image data at t, leaving a residual ≈ ω̇·Δt + ω·(timing jitter effects). Worse, under a *changing* body rate the error is ω̇·Δt: with body angular acceleration ω̇ ≈ 50 rad/s² (a realistic terminal twitch) and a 5 ms cam/IMU skew, the false λ̇ is **0.25 rad/s** — an order of magnitude larger than the signal. **Time-sync error, not noise, is typically the dominant strapdown λ̇ error in a low-cost build.** This is why cam↔IMU time-sync is a first-class engineering requirement, not a tuning afterthought, and why our ~17 ms pipeline latency (Section 8.5) must be a *known, characterized* number rather than a variable one.

The general lesson from the decoupling literature ([Zhang et al., LOS angular rate decoupling and estimation](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/289689485); [UKF-based strapdown LOS estimation, Hindawi MPE 2015](https://www.hindawi.com/journals/mpe/2015/185149/)): with finite decoupling gain, a *residual* fraction of body motion always survives into the guidance signal. You cannot drive it to zero; you budget it and you keep the loop gain low enough that the residual does not destabilize.

### 8.4 Hard-mount vs soft-mount: why we mandate the hard mount

A naïve instinct is to **soft-mount** the camera (rubber isolators, foam) to protect the sensor from airframe vibration and launch shock. For a strapdown seeker this is actively dangerous, and our doctrine's hard-mount mandate is the correct call.

The reason is the de-rotation identity. De-rotation assumes the camera rotates *rigidly with the gyro*: ω measured by the IMU = ω experienced by the optics. A soft mount inserts a mechanical transfer function H(s) between body and camera — a second-order spring-mass system with resonance ω_n and damping ζ:

  H(s) = ω_n² / (s² + 2ζω_n·s + ω_n²).

Now the camera experiences ω_cam = H(s)·ω_body, but the IMU (mounted on the body, or on the camera but reading a different mode) sees a *different* spectrum. The de-rotation subtracts the wrong quantity at all frequencies near ω_n:

- **Near resonance** (typical isolator ω_n ≈ 30–80 Hz for a few-gram module on soft foam), |H(jω_n)| amplifies body motion into the camera by Q = 1/(2ζ) — a factor of 3–10 for lightly damped foam. The camera sees *more* body motion than the gyro reports, so de-rotation *under-subtracts* and leaves large residual λ̇.
- **Phase lag.** Below resonance H(s) adds phase lag rising to −90° at ω_n and −180° above. De-rotation that is *out of phase* with the actual camera motion doesn't merely fail to cancel — it can **add** body motion into λ̇ (anti-cancellation at −180°). The single most safety-critical signal in the whole system (λ̇, which directly commands lateral g) gets corrupted by a passive mechanical resonance you installed to "protect" the camera.

So the soft mount converts a *broadband vibration* problem (which the camera's own thermal time constant and the tracker's temporal filtering can tolerate) into a *narrowband phase-corrupted λ̇* problem (which the guidance loop cannot). **Hard-mount is mandatory** precisely because it makes ω_cam ≡ ω_body, validating the de-rotation assumption. We accept more raw vibration on the FPA in exchange for a *trustworthy* λ̇. The residual vibration is then handled where it is safe — in the temporal/structure filtering of the detector and the IMM's process noise — never in the kinematic-truth path.

### 8.5 End-to-end latency budget and its destabilizing effect

A homing loop is a feedback loop, and pure transport delay τ_d is the classic destabilizer: it costs phase margin ω_c·τ_d (radians) at crossover ω_c without touching gain, so a high-gain terminal loop with hundreds of ms of delay simply goes unstable and misses ([predictor-observer for seeker delay, Vergez/IFAC](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405896315015177); [two-step robust LOS-rate estimation with delay](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381994895)). Our chain, itemized (order-of-magnitude, measured where noted):

| Stage | Mechanism | Latency |
|---|---|---|
| Detector integration / thermal time constant | VOx microbolometer τ_thermal | **~8–12 ms** ([AT-Sensors microbolometer basics](https://www.at-sensors.com/knowledge-base/basics-microbolometer/); the IRSX device is quoted up to 12 ms) |
| FFC (flat-field/shutter) | periodic NUC blanking | ~0 ms steady, but **blind frames** (~0.2–0.5 s) on each FFC event |
| Analog encode (CVBS) | NTSC/PAL field formation | ~half-field, ~8 ms |
| CVBS→USB grabber | digitize + USB transport + buffer | ~one frame, ~17–33 ms typical |
| Compute (Pi 5, single A76) | detect → de-rotate → IMM → command | **~17 ms measured** |
| Command actuation | servo/control surface | airframe-dependent |

The two structural facts: (1) the **microbolometer thermal time constant itself is a low-pass pole**, not just a delay — it both lags and *attenuates* fast target motion, which is a hard floor on how fast a λ̇ transient we can even observe (a target crossing the frame faster than ~1/τ_thermal smears); (2) the **CVBS analog path and USB grabber add roughly a frame of latency each** and, critically, *jitter* — the grabber buffer depth is not deterministic, which directly feeds the cam↔IMU time-sync error of Section 8.3.

Summing the deterministic terms gives a transport delay on the order of **40–70 ms** from photons to command, with the ~17 ms compute being only one slice. At a terminal closing where the "ten-tau" wall is **1.0–1.4 s**, a 50 ms delay is ~3–5% of the entire correction budget — small enough to be survivable, large enough that it *must* be compensated, not ignored.

**Compensation.** Two doctrinally-compatible tools:
- **State-lead / predictive de-rotation.** The IMM already propagates [az, el, az_rate, el_rate]; we can output the *predicted* state at t + τ_d rather than the filtered estimate at t, using the known total latency as a fixed lead. This is cheap (one propagation step) and is the practical form of a **Smith predictor** for our loop — predict the plant output forward by the known delay so the controller "sees through" the lag ([Smith predictor overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/smith-predictor); [predictor-observer for homing loops](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405896315015177)). Crucially, the lead is applied to the *kinematic* state from the IMM — it does **not** introduce any range-dependence, so it respects the doctrine invariant that range never scales the guidance.
- **Tight, characterized time-sync** so the prediction horizon τ_d is a *constant* and not a random variable. The lead compensator is only as good as the certainty of the delay it removes.

**Sampling / Nyquist limits on λ̇.** λ̇ is obtained by differentiating a bearing sampled at the frame rate f_s (≈ 25–30 Hz for CVBS, ≈ 50–60 fps best case). Differentiation is a high-pass operation: it amplifies high-frequency noise by ω and aliases any target/body motion above the Nyquist f_s/2 into the λ̇ estimate. With f_s ≈ 30 Hz, Nyquist is **15 Hz** — body twitches or rotor-induced flicker above 15 Hz fold directly into false LOS rate. This caps the usable λ̇ bandwidth to a few Hz at most after anti-alias/temporal filtering, which is comfortably above our terminal correction bandwidth (sub-Hz to ~1 Hz given the 1.0–1.4 s wall) but leaves *little margin* and forbids aggressive numerical differentiation. We must use a *filtered* derivative (the IMM's az_rate/el_rate states *are* exactly this — a model-based, noise-shaped derivative — which is the correct way to get λ̇, not finite-differencing raw centroids).

### Implications for our seeker
- **Hard-mount is non-negotiable and is a λ̇-integrity decision, not a vibration decision.** A soft mount inserts a resonant, phase-lagging transfer function (Q ≈ 3–10 near 30–80 Hz) between optics and gyro that anti-cancels de-rotation and corrupts the single most safety-critical signal. Keep the FT640 rigidly co-located with the IMU.
- **Cam↔IMU time-sync is the dominant strapdown error term, not detector noise.** With ω̇ ≈ 50 rad/s², a 5 ms skew injects ~0.25 rad/s of false λ̇ — 10× the signal. Hardware-timestamp the grabber and IMU to a common clock; treat the ~17 ms compute + grabber latency as a *measured, characterized constant*, and budget jitter explicitly.
- **Never finite-difference raw centroids for λ̇.** Use the IMM az_rate/el_rate states as the model-based filtered derivative. Raw differentiation aliases everything above the 15 Hz Nyquist (rotor flicker, body twitch) straight into the LOS-rate channel.
- **The low-gain bearing-rate nuller is the *physically* correct law given the parasitic loop.** Sub-percent gyro scale-factor and sub-degree misalignment each already produce target-signal-sized residual λ̇; a high-gain range-scaled PN law would multiply against that residual and risk parasitic-loop instability. The doctrine's refusal to range-scale is also a strapdown-stability requirement.
- **Apply a fixed state-lead (Smith-predictor-style) over the ~40–70 ms total transport delay**, propagating the IMM state forward by the known τ_d. This stays inside doctrine: the lead is purely kinematic and introduces no range dependence.
- **Budget FFC blind frames as track-coast events.** Each shutter NUC blinds the seeker for ~0.2–0.5 s; against the 1.0–1.4 s terminal wall the IMM must coast through them on prediction, and engage-permission logic must not interpret an FFC dropout as target loss.

### Open questions / frontier
- **What is our *actual* cam↔IMU time-sync residual and its jitter distribution?** Until the CVBS→USB grabber latency is characterized end-to-end (mean and variance, including FFC-correlated stalls), the dominant λ̇ error term is unquantified. This is the highest-value measurement we are missing.
- **Can we estimate gyro scale-factor / misalignment *online* during the LOBL track phase** (e.g. by correlating known commanded body motion against observed image motion) to shrink the parasitic-loop residual without a per-unit bench calibration? Self-calibration vs. factory-calibration is an open cost/accuracy trade.
- **Where exactly does the microbolometer thermal time constant (8–12 ms) cap observable λ̇ for a fast crossing CUAS target near impact?** A target that crosses a frame faster than 1/τ_thermal smears; we have not mapped the closing-geometry region where the detector itself, not the algorithm, is the limiter.
- **Is a predicted state-lead stable across the parasitic loop?** Lead compensation adds phase but also amplifies the very high-frequency residual the parasitic loop feeds back. The interaction between Smith-style prediction and scale-factor parasitic feedback needs a stability analysis on *our* loop gains, not a generic one.
- **How does roll behave?** The roll×off-boresight coupling term is first-order for an unrolled strapdown round. Do we passively roll-damp the airframe, actively estimate roll, or constrain the centroid to stay near boresight (and what does that cost in tracking a maneuvering edge-of-FOV target)?
- **FFC scheduling under doctrine.** Can FFC be *commanded* (deferred out of the terminal ten-tau window) rather than fired autonomously, so we never blind the seeker during the irreversible correction phase — and what is the NUC-drift penalty of deferring it?

## 9. Countermeasures, ECCM & Failure Modes

### 9.0 Framing: who is the adversary, and what can they actually do?

Most of the classical infrared electronic-warfare (IR EW) literature was written against a different threat than ours. It assumes a fast, cooled, *non-imaging or coarsely-imaging* MWIR (3–5 µm) reticle/rosette/pseudo-imaging seeker on a missile attacking a hot jet exhaust, defended by a well-resourced platform carrying dispensers and laser turrets. We are the *opposite end of the food chain*: an uncooled LWIR (8–14 µm) imaging seeker on a cheap interceptor, attacking a cold, slow, electrically-propelled drone whose "platform" is a hobby-grade airframe with essentially zero EW payload budget. The right way to read this section is to map each classical countermeasure onto our parameters and ask: *does the physics even reach us?* Frequently it does not — but the threats that survive that filter (cold-running, clutter exploitation, decoy saturation, perception spoofing) are precisely the ones our doctrine must absorb.

A second framing point: our cost function is *asymmetric by design*. ABORT is a PASS; a WRONG HIT is a HARD FAIL. This converts the entire countermeasures problem from "maintain track at all costs" (the air-to-air doctrine, where breaking lock means you die) to "break clean when in doubt." That single inversion makes us far more robust to most countermeasures than a same-sized cooled seeker would be, at the cost of throughput (we will abort engagements we could have won). The whole chapter should be read against that trade.

### 9.1 Classical IR countermeasures and their (ir)relevance

**Pyrotechnic flares.** Conventional MTV (magnesium/Teflon/Viton) flares radiate as a ~2000 K graybody, spectrally peaking (Wien, λ_peak ≈ 2898/T µm·K) near **1.4 µm** with most energy in SWIR/MWIR. Their entire design intent is to dominate a *MWIR* seeker's band and present a higher-contrast point source than a 600–900 K jet plume. Against an LWIR imaging seeker this is largely a category error: (i) a 2000 K source radiates relatively little of its *fractional* power in 8–14 µm, and the band-integrated contrast advantage that makes flares lethal in MWIR collapses in LWIR; (ii) our seeker is *imaging*, not a centroid-of-energy reticle — a flare appears as a distinct hot blob with its own kinematics (ballistic decel, buoyant rise, rapid cooling), trivially separable from a drone by the IMM's angular-rate state and our combined-cost association. More to the point, a small multirotor cannot carry, dispense, or thermally survive a flare. Flares are **effectively irrelevant** to us — except as a *generic hot-decoy-in-FOV* perception problem (§9.3), which is the residual we actually defend against. **Pyrophoric** ("special material," self-igniting foil) decoys generate a diffuse LWIR-relevant warm cloud and are the more LWIR-credible class in principle, but again: not a multirotor payload, and spatially diffuse rather than drone-shaped.

**DIRCM / laser jamming.** Directional IR countermeasures inject a modulated laser into the seeker to corrupt its angle-tracking demodulation; deployed systems operate predominantly in SWIR/MWIR bands matched to the *cooled* threat seekers they defend against, with LWIR sources only beginning to be explored ([EMSOPEDIA, DIRCM](https://www.emsopedia.org/entries/directed-infrared-countermeasure-dircm/); [Aivon](https://www.aivon.com/blog/measurement-test-instruments/principles-of-directional-infrared-countermeasure-systems/)). The jamming-code concept ("inject a modulation the seeker reads as valid target location") presupposes a *reticle/scanning* angle-encoding scheme — it has *nothing to grip on* in a staring imager that derives angle from a 2-D pixel centroid. The credible LWIR threat from a laser is therefore not *deception* but *energy*: dazzle/saturation. A modest LWIR laser (or even a defocused high-power source) in our FOV will bloom the microbolometer locally and, via hardware AGC, *re-map the whole 8-bit dynamic range around that source* (§9.4). That is a real attack surface — but it is a *blinding* attack we detect as a global-statistics anomaly, not a *spoofing* attack that walks our LOS. And no realistic CUAS target drone carries one. **DIRCM-as-deception: irrelevant. Laser-as-dazzle: physically possible, not weaponizable by the target class, treated as a clutter/saturation failure mode.**

**Obscurants / smoke / aerosol.** This is the classical countermeasure with the *most* LWIR relevance, because it is pure propagation physics and band-honest. Clear-air LWIR extinction at 10 µm is tiny — order **4.5×10⁻³ km⁻¹ (~0.02 dB/km) clear, ~0.08 dB/km hazy** ([ScienceDirect, atmospheric attenuation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/engineering/atmospheric-attenuation)) — negligible over our **115–190 m DETECT / 18–30 m ID** envelope (a few thousandths of a dB). LWIR famously *sees through* visible-band smoke and light fog far better than the eye, which is the whole reason it is the CUAS band of choice. But the protection is *not* unconditional: aerosols whose particle radius approaches the wavelength (water fog droplets ~5–15 µm, deliberate IR-tuned obscurant: graphite flake, brass/metal-flake screening smoke) scatter strongly in LWIR. Dense fog drives LWIR loss to ~0.2–0.8+ dB/km ([fog scattering figures](https://www.ece.mcmaster.ca/~hranilovic/woc/resources/local/spie2000b.pdf)); a purpose-built IR screening smoke is worse. Over 30 m that is recoverable; over 190 m a deliberate graphite screen could erase a marginal-contrast drone signature. **Obscurants are the one classical CM that is both physically effective in our band and conceivably battlefield-emplaced** — though emplacing a screen around a maneuvering FPV target is an area-denial act, not a per-drone self-protect measure. For us it degrades DETECT range and contrast (ΔT collapse), which our default-deny posture converts to an abort rather than a mis-hit.

### 9.2 What the target drone can actually do (self-protection within the threat class)

Strip away everything a multirotor cannot carry, and the residual target playbook is *thermal* and *kinematic*:

- **Running cold / low-emissivity skinning.** Our entire detection chain lives on ΔT — the apparent radiometric contrast between drone skin and sky/ground background. The signal scales as the band-integrated Planck derivative: in LWIR, dL/dT ≈ const over our small ΔT, so detectable SNR ∝ ε·A·ΔT / (NETD-limited noise), with **NETD ~50 mK** for the FT640 class. A drone that (i) minimizes self-heating (efficient ESCs, ducted/low-RPM props, shaded battery), (ii) wraps motors/battery — the hot spots — in **low-emissivity (low-ε) tape or coating** (bare aluminized Mylar can hit ε ≈ 0.05–0.1 vs ε ≈ 0.9 for painted plastic), and (iii) flies on a *cloudy day against a thermally uniform overcast sky* (sky and drone within a few K) can collapse ΔT from a comfortable ~5–15 K down to **<1 K**, i.e. only ~20× NETD or worse. Because we have **no radiometry — 8-bit CVBS with hardware AGC, not 16-bit Y16** — we cannot even measure absolute ΔT; we infer contrast post-AGC, after the scene's own dynamic range has competed for our 256 levels. This is the *single most effective, most accessible, and least defeasible* countermeasure against us. It is **not spoofing and not jamming — it is signature denial**, and it pushes the engagement from "honest envelope" straight into "abort." We cannot solve it; we can only honestly *fail to detect* (a PASS), which is acceptable, versus *false-lock on warmer clutter* (a FAIL), which our anti-pull-off logic must prevent.

- **Erratic high-g jinking inside our envelope.** Our airframe is limited to **≤0.84 g lateral (θ_max 40°)** and our *honest* envelope is non-/mildly-maneuvering targets (<~0.28 g). A small quad can pull several g instantaneously. The relevant physics is the **"ten-tau" terminal wall**: in the last ~1.0–1.4 s, time-to-go t_go is too small for our limited-g body to null an LOS-rate excursion the target induces. A target that *withholds* its jink until inside that wall imposes a miss our control authority physically cannot remove — this is a *fundamental* (not merely hard) limitation set by the (a_target/a_interceptor) ratio and t_go, the same algebra that bounds classical PN miss distance, made worse for us because we **cannot range-scale gain** and therefore cannot tighten the loop as closure increases. **Late, hard jinking is a real and partly-unsolvable kill defeat** — but note it produces a *miss* (a PASS-equivalent), not a wrong hit. It is throughput loss, not safety loss.

- **Swarm / decoy saturation.** Multiple drones (or one drone trailing a cheap warm decoy — a resistor-heated foam glider, a thermal-tape kite) present multiple credible blobs. With LOBL we commit to *one* track; the attack is to make association ambiguous so we either (i) split/wander between tracks or (ii) pull off onto the decoy. Our defense is the **combined-cost association** (kinematic gate + temporal persistence + structural/shape consistency) and the doctrine that *the AI may only lower engage-permission, never repaint the centroid*. A decoy that is kinematically inconsistent (ballistic, drifting) is rejected by the IMM rate state; one that is co-flying and drone-shaped is, by construction, nearly as good a target as the real one — at which point hitting *either* may be acceptable, or, if confidence splits, we abort. **Saturation degrades selection confidence, which by default-deny becomes an abort.**

- **Hiding against hot clutter or the sun.** Flying a cold drone *across* a hot rooftop, a sun-warmed wall, or — worst case — **transiting the solar disk or a bright cloud edge** in our FOV destroys local contrast and invites the AGC to renormalize against the clutter, not the target (§9.4). Sun in LWIR is not the overwhelming source it is in SWIR, but a sun-warmed background or specular glint off water/metal can exceed drone ΔT. **This is the bridge from "target tactic" to "intrinsic failure mode" (§9.3–9.4): the drone doesn't spoof us, it maneuvers us into our own chain's weak points.**

### 9.3 Spoofing the *perception* (the residual deception surface)

Because deception against our *angle channel* is nearly impossible (no reticle to fool), the only deception surface is the *blob/discriminator/association* logic that decides *which* blob is the target:

- **Hot-object decoys in FOV.** A warm point source (flare-equivalent, a thrown chemical heat pack, a co-flown heater) that out-contrasts the drone post-AGC is the canonical pull-off bait. Defeated *only* by kinematic + structural consistency, never by intensity (intensity is exactly what AGC corrupts).
- **Solar glint / specular modulated reflections.** Rotating prop blades or a tilted reflective panel can chop sunlight/skylight into a *modulated* IR return. A reticle seeker would be vulnerable to modulation; we are not in the angle channel, but a learned *discriminator* trained on "drone-like temporal texture" could be fooled by an engineered flicker. Mitigation: the learned component is *firewalled to lock-quality/engage-permission only* — a fooled discriminator can *grant or deny permission*, but cannot move the LOS or the centroid. Worst case is a false *deny* (PASS, safe) or a false *grant* on a non-drone (caught by the kinematic/structural gates that are *not* learned).
- **Adversarial patterns vs a learned discriminator.** A printed adversarial thermal pattern (engineered emissivity texture) could in principle flip a CNN classifier. This is real for vision systems but **structurally defanged in our design**: (i) the discriminator is one vote among non-learned kinematic/temporal/structural votes; (ii) it can only touch engage-permission; (iii) LWIR adversarial patches are *far* harder to realize than visible ones — you are painting in *emissivity/temperature*, an 8–14 µm channel with ~50 mK resolution and AGC nonlinearity in front of the network, which destroys the precise pixel-value control adversarial attacks require. **Adversarial spoofing is plausible against the permission bit, impossible against the LOS, and the asymmetric cost makes a spoofed *deny* free and a spoofed *grant* still gated by physics.**

### 9.4 Intrinsic failure modes of the chain (no adversary required)

These occur unprompted and are the failures an adversary will *try to induce*:

- **FFC blackout window.** The uncooled array needs periodic flat-field correction; a mechanical shutter closes (deploying on a timer ~minutes, or on detector-temperature change — [arXiv 2302.05198](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.05198)) for tens to ~hundreds of ms, during which the imager is *blind*. With a thermal time constant of ~**7–12 ms** ([AT Sensors](https://www.at-sensors.com/knowledge-base/basics-microbolometer/)) and our **~17 ms/frame** pipeline, an FFC event drops *several frames*. Within the terminal wall this is potentially fatal to track. **Mitigation: FFC coast** — propagate the IMM angular state through the blackout on its constant-velocity-in-angle prediction, with inflated covariance, and *suppress association/re-acquire decisions until valid frames resume*. An adversary who could *trigger* FFC (rapid scene-temperature swing — e.g., sweeping a hot source across FOV) gets a free blind window; this is a reason to *gate, not trust*, the frames immediately around an FFC.
- **AGC saturation / pumping.** Hardware 8-bit AGC continuously rescales the scene to 256 levels. A hot intruder (sun, fire, the decoy) entering FOV **re-maps the whole histogram**, dimming the drone toward the noise floor and changing its apparent contrast frame-to-frame ("pumping"). This directly attacks blob detection and any intensity-based feature. **Mitigation: never trust absolute or relative intensity for association; rely on kinematics/structure; monitor global histogram statistics as a *health/abort* signal.**
- **Lock pull-off onto a hotter intruder / clutter.** The composite failure: AGC favors the hotter object, the blob detector hands the association a brighter candidate, and a naive nearest-neighbor tracker migrates. **Mitigation: combined-cost association with kinematic-gate priority + temporal persistence + structural consistency, explicitly weighted so that *intensity cannot override kinematics*** — this is the anti-pull-off design and it is the single most important ECCM mechanism we own.
- **Horizon / cloud-edge lock.** Long, high-contrast LWIR edges (horizon, cloud boundary, rooftop line) are gradient-rich and can capture an edge/feature tracker, especially as a drone crosses them. **Mitigation: structural rejection of extended/linear features (a drone is a compact blob, not an edge), and abort if the track snaps to a persistent extended structure.**
- **Ego-mis-registration.** We are **strapdown with gyro de-rotation**: every frame, body angular rate is subtracted to separate target LOS-rate from our own rotation. Gyro bias, scale-factor error, vibration aliasing, or grabber/gyro timestamp skew injects a *false LOS-rate* — which a λ-dot nuller will faithfully try to null, *steering us off the true target*. This is uniquely dangerous because it corrupts the very quantity guidance acts on. **Mitigation: tight time-sync, vibration isolation, bias estimation in the IMM, and — critically — sanity bounds: a commanded correction that implies an implausible target maneuver triggers abort rather than execution.**

### 9.5 Why "abort-is-a-pass / default-deny" is the principled backstop

Every failure and countermeasure above resolves into one of two outcomes: **we lose the target (miss/PASS)** or **we lock the wrong thing (wrong hit/FAIL)**. The asymmetric cost function makes these radically unequal — and the *entire* robustness argument is that our design converts as many FAIL-paths as possible into PASS-paths:

- Signature denial (cold-running, low-ε, obscurant ΔT collapse) → **no detection → PASS**. Fundamentally unsolvable, doctrinally acceptable.
- Late hard jink inside the ten-tau wall → **miss → PASS**. Physically unsolvable by our g-limit, doctrinally acceptable.
- Pull-off bait, hot clutter, AGC pumping, edge capture, ego-error → would be **wrong hit → FAIL**, *but* are intercepted by combined-cost anti-pull-off association, FFC coast, structural rejection, and the **abort ceiling** (confidence/health below threshold ⇒ break clean). Default-deny means *doubt resolves to PASS, never to a guess.*
- Spoofed learned discriminator → constrained by firewall to the permission bit; spoofed *deny* is a PASS, spoofed *grant* is still gated by non-learned physics.

The doctrine is not a safety nicety bolted on top; it is *the* mechanism that makes a cheap, uncooled, range-blind seeker tolerable. We accept large throughput loss (we will abort engagements a cooled seeker would win) to drive wrong-hit probability toward zero. Against an adversary whose best moves (cold-running, jinking, saturation) all produce *misses* rather than *mis-hits* in our cost frame, that is the correct surrender.

### Implications for our seeker

- **Treat flares and DIRCM-deception as out-of-scope** for the target class and band; budget engineering effort instead on **cold-running / low-ε signature denial and obscurants**, the only classical CMs that physically reach an LWIR CUAS engagement.
- **Harden the chain at the AGC/FFC seams, not the angle channel.** Our angle channel is nearly un-spoofable; our *intensity and registration* channels are the soft targets. Enforce "intensity never drives association," ship FFC-coast with association suppression across the blackout, and add global-histogram health monitoring as an abort input.
- **Make ego-registration a first-class safety item.** Time-sync, vibration isolation, IMM bias estimation, and *implausible-maneuver* sanity bounds on the guidance command — because a de-rotation error is the one fault that actively steers us onto the wrong target.
- **Keep the learned component firewalled to engage-permission**; verify in test that no adversarial/glint input can perturb centroid or LOS, only the permission bit, and that a spoofed permission *grant* is still vetoed by kinematic/structural gates.
- **Tune the abort ceiling against measured pull-off/saturation statistics**, accepting throughput loss: a higher abort rate is the price of near-zero wrong-hit probability, and that trade is doctrinally correct.

### Open questions / frontier

- **Quantify the ΔT-denial floor empirically.** What is the realistic minimum apparent ΔT a low-ε, cold-run drone presents against worst-case overcast through our *8-bit AGC* pipeline (not radiometrically)? Where exactly does DETECT collapse, and can temporal/structural cues recover any of it?
- **FFC-trigger exploitation.** Can an adversary deterministically *induce* an FFC blackout by sweeping a hot source across FOV, and how many terminal-wall engagements does that cost? Is a shutter-less NUC variant worth the integration risk to remove the blind window entirely?
- **Obscurant threat realism.** Is battlefield-emplaced IR-tuned screening smoke (graphite/metal-flake) a credible per-engagement defeat at 100–190 m, or only an area-denial nuisance? Need measured LWIR transmission through candidate screens.
- **Adversarial LWIR patches under AGC.** Empirically, can *any* printed emissivity pattern survive 50 mK + 8-bit AGC nonlinearity to flip our discriminator's permission bit? If unattackable in practice, we can relax constraints; if attackable, the firewall must be proven airtight.
- **Co-flying decoy ambiguity.** When a kinematically- and structurally-consistent warm decoy co-flies with the real drone, what is the correct doctrine — abort, or accept that hitting either is mission-acceptable? This is a rules-of-engagement question physics cannot answer alone.
- **Ego-error vs target-maneuver disambiguation.** Can the IMM/innovation sequence reliably distinguish a real target jink from a gyro/registration fault in the terminal phase, where both manifest as sudden LOS-rate? If not, both must route to abort — quantify the resulting throughput cost.

## 10. Fundamental Limits & the Honest Performance Envelope

This chapter is the bill for everything the preceding physics chapters spent. It converts radiometry, optics, estimation theory, and guidance kinematics into a single quantitative ledger for *our* round — a Foxeer FT640 V2 uncooled LWIR core, 8-bit hardware-AGC CVBS, ~48.7° × 38.6° FOV, ~50 mK NETD, passive monocular strapdown, on a Pi-5 with a measured ~17 ms/frame pipeline — and states, regime by regime, what is **fundamentally impossible** versus **merely hard**, and where each regime's performance binds.

### 10.1 The acquisition budget: detection and ID range from the radiometric + Johnson chain

**Pixel-on-target is the master variable.** The FT640 V2 is a 640×512 array over 48.7° × 38.6°, giving an instantaneous field of view (IFOV) of

```
IFOV ≈ 48.7° / 640 ≈ 0.0761°/px ≈ 1.33 mrad/px   (horizontal)
```

A small quadrotor presents a critical thermal dimension *d* of roughly 0.10–0.25 m (motor pods, battery, ESC plate — the airframe itself is near-ambient). The angular subtense is θ = d/R, and the number of pixels across target is

```
N_px(R) = d / (R · IFOV) = d / (R · 1.33e-3)
```

Johnson/NVThermIP criteria (50% task probability) require roughly **1.0 cycle** for detection, **3–4 cycles** for recognition, **6–6.4 cycles** for identification, where 1 cycle ≈ 2 pixels at ideal (Nyquist) sampling ([Wikipedia: Johnson's criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson's_criteria); [Kintronics DRI](https://kintronics.com/detection-recognition-identification-new-criteria/)). So detection needs N_px ≈ 2, ID needs N_px ≈ 13.

Inverting for a d = 0.18 m effective hot dimension:

```
Detection (N=2):  R_det = 0.18 / (2 · 1.33e-3)  ≈ 68 m   (50% Johnson, ideal)
ID        (N=13): R_id  = 0.18 / (13 · 1.33e-3) ≈ 10 m
```

These are the *geometry-only* numbers and they are **optimistic** — they assume the target is resolved against zero clutter at infinite SNR. Our stated honest envelope (DETECT 115–190 m, ID 18–30 m) is *larger* than the bare 2-px Johnson number, which tells you something important: **at our ranges, detection is not resolution-limited, it is contrast-limited.** A drone hot-spot smaller than one pixel is still detectable as a *sub-pixel point source* if its in-band irradiance clears the AGC-degraded noise floor. The relevant figure of merit shifts from "cycles across target" to **point-source SNR**:

```
SNR = ΔT_apparent / NETD_eff
```

where ΔT_apparent is the target's *apparent* temperature contrast at the aperture after the hot motor/ESC energy is diluted across the pixel solid angle and attenuated by path transmittance τ_atm. A motor running 20–40 K above ambient, smeared over a sub-pixel footprint and a 100–190 m path (LWIR τ ≈ 0.7–0.9/km in clear air, far worse in haze/humidity), can collapse to an apparent ΔT of a few hundred mK — only a handful of NETD's. That is why our DETECT band is *wide and conditional*: it is a humidity-, aspect-, and motor-load-dependent contrast race, not a clean resolution threshold.

**The 8-bit AGC tax.** A 16-bit Y16 radiometric core preserves the full ~50 mK NETD as quantization-negligible. Our CVBS path quantizes the *AGC-companded* scene to 8 bits (256 levels). When AGC stretches a high-dynamic-range scene (sky + sun-warmed ground + target), one DN can map to several hundred mK of scene temperature, so the **effective NETD seen by the algorithm is AGC-state-dependent and can be 3–10× the sensor's intrinsic 50 mK.** This is a *merely hard* (mitigable by temporal integration, never solved) loss, and it is the single largest controllable degradation in the acquisition budget. Frame-to-frame temporal averaging of a stationary-on-FPA point recovers √N in SNR; at ~58 fps a 0.5 s dwell buys √29 ≈ 5.4×, partially clawing back the AGC tax — but only while ego-motion keeps the target inside a small de-rotated gate.

**Acquisition binding constraint:** *apparent point-source contrast vs. AGC-degraded noise floor*, dominated by atmospheric transmittance and motor thermal load. Resolution (Johnson cycles) only binds at the ID end of the chain.

### 10.2 The capture cone, not a sphere

A strapdown body-fixed seeker with a ±~19–24° half-FOV and a hard ≤0.84 g lateral envelope (θ_max ≈ 40°) cannot engage omnidirectionally. The reachable set is a **forward cone**, bounded by two independent limits:

1. **Field-of-regard limit:** the target must stay within the optical FOV through the de-rotated track. With no gimbal, every body rotation costs FOV budget; the usable acquisition cone half-angle is roughly the optical half-FOV minus the worst-case body-attitude excursion during the correction, call it ~15–18° half-angle in practice.
2. **Kinematic-reachability limit:** with lateral authority a_lat ≤ 0.84 g ≈ 8.24 m/s² and a closing geometry, the heading-correction the round can fly before the terminal wall sets the maximum off-boresight initial geometry it can null. For a closing speed V_c and time-to-go t_go, the maximum lateral position correction is ≈ ½·a_lat·t_go², i.e. the cone is *closing-speed and time dependent*.

The product of these is a **near-head-on cone**, consistent with the stated envelope. A tail-chase or high-aspect crossing target either leaves the FOV (FOR-limited) or demands lateral g we do not have (kinematic-limited). This is geometry, not tuning: **the engageable solid angle is a small forward cap, on the order of a few percent of 4π**, and broadening it requires either a gimbal (more FOR) or more g (more reachability) — both outside the Block-3 body envelope.

### 10.3 Terminal miss vs. target lateral-g: the observability split

Here is the cleanest impossible/hard boundary in the whole system.

**The fundamentally impossible part.** Passive monocular bearings-only measurement of a target with unknown size makes **range and range-rate unobservable** from a non-maneuvering (or radially-maneuvering) platform: the Fisher information matrix for the range coordinate is singular and the CRLB → ∞. This is textbook bearings-only TMA: a unique solution requires *ownship maneuver that is not along the line of sight*, and even then certain maneuvers leave the system unobservable despite nonzero bearing rate ([Nardone & Aidala, observability criteria](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Observability-Criteria-for-Bearings-Only-Target-Motion-Nardone-Aidala/3e40ae620c1a3619efb1c84a4fb228727b5f28d4); [observability when observer maneuvers smoothly](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319364377)). A strapdown interceptor on a collision triangle is, by *design*, flying to **null** bearing rate — i.e., it is deliberately removing the very excitation that would make range observable. We cannot have both a collision course and good passive range. This is not an engineering gap; it is a singularity. Hence our doctrine: **never let range scale the guidance gain.**

**Why the nuller is the correct surrender.** Classical PN commands a_cmd = N·V_c·λ̇ — it *needs* closing speed V_c, which is unobservable for us. A pure **λ̇-nuller** (drive line-of-sight rate to zero) is the projective-invariant residue of PN that survives when V_c and R are unknown: on a constant-bearing collision geometry, λ̇ = 0 *is* the intercept condition independent of range. The cost we pay is the classic PN result that **miss distance against a maneuvering target scales with target acceleration and the square of the effective guidance time constant**: for a step target maneuver a_T entering with time-to-go t_go and effective system lag τ,

```
miss ≈ a_T · τ² · f(t_go/τ)          (PN family, ZEM form)
```

with f(·) a dimensionless function that saturates for t_go ≫ τ ([ZEM-shaping guidance, Lee & Shin](https://dspace.lib.cranfield.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/f79e80a8-de46-4b52-a2c3-22995a8cbb80/content); [SIAM, PN optimal homing/evasion](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/pdf/10.1137/130947301)). Two consequences for us:

- **Without range observability we cannot run augmented PN** (APN needs an estimate of a_T *and* V_c to add the ½·N·a_T·t_go² lead term). So our miss against a maneuvering target is the *un-augmented* PN miss — strictly worse than a radiometric/two-color or active-ranging seeker against the same maneuver. This is a real, quantifiable penalty, not a wash.
- The miss is dominated by the **last few τ of flight** ("ten-tau" terminal wall, ~1.0–1.4 s). A maneuver that begins inside ~1 τ of impact is **uncorrectable** regardless of guidance law — there is no time to fly out the ZEM. Against our ≤0.28 g honest-target assumption the residual miss is sub-airframe; against a >~0.3–0.5 g step inside the wall it diverges.

**Maneuver-headroom limit at 0.84 g.** The interceptor can only null bearing rates it can physically out-accelerate. The condition to hold a collision triangle against a target pulling a_T is roughly a_interceptor ≥ (V_c/V_m)·a_T-class terms; with only 0.84 g of body-lateral authority, the **headroom ratio** a_lat/a_T must stay comfortably above ~3:1 for the nuller to keep λ̇ bounded. That puts the *hard* maneuver ceiling on engageable targets at roughly **a_T ≲ 0.25–0.28 g** — exactly the stated honest envelope, which is therefore physics-derived, not arbitrary. Beyond it, commanded λ̇-correction saturates against the 0.84 g wall and miss grows without bound.

### 10.4 The λ̇ measurement-noise floor → terminal accuracy

Even for a non-maneuvering target, miss is floored by **angular measurement noise propagated through the guidance**. The seeker measures bearing to a per-frame angular accuracy σ_θ set by centroid jitter on a low-contrast, AGC-quantized blob. A reasonable budget: sub-pixel centroiding on a ~few-pixel blob gives σ_θ ≈ 0.1–0.3 px ≈ 0.13–0.40 mrad, degraded by AGC flicker and platform micro-vibration. The λ̇ estimate is a *differentiated* angle, so its noise is amplified by the tracker bandwidth:

```
σ_λ̇ ≈ σ_θ · √(bandwidth-dependent factor) / Δt_eff
```

Our IMM on the 4-state modified-polar angular vector [az, el, az_rate, el_rate] is precisely the right machine to suppress this: it trades responsiveness (needed late) for smoothing (needed early) via model-probability weighting. But the floor is real — terminal angular accuracy can't beat the centroid σ_θ, and the **terminal lateral miss from noise alone** is approximately

```
miss_noise ≈ R_terminal · σ_θ(residual after IMM smoothing)
```

At the ~1 s terminal wall, even with R unobservable, the *angular* miss maps to lateral miss through the (unknown) range — which is why we specify success as **angular nulling to within the target's angular extent**, not a metric miss distance. If σ_θ residual ≈ 0.2 mrad and the target subtends several mrad in the endgame, the noise floor is comfortably inside the airframe. The binding term in the endgame is therefore **not noise** but **(a) target maneuver inside the wall and (b) AGC/contrast loss causing centroid bias or track-break**, in that order.

### 10.5 Discrimination AUC ceiling at 8-bit single band

We have **one** intensity channel (no two-color, no Y16 radiometry, no polarization). Information-theoretically, single-band 8-bit AGC intensity carries far less class-separating information than a radiometric or multi-band signature. The achievable discrimination ROC — bird vs. quad, friendly-vs-hostile drone, clutter-vs-target — is therefore *ceiling-limited* by available features:

- **Intensity/shape alone (per-frame):** weak. A bird and a small drone at 100 m are both sub-resolution warm blobs; AUC on a single frame is plausibly only ~0.6–0.75. This is the *merely hard* regime where single-band hurts most.
- **+ kinematics (track-level):** drones hold straighter, faster, more powered tracks than birds; flapping birds show characteristic ~Hz periodicity. Track features lift AUC substantially — to perhaps ~0.85–0.92 — but require a *maintained track*, which the acquisition contrast budget doesn't always grant.
- **+ temporal/structure (micro-motion, rotor flicker):** rotor blade-pass and frame-to-frame structure add discriminating energy, pushing the realistic operational ceiling toward ~0.90–0.95 under good contrast, degrading sharply as range and AGC quantization erode the features.

This is **mitigated, never solved.** The honest statement: at the 8-bit single-band physical layer, we cannot reach the AUC of a two-color or radiometric seeker, and there is a hard floor where birds and small drones are *genuinely ambiguous* to our sensor. Doctrine converts this limit into safety: discrimination feeds **engage-permission / lock-quality only**, never the centroid or LOS, and **ABORT is a PASS while a WRONG HIT is a HARD FAIL** — so the asymmetric cost is paid by defaulting to deny under discrimination doubt, exactly where the AUC ceiling bites.

### 10.6 Latency–stability margin

The guidance loop is a sampled feedback system with measured pipeline latency ~17 ms/frame plus tracker and actuator lag, aggregating into the effective τ. Loop stability requires the total phase lag at the guidance crossover to stay bounded; the classic rule of thumb is that **effective time delay should be a small fraction (≲10–20%) of the guidance time constant τ**. With a ~1.0–1.4 s terminal τ-scale, a ~17 ms sensing latency plus, say, 30–60 ms of total transport/compute/actuation delay is ~3–6% of τ — **comfortably inside the stability margin** in midcourse. The risk is not steady-state instability; it is **latency-induced miss growth in the last τ**, where the loop has too few correction cycles left, and **frame-drop / track-break transients** on the single-core Pi-5 (a dropped frame doubles instantaneous Δt and momentarily halves loop bandwidth). The binding latency concern is therefore *jitter and dropouts in the endgame*, not nominal delay.

### 10.7 The impossible/hard ledger and the scorecard

| Quantity | Value (honest envelope) | Class | Binding constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Detection range | ~115–190 m (contrast-limited) | hard | apparent ΔT vs AGC noise floor, τ_atm |
| ID range | ~18–30 m (Johnson 6-cyc) | hard | resolution (cycles-on-target) |
| Passive range / closing speed | unobservable, CRLB → ∞ | **impossible** | bearings-only Fisher singularity |

**Defensible round-level scorecard (near-head-on, mildly maneuvering, good contrast):** within the honest envelope we should hold **Pd ≈ 0.8–0.9** at acquisition, a **low Pfa** *enforced by default-deny* rather than by sensor separability, **terminal miss inside the airframe** for a_T ≲ 0.28 g targets that do not maneuver inside the last τ, and **discrimination AUC ≈ 0.9** at track level. **The round succeeds** when: target is in the forward cone, contrast clears the AGC floor long enough to build a track, target stays below ~0.28 g and does not step-maneuver inside the terminal wall, and the Pi-5 holds frame cadence. **The round must abort** when: lock quality / discrimination confidence falls below threshold (ambiguous bird-vs-drone), track breaks under AGC/contrast loss, target geometry leaves the cone, or a late maneuver saturates the 0.84 g authority — and by doctrine, **abort is the correct, non-penalized outcome.**

### Implications for our seeker
- **Engage only inside the physics cone.** The ~0.28 g maneuver ceiling and ~15–18° forward half-cone are *derived* limits, not tunables — bake them into LOBL hand-off geometry and refuse off-cone or high-aspect setups by default.
- **Spend the SNR budget on temporal integration, not on chasing range.** A 0.3–0.5 s de-rotated dwell recovers ~3–5× of the AGC tax; range estimation is a dead end (CRLB → ∞) and must never enter the guidance gain.
- **Protect the terminal wall.** The dominant miss source is target maneuver inside the last ~1 τ; prioritize early λ̇-nulling so the ZEM is small *before* the wall, and treat any endgame frame-drop as a high-severity event.
- **Keep AI strictly on the permission side.** The 8-bit single-band AUC ceiling means discrimination is fallible; route every learned output to lock-quality/engage-permission and default-deny, never to centroid/LOS/tracker — this turns a sensor limit into a safety property.
- **Specify miss angularly, not metrically.** Since range is unobservable, success criteria, test scoring, and IMM tuning should all be in bearing/λ̇ space (null to within target angular extent), consistent with what the sensor can actually observe.

### Open questions / frontier
- **Quantify the real σ_θ centroid floor** on AGC-quantized CVBS blobs across contrast and vibration — the endgame miss model is only as good as this number, and we are currently estimating it.
- **Bound the AGC-effective NETD** as a function of scene dynamic range empirically; the 3–10× degradation factor is an estimate, and it directly sets DETECT range.
- **Is there safe, observability-respecting micro-maneuver?** A small non-radial ownship weave could partially de-singularize range without breaking the collision triangle — but it costs g-headroom and may worsen miss; the trade is unquantified.
- **AUC vs. range curve for bird/clutter rejection** under our exact pipeline — where exactly does single-band discrimination collapse, and does temporal rotor-flicker survive AGC at our DETECT ranges?
- **Pi-5 frame-cadence reliability in the wall** — characterize the tail of the frame-time distribution under thermal/CPU load; a rare 2-frame stall in the last τ is a plausible silent failure mode.
- **Two-color or polarization retrofit** — the single largest lever on the discrimination ceiling is a second channel; what is the minimum hardware change that breaks the 8-bit single-band information limit without exceeding the Block-3 SWaP budget?

## 11. Research Frontier & Upgrade Paths

This chapter is the upgrade agenda — a ranked, science-driven backlog of how to make the Block-3 interceptor *less blind* without violating the doctrine invariants that keep it safe. The organizing principle is leverage-per-unit-risk: every candidate is scored against three axes — (1) how much it relaxes a *fundamental* limit versus merely a *practical* one, (2) its SWaP/compute cost on a single Cortex-A76 quad-core with a ~17 ms/frame budget, and (3) whether it can be integrated *without* letting a learned or external signal touch the centroid / LOS / tracker. That last constraint is not negotiable: the doctrine says range never scales gain, AI writes only to lock-quality/engage-permission, ABORT is a PASS and a WRONG HIT is a HARD FAIL. An "upgrade" that requires breaking those rules is not an upgrade; it is a different, more dangerous weapon.

I divide the agenda into **Sensor-layer** upgrades (which change what physics we can see) and **Algorithm/compute-layer** upgrades (which change what we extract from what we already see). Sensor upgrades dominate the leverage ranking, because our deepest pain — range/closing-speed unobservability and single-band 8-bit discrimination — is set by the *physics of the input channel*, and no amount of cleverness downstream recovers Fisher information that was never collected.

### 11.1 The governing constraint: what we are actually trying to buy

Two fundamental limits define our current envelope. First, **range observability**. For a passive monocular strapdown seeker the measurement is a unit LOS vector u(t); range r enters the dynamics only multiplicatively (ṙ and r are coupled through the same bearing-rate equation), so the Fisher information matrix for r is singular and CRLB(r) → ∞ for non-maneuvering, non-accelerating own-ship geometry. This is *fundamentally impossible* to fix with better processing of the same channel — it is why our guidance correctly surrenders to a λ̇-nuller rather than faking a range-scaled PN gain. Only a new *physical* channel (a ranging measurement, or self-induced parallax with known own-motion) restores it. Second, **discrimination**. Our FT640 V2 delivers an 8-bit, hardware-AGC, CVBS-digitized luminance frame — not radiometric Y16. The AGC continuously remaps the scene, so absolute apparent temperature is destroyed frame-to-frame; we have a *relative*, drifting, ~8-bit contrast channel against a ~50 mK NETD floor. Discrimination is therefore *hard, not impossible* — it is recoverable in principle by restoring radiometry and/or adding a spectral axis.

Everything below is, in effect, a way to buy back one of these two quantities.

### 11.2 Sensor-layer upgrades (ranked)

**(1) Radiometric 16-bit Y16 core — HIGHEST leverage, lowest doctrinal risk.** This is the single highest-return change and should be treated as the default next hardware step. Moving from analog CVBS/8-bit-AGC to a digital 16-bit radiometric core (e.g., a Boson/Tau-class or equivalent module exposing Y16) gives us *absolute, stable* apparent-temperature per pixel. Concretely it buys: (a) **absolute CFAR** — a constant-false-alarm-rate detector thresholding on true ΔT against a slowly-varying sky-radiance background, instead of our current AGC-relative blob/MTI heuristics; (b) **frame stability** — no AGC "breathing," so temporal filters (our IMM innovations, MTI residuals) are no longer corrupted by a global gain that jumps when a hot object enters/leaves the field; (c) ~8 extra bits of dynamic range, letting a faint drone-against-cold-sky (ΔT possibly only a few hundred mK over background) survive without being crushed by AGC quantization. Uncooled LWIR NETD is ~30–50 mK for VOx/a-Si microbolometers ([LightPath](https://www.lightpath.com/blog/making-the-right-choice-lwir-vs-mwir-for-your-thermal-imaging-strategy), [Pembroke](https://pembrokeinstruments.com/resources/thermal-imaging-scientific-guide-to-long-wave-infrared-lwir/sensor-technology-cooled-vs-uncooled-systems/)) — but that floor is *useless to us today* because 8-bit AGC throws away most of the bits between it and saturation. Y16 is the upgrade that lets us actually *use* the NETD we already paid for. Doctrinally it is clean: better measurement quality flows into the existing tracker and into lock-quality scoring; it does not invent range and does not touch the λ̇ law. SWaP is favorable (similar core size, USB/CSI digital path removes the lossy CVBS→USB grabber and likely *cuts* the 17 ms pipeline). **Verdict: do this first.**

**(2) Terminal single-point ToF / LIDAR ranger — HIGH leverage, the only thing that restores range observability.** A single bore-sighted ToF or short-range LIDAR ranger (1.5 µm eye-safer band, single-pixel or few-pixel) is the *only* candidate that attacks the fundamental unobservability rather than the discrimination problem. Range from time-of-flight is r = c·Δt/2; for the endgame band of interest (say 5–150 m) Δt spans ~33 ns–1 µs, trivially resolvable. Crucially it is needed *only in the terminal wall* — the last ~1.0–1.4 s "ten-tau" — where the target subtends enough pixels that a 1–3° divergence single beam stays on it. With measured r(t) we recover ṙ by differentiation and can, **for the commit window only**, switch from the λ̇-nuller to a *real* Zero-Effort-Miss / Augmented-PN law with a genuine decreasing-range gain and a physically-grounded time-to-go τ = −r/ṙ. This is doctrinally delicate but *defensible*: it does not retroactively make the mid-course PN "range-scaled" (that invariant holds where range is unobservable); it *adds* observability so the invariant's precondition no longer applies in the endgame. Guardrails are mandatory: the ToF range must gate as engage-permission/quality input first, and only promote to a ZEM law when range innovations are self-consistent (set-membership validated) — a spoofed or dropout ranger must fail *closed* to the λ̇-nuller, never open to a hallucinated commit. Risks: LIDAR is an *active emitter* (counter-detectable; against a CUAS target that rarely matters), atmospheric/obscurant dropout, beam-pointing on a strapdown body during high-rate terminal maneuver, and cost/SWaP of a ranging module + its own timing electronics. **Verdict: the marquee Phase-D/E item; build the law and the fail-closed gate before the hardware exists.**

**(3) Dual-band / two-color discrimination — HIGH leverage for the WRONG-HIT problem.** Our asymmetric cost function makes false target ID the dominant failure; two-color is the textbook countermeasure-and-clutter discriminant. A two-color seeker comparing a 3–5 µm band against an 8–12 µm band yields a band *ratio* R = L₁/L₂ that, via Planck, encodes color temperature largely independent of (unknown) emissivity and absolute range — emissivity-free colorimetric thermography derives temperature from the inter-band signal ratio ([Wiley/Adv. Sci.](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.202408683)). Operationally this is how IR missiles reject flares and clutter: comparing emissions across bands lets the seeker reject a countermeasure whose spectral signature differs from the target ([ScienceDirect, two-color seekers](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1350449514000759)). For us the payoff is *discrimination*, not range: a band ratio is a strong, AGC-immune feature for "is this a drone or a hot rooftop/sun-glint/bird," feeding lock-quality and engage-permission — exactly where the doctrine permits a discriminant. Cost: a true two-color FPA or a filter-wheel/metalens dual-band path adds optics, registration, and often a cooled MWIR channel (InSb-class, ~77 K, NETD <20 mK — [LightPath](https://www.lightpath.com/insights/ir-imaging-comparison-the-basics)), which blows the uncooled SWaP envelope. **Verdict: high value, high integration cost; prototype the discriminant offline on dual-band imagery before committing optics.**

**(4) Event-camera (DVS) fusion for low-latency λ̇ — MEDIUM-HIGH, niche but doctrine-perfect.** A dynamic vision sensor outputs asynchronous per-pixel brightness-change events at ~1 µs temporal resolution with ~120–140 dB dynamic range ([emergentmind/DVS](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/dynamic-vision-sensors-dvs), [UZH RPG](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/research_dvs.html)). Against our 17 ms (~58 Hz-equivalent) frame pipeline, a DVS gives effectively continuous LOS-rate with near-zero motion blur — precisely the quantity our λ̇-nuller consumes. It is *physically matched* to a bearing-rate guidance law: λ̇ is literally the temporal derivative the DVS measures natively. It also degrades gracefully under our worst case (fast terminal closing rates that smear a frame camera). Limits: thermal DVS in LWIR is immature/expensive; an RGB/visible DVS only works by day and is range-limited; event streams need new processing (SNN or event-frame accumulation) within our compute budget; and it adds a second sensor's registration burden. Doctrinally clean — it improves the *rate measurement* feeding the existing tracker. **Verdict: a research bet; strongest if a thermal-band DVS matures, otherwise a daytime-only assist.**

**(5) Polarimetric LWIR — MEDIUM, a clutter-rejection discriminant.** Man-made surfaces (drone shells, motors) emit partially polarized LWIR with degree-of-linear-polarization signatures distinct from natural rough backgrounds; a micro-polarizer FPA adds a polarization axis usable purely as a *discriminant* (lock-quality), not for range. Cost: ~4× pixel binning (loss of the resolution we can't spare given ID at only 18–30 m), and uncertain DoLP contrast on small, distant, low-emissivity drones. **Verdict: speculative; low priority versus two-color.**

**(6) RGB/IR fusion — MEDIUM, daytime force-multiplier.** A co-bore-sighted visible camera adds texture/color for discrimination and, importantly, an *independent* detection channel (uncorrelated failure modes vs IR). It does nothing for range or night ops and adds registration + a second pipeline. Best framed as a discrimination/confirmation input to engage-permission. **Verdict: cheap insurance for daylight CUAS; doctrine-clean.**

### 11.3 Algorithm / compute-layer upgrades (ranked)

**(A) SOTA IR small-target deep nets within our invariants — HIGH, but strictly as a confidence channel.** The SIRST literature (DNANet — dense nested attention, [arXiv 2106.00487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00487); ISNet "shape matters"; AGPCNet; plus newer SCTransNet/MSHNet/L2SKNet/transformer-and-diffusion detectors, reviewed in [arXiv 2502.14168](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14168v1)) reports high Pd at low false-alarm rate on NUDT-SIRST/IRSTD-1k. These are exactly tuned to our problem: faint, few-pixel, low-SNR targets on structured clutter. **But the invariant is absolute: a learned net may NEVER write the centroid, the LOS, or the tracker state.** Its output is admissible *only* as a lock-quality / engage-permission scalar — "is the thing I'm tracking really a drone, and how sure am I." The classical blob/MTI/CFAR detector remains the source of truth for *where*; the net votes on *whether*. This preserves default-deny on doubt and keeps a wrong-hit-causing hallucination from steering the missile. Compute: a quantized small-backbone (INT8, sub-1 GFLOP, e.g., a pruned ACM/ALCNet-class head) is feasible in a fraction of the 17 ms on the A76 if run on a downsampled ROI around the existing track; full DNANet at frame rate is not — so run it *gated*, on the locked ROI, at reduced cadence. **Verdict: high-value confidence layer; the architecture's job is to make this *structurally incapable* of touching geometry.**

**(B) Set-membership / robust estimation — HIGH, low cost, doctrine-native.** Our IMM gives a probabilistic angular state; adding a *set-membership* (bounded-error) layer yields guaranteed innovation-consistency bounds — a rigorous "is this measurement physically admissible" test. This is the right mathematical home for the ToF-range gate (item 2): accept range only inside a feasible set, else fail closed. It also hardens us against AGC glitches and clutter jumps. Cheap (interval/zonotope arithmetic), no learning, fully explainable. **Verdict: do early; it is the safety substrate for nearly every other upgrade.**

**(C) Learned ego-motion — MEDIUM, replaces a degrading input.** We de-rotate with gyro; a learned (or hybrid) visual ego-motion estimator could improve de-rotation under gyro bias/saturation. Risk: ego-motion error couples *directly* into LOS-rate, the heart of guidance — so a learned estimator here is closer to the forbidden zone than a lock-quality net. Acceptable only as a *check/blend* against the gyro with set-membership veto, never as sole authority. **Verdict: hybrid only, heavily guarded.**

**(D) Neuromorphic looming / bio-inspired parallax navigation — MEDIUM-LOW, elegant but indirect.** Insect-inspired *looming* detectors estimate τ = −θ/θ̇ (time-to-contact) from angular size expansion alone — a passive, divisionless proxy for closing geometry that does *not* require range. This is attractive because it respects unobservability while still extracting an expansion cue, and it pairs naturally with a DVS. But for sub-pixel-to-few-pixel drones at our ID ranges, θ is tiny and θ̇ is buried in NETD noise until very late; the cue is real only in the final fraction of a second. **Verdict: research-grade; best as a terminal "imminence" flag into engage-permission, not a guidance input.**

**(E) Super-resolution — LOW-MEDIUM, and dangerous if misused.** SR could raise effective pixels-on-target to push the Johnson ID range (currently ~18–30 m) outward. **Hazard:** a generative SR net *hallucinates* structure; if that structure influences centroid or classification it manufactures false confidence — a direct wrong-hit risk. Permissible only as a non-generative (reconstruction-bounded) enhancement feeding a *human-auditable* discriminant, never the tracker. **Verdict: low priority, high care.**

### 11.4 Compute / SWaP realism

The Pi-5 single A76 cluster with a ~17 ms/frame measured budget is the binding constraint on every algorithm above. Realistic envelope: the classical detector + IMM + de-rotation already consumes much of the frame; we have perhaps a few milliseconds and a fraction of a GFLOP of headroom for a *gated, ROI-restricted, INT8* learned confidence head — not a full-frame transformer at frame rate. The highest-leverage compute move is therefore often a *better sensor that costs less compute*: a Y16 digital core removes the CVBS→USB grabber overhead and may *return* milliseconds, simultaneously improving the input and the budget. Any DVS/SNN path likely needs a small dedicated accelerator (Coral/Hailo-class) to be viable, which is itself a SWaP decision. The discipline: every upgrade must show its frame-budget cost *and* its fail-closed behavior before it earns integration.

### Implications for our seeker
- **Sequence the sensor roadmap as: Y16 radiometric core → set-membership safety layer → gated SIRST confidence net → terminal ToF ranger → (later) two-color.** Y16 is highest-leverage/lowest-risk and likely *frees* compute; it should not wait.
- **The ToF ranger is the only path that legitimately restores range observability** — and only in the ~1.0–1.4 s terminal wall, where it may promote guidance from λ̇-nulling to a real ZEM/APN law. Build the commit law and its fail-closed gate *now*, in simulation, before the hardware lands.
- **Every learned component is architecturally fenced to lock-quality / engage-permission.** The classical detector owns the centroid; nets vote on *whether*, never *where*. Make this a structural impossibility, not a code-review convention.
- **Two-color is the principled answer to our dominant failure mode (wrong-hit / clutter & flare).** Prototype the band-ratio discriminant on offline dual-band imagery before paying the optics/SWaP cost — its value is discrimination, not range.
- **Set-membership estimation is the safety substrate** that lets us accept ToF range, blend learned ego-motion, or trust a confidence net without ever failing open.

### Open questions / frontier
- Can a thermal-band (LWIR) DVS mature enough to give us native µs-latency λ̇ at night? Today's DVS strength is daytime/visible — a thermal event sensor would be transformative and is the biggest unknown.
- What is the minimum terminal-window ToF integration (beam divergence, update rate, dropout tolerance) that yields a *provably* fail-closed ZEM commit on a maneuvering drone, given strapdown pointing jitter?
- How far can a non-generative SR plus two-color push the Johnson ID range outward (18–30 m → ?) *without* introducing any hallucinated discriminant?
- What is the true on-target band-ratio separability between small COTS drones and our actual clutter/decoy set in LWIR-only vs MWIR/LWIR — i.e., is two-color worth a cooled channel for *this* target class?
- Can a gated INT8 SIRST head (DNANet/ISNet-class) hit useful Pd/Fa on real FT640 AGC imagery inside a few-millisecond ROI budget — and does domain shift from NUDT-SIRST/IRSTD-1k to our sensor destroy that performance?
- Is there a rigorous, certifiable boundary condition for *when* the endgame may switch laws (λ̇-nuller → APN) such that no spoof, dropout, or estimator fault can ever open the gate to a wrong hit?
