## 1. Decisive Lessons From the World's Systems

The 8–12 things mature seekers/trackers do to never lose a target, each with why it matters for *us specifically*.

1. **Run dual-mode trackers with mutual reset (centroid ⊕ area-correlation), never one.** [Ch.1, US4849906/US4958224] Centroid gives an unbiased aimpoint but provably *breaks down exactly at our endgame* (image overflows FOV, thermal signature changes aspect); correlation holds structure through clutter transitions but drifts. They continuously re-initialize each other. **For us:** we are a *pure detection+association+centroid* tracker — the literature's single most fragile family for our exact endgame.

2. **Derive the angle error from intensity-*normalized* geometry, monopulse-style — never from frame-to-frame brightness change.** [Ch.2] A scheme that infers position from brightness flicker is conescan, and an 8-bit AGC CVBS sensor is brightness-deceivable like a spin-scan reticle. **For us:** confirm AGC normalization precedes centroiding, or we have a conescan-class vulnerability hidden in the FT640 pipeline.

3. **Migrate the aimpoint from hotspot → silhouette-centroid as the target resolves.** [Ch.1, Ch.5, EP0386231] Early you can only track the brightest pixel (motor glow); but the hotspot is not a stable aimpoint, and it *walks across the airframe* as aspect rotates head-on→beam. Mature seekers segment the body and switch the aimpoint to a geometric point. **For us:** this is the missing stage directly on our critical path, and we already own the registration primitive (ORB+RANSAC MTI) to do it nearly free.

4. **Keep the aimpoint strictly geometric/structural — never a brightness extremum.** [Ch.1, Ch.5] The reticle-deception lesson generalized: the moment a brightness peak can vote on the aimpoint, sun glint / a hotter ground feature / a brighter second drone pulls you off. **For us:** this *is* our doctrine ("AI never writes centroid/LOS"); the world validates it as the central anti-pull-off principle.

5. **Make pull-off resistance kinematic + structural, living in association/gating — and have it write only to engage-permission.** [Ch.1, Ch.2-RGPO, Ch.4] Two-color band-ratio is the best fielded IRCCM and is *genuinely unavailable to us* (single uncooled band, no radiometry) — so motion-consistency becomes our only flare/glint/decoy test. A candidate demanding implausible LOS-rate to capture the gate is rejected while the filter coasts. **For us:** doctrine-legal and already half-built (combined-cost veto + NIS alarm).

6. **Coast on the filter prediction through dropouts; grow the gate by propagated covariance; reacquire the SAME track inside that ellipse.** [Ch.1, Ch.2-TWS, Ch.5] Brief loss → predict, don't search. Longer loss → expanding covariance-driven window, re-associate to the *same identity*, never spawn a fresh one. **For us:** our LOCKED→PREDICTIVE_TRACK→REACQUIRE→HARD_LOST FSM is exactly this lifecycle — but it coasts on a crude 2-point CV velocity, not the IMM's covariance.

7. **Rate-aid the *search window*, not just the filter state.** [Ch.1, Javelin/US-H455] Feed gyro rate into where you place the gate, so a hard target jink or own-transient *predictively slews the gate* instead of breaking lock during the moment the image is unusable. **For us:** we de-rotate but don't rate-aid the gate placement.

8. **Adaptive bandwidth: smooth λ̇ hard when innovations are quiet, open the loop the instant the maneuver alarm fires.** [Ch.6, Palumbo/APL] Filter glint hard and you lag a real jink; filter lightly and glint walks the aimpoint — the innovation (NIS) is the *only* honest discriminator between noise and maneuver. **For us:** we compute NIS but don't use it to schedule λ̇ smoothing.

9. **Switch GNN→JPDA-soft-update once the gate is cluttered (>~1.2 returns/gate).** [Ch.2] Hard winner-take-all is precisely when thermal pull-off happens at the horizon crossing; a likelihood-weighted update biased toward the predicted state is the principled fix. **For us:** the cold-sky→hot-ground transition routinely crosses this threshold.

10. **Delete tracks on score-below-its-own-peak, not an absolute floor.** [Ch.2-SPRT, Ch.3-LTMU] A continuous lock-score with hysteretic, peak-relative deletion resists single-frame dropouts (wing-flash, AGC blowout) while still releasing a genuinely-gone target. **For us:** our FSM drops on miss-count; peak-relative is strictly better and is the doctrine-sanctioned home for the learned/quality layer.

11. **Treat scale/aspect as a *tracked state*, not a nuisance — let gate, R, and aimpoint adapt to closure phase.** [Ch.3-DSST, Ch.4, Ch.5] The IR-seeker literature names dramatic scale change as the dominant terminal track-loss mode; the combat-proven Ukrainian modules answer it with a continuously re-fit "green-contour." **For us:** our angular IMM tracks `[az,el,az_rate,el_rate]` and estimates neither extent nor aspect.

12. **Terminal autonomy robust to information loss is proven on our exact hardware class.** [Ch.4: TFL-2/P1-SUN on RPi5+uncooled-thermal, ~80% hit] The premise is validated in combat. **But reject two of their choices:** their neural net *is* the centroid (we forbid this — WRONG-HIT is a HARD FAIL, their attritable economics tolerate 80%), and they coast on GPS/INS (we coast on IMM-CV in angle, correct because range is unobservable).

## 2. Key Info We Might Have Missed (highest-value, honest)

Ranked by leverage. Each is something our *current* stack does not exploit, verified against the code.

**M1 — No structural/correlation track channel (the largest gap).** `track.py` associates a CFAR blob centroid by combined-cost and coasts on CV. There is no template, no response map, no appearance model. The dual-mode literature [Ch.1] says this is the *most fragile family for our exact endgame*. The cheapest fix that fits 17 ms on a Pi is a **MOSSE/DSST-class FFT correlation filter** confined to the IMM gate, with a **separate 1-D scale filter** for the monotone scale growth.

**M2 — No aimpoint-migration stage; the aimpoint will physically jump.** `area_extended_px` is computed in `detect.py` (lines 483–488) **and then ignored** — it is diagnostic-only. The top-hat is a band-pass that *suppresses the interior of a resolved target*, so `area_px` collapses exactly when the UAV resolves, and the centroid is driven by the AGC-saturating hot pixel, which migrates with aspect. This is a silent λ̇ disturbance at the worst moment (glint error ∝ L/R *grows* as range closes, and is heavy-tailed/non-Gaussian). **We have the cue and the registration primitive (MTI homography); we are not acting on either.**

**M3 — No image-space confidence metric (PSR/APCE).** We gate in *state* space (NIS, covariance gate) but have no *appearance* confidence on the lock — the cheapest, most-proven CV health signal, and one doctrine explicitly *permits* on the lock-quality channel. [Ch.3]

**M4 — The IMM's hardening outputs are computed but not consumed by the tracker.** `imm.py` already emits `gate_sigma_az/el_rad` (covariance-sized box), `model_wrong_alarm` (sustained true-NIS), and `lock_quality`. But `track.py`'s coast uses a **2-point pixel velocity**, not the IMM state, and `set_search_radius()` exists yet the FSM coast/reacquire prediction is not IMM-driven. **We built the estimator hardening and left the tracker on the old CV predictor.** Wiring these together is nearly free and high-value.

**M5 — λ̇ smoothing is not NIS-scheduled; no robust (Huber) innovation.** [Ch.6] The χ²/Mahalanobis *gate* rejects whole measurements, but in-gate-but-tail glint excursions are not *down-weighted*. The standard endgame glint defense (robust loss + NIS-scheduled bandwidth) is absent. R is also fixed, not inflated as pixel-extent grows.

**M6 — `looming.py`/τ is used only as a weak guidance blend-factor, never as the terminal clock or the scale-predictor.** [Ch.5, Ch.8] τ = A/(dA/dt) is wired into `bearing_rate.py` to blend pursuit↔bearing-rate-null, but `command_map.py` still triggers the terminal/acro phase off `estimated_range_m`/fixed-`tau_threshold` timers. We leave a fully passive, range-free time-to-contact observable underused: τ should *schedule the ten-tau commit* and *drive the appearance scale-warp* (it self-scales: faster closure shrinks τ, advancing the commit automatically; τ̇≈0.5 is the contact-with-zero-velocity reference).

**M7 — We operate purely in the intensity domain and *fight* AGC/background with filters, when a log-contrast motion channel makes those nuisances disappear.** [Ch.7, Ch.8] The four hardest nuisances (AGC gain, background march, signature change, scale growth) are precisely what a log-contrast / "synthetic-event" channel is blind to. We can emulate this on the FT640 *today* (per-pixel log-intensity temporal difference on de-rotated frames) and feed λ̇ preferentially from it — no new hardware.

**M8 — No dual-channel intra-sensor corroboration before LOCKED.** [Ch.4] Fielded fusion requires ≥2 independent sensors to agree. We can approximate this *inside one camera* by requiring our **intensity-blob track and our MTI motion track to agree** before declaring LOCKED — a feature-level analog we don't yet enforce, and `mti.py` already produces the motion mask.

**M9 — No deferred-decision / 2-best track memory through a clutter crossing.** [Ch.6] A *coordinated maneuver hidden inside a clutter crossing* is the single most dangerous joint event and CV-coast cannot survive it. A cheap 2-hypothesis carry through the clutter band, resolved by N-of-M continuity (which `track_manager.py` already implements), approximates MHT.

**M10 — Coast budget is frame-count, not geometry/τ-driven.** [Ch.5] At high closure the time-behind-occluder and time-to-FOV-edge scale with the engagement, not a fixed `predictive_track_frames=18`. Budgets should be set from predicted out-of-view time (τ).

**Genuine hard limits to acknowledge, not work around:** two-color IRCCM is unavailable (single band); a strapdown seeker has **no gimbal field-of-regard margin** — beyond a critical jink-vs-closure ratio the engagement is geometrically un-winnable and the correct output is ABORT (a PASS); an LWIR-band event *sensor* is not buyable, so the event idea transfers as an *algorithm*, not hardware.

### 3.2 Lock-persistence state machine (explicit)

The FSM gains a **second, orthogonal axis**: the existing *lock lifecycle* (presence) **×** a new *regime* (target angular scale). Both carry one persistent track ID.

**Lifecycle axis** (CHANGE the deletion/coast logic, KEEP the states):
```
NO_TARGET → CANDIDATE → LOCKED → PREDICTIVE_TRACK → REACQUIRE → HARD_LOST
```
- **CANDIDATE→LOCKED:** require N-of-M stable association **AND intensity∧motion consensus** (M8). 
- **LOCKED:** continuous **lock-score** = f(PSR/APCE [M3], IMM lock_quality, continuity). Update template **only** here, NIS-in-gate, single in-gate candidate (confidence-gated, distractor-aware, FEAR dual-template freezes the immutable ref).
- **LOCKED→PREDICTIVE_TRACK:** miss within gate → coast on **IMM mixed-state prediction** (CHANGE from 2-point CV), gate grows by `gate_sigma` (M4), **freeze template**.
- **coast budget:** **τ-scaled, not fixed frame count** (M10).
- **PREDICTIVE_TRACK→REACQUIRE:** search the **propagated covariance ellipse** for the SAME ID; firewalled learned re-detector only *scores* deterministic candidates for lock-quality (never proposes a centroid).
- **Deletion → HARD_LOST:** on **score-below-its-own-peak** (M, Ch.2-SPRT), not absolute floor.
- **Default-deny:** low lock-score / model_wrong_alarm / FOR-exhaustion → **ABORT (PASS)**, never a confident wrong re-lock (WRONG-HIT = HARD FAIL).

**Regime axis** (ADD — keyed on `area_extended_px`/top-hat-area-collapse, hysteretic to prevent chatter):
- **POINT:** matched-filter/CFAR detection; aimpoint = hotspot = centroid; energy-gated. *(our current strength)*
- **RESOLVED:** segmentation + correlation/DSST template; **aimpoint migrates to silhouette-centroid** (MTI-registered frame difference, MAX−MIN); R inflates with extent; scene-class (sky/horizon/ground from the CFAR region map) sets template learning-rate (sky=plastic, horizon/ground=stable).
- **FILL/OVERFILL:** whole-body centroid abandoned; track a chosen **sub-feature/leading edge** along the closure vector; freeze plasticity; **freeze/heavily-damp the aimpoint inside the ~1.0–1.4 s ten-τ wall** (late innovations can only add miss).

**Doctrine invariants honored throughout:** range never scales gain (a_cmd=N·Vc_sched·λ̇, KEEP); learned/template/scene/PSR/looming write only to lock-quality/engage-permission; ABORT is a PASS; default-deny; the IMM alone produces the LOS that guidance consumes.

## 4. The Novel / Ideal Edge (nobody-does-this-cheaply)

Three ideas, with physics and risk, ranked by leverage-per-cost.

### Edge 1 — Software "synthetic-event" λ̇ channel (the FENCE *algorithm*, not the hardware)
**Physics:** A DVS responds to ∂(log I)/∂t. The log makes it invariant to the *multiplicative* AGC gain; the temporal-difference makes static background (cold sky, hot ground) *vanish from the data* until it moves. Our λ̇ today is differentiated from a 17 ms-cadence centroid, so its noise floor is (centroid jitter)/Δt — it blows up exactly on the terminal wall where λ̇ matters most and the bolometer's ~10–15 ms thermal τ smears the frame. A per-pixel log-intensity temporal difference on **de-rotated** frames (we already de-rotate in `los.py`) yields a motion field whose first moment gives a cleaner, phase-lead-free λ̇.
**Why nobody does it cheaply:** everyone reaches for an actual event camera (visible-band, blind at night, bus-flood under ego-motion). We get ~80% of the invariance benefit in software on hardware we own.
**Risk:** low — pure software, testable on bench. The thermal time-constant caps the speed benefit (the bolometer can't change temperature in µs), so this sharpens λ̇ and kills AGC/background sensitivity but is not a true µs sensor. Honest framing: an *invariance* win, not a *latency* win.

### Edge 2 — Anticipatory template pre-warp driven by τ (forward-model the *image*, not just the state)
**Physics:** Our IMM forward-models the *angular state* but never the *appearance*, so every frame the tracker is *surprised* by the new scale/aspect/signature — the proximate cause of terminal pull-off. But scale is predictable without range: θ̇/θ = −Ṙ/R = −1/τ, so the template scales by **exp(Δt/τ)** using the looming τ we already compute — *no range needed*. Aspect-rotation rate comes from the IMM cross-LOS velocity (affine warp); the aspect-dependent motor hotspot is handled with a tiny aspect-indexed thermal-template dictionary. The matched filter at t+1 then runs against a *predicted* image, so residuals stay small and the gate stays tight exactly when raw appearance changes fastest.
**Why nobody does it cheaply:** this is the appearance-domain analog of the IMM, and the elegant coupling — **looming both times the terminal phase AND predicts the scale warp** — is, to our knowledge, not done in cheap seekers. A physics-parameterized affine+scale warp needs **no training data** and is fully interpretable.
**Risk:** medium. τ confidence collapses at acquisition and saturation (handled by `looming.py` gating); a wrong τ mis-warps the template, so the warp must be confidence-gated and fall back to the immutable FEAR template when τ_confidence < threshold.

### Edge 3 — Looming-as-imminence: τ̇ as the terminal clock and engage-permission arm
**Physics:** Lee's tau theory: holding τ̇ = 0.5 brings you to contact with zero relative velocity; τ̇ < 0.5 guarantees arrival. τ self-scales with closure. **For us:** replace the fixed 1.0–1.4 s timer and `estimated_range_m` triggers in `command_map.py` with a τ-derived commit — physically grounded, range-free, and automatically advancing as closure speeds up. Falcons (PNAS) validate our whole guidance family: they run PN at **low N≈2.6** (matched to a low-g, rate-limited airframe — exactly our ≤0.84 g regime) with a feed-forward lock-on phase before terminal PN (mirroring our planned A1 ACQUIRE state). Dragonfly motion-camouflage (constant-bearing = λ̇-null) gives our law a free counter-detection benefit and motivates *predictive* (not just subtractive) ego de-rotation.
**Why nobody does it cheaply:** passive, range-free terminal timing from monocular looming is on the table and we (and most) leave it unused.
**Risk:** low–medium. τ is unreliable at the saturation regime (blob fills ROI) — but that is *past* the commit point, so the commit fires while τ is still observable; below it we freeze. Must remain a *timing/permission* signal, never a guidance gain.

## 1. World Survey — Imaging-Infrared Seekers & Their Track-Maintenance

The engineering problem in TASK #1 — *acquire a winged UAV and never let it leave the seeker's view from lock-on through closest-point-of-approach* — is exactly the problem that fifty years of imaging-IR (IIR) terminal-homing seeker development has been solving, under names like "track-maintenance," "track-point stability," and "aimpoint migration." This section surveys the publicly documented art at the principles level, then distills what a passive monocular uncooled-LWIR strapdown seeker on a Pi can and cannot borrow.

### 1.1 The reticle-to-imaging transition: why we are an imaging seeker, and what reticles still teach us

The first generation of IR homing — the AIM-9B Sidewinder and the early FIM-92 Stinger — used a **spin-scan reticle**: a single cooled detector behind a spinning, patterned chopper disk. Target angular position was encoded in the *phase* and *amplitude* of the modulated detector output; the missile nulled boresight error by driving phase to a reference. Spin-scan has a fatal flaw for track-maintenance: a target near boresight produces little modulation (the "spin-scan null"), and a continuous off-axis IR source (the sun, a flare, hot clutter) produces *large* steady modulation that the seeker happily chases. The **con-scan** reticle (Stinger Basic) moved the energy in a nutating circle so a centered target still modulated, improving null behavior. The **rosette-scan** seeker (Stinger-POST/RMP, per the designation-systems and NDU histories) replaced the spinning reticle with a small instantaneous-FOV detector scanned in a Lissajous "rosette" pattern that *samples the scene as a crude image*, letting the processor separate a compact target from a spatially-distinct flare by where in the rosette each appears — the first step toward spatial, image-based discrimination. The endpoint of this evolution is the **staring focal-plane array (FPA)** imaging seeker (AIM-9X, IRIS-T, ASRAAM, Python-5, Stinger Block II per the same sources), which delivers a true 2-D image to a processor that "determines target from background clutter and tracks the target" (NAVAIR/GlobalSecurity on AIM-9X).

**Transferable lesson.** The reticle lineage is the canonical lesson in *modulation-based deception*: any seeker that homes on a scalar feature (brightness, phase) can be pulled off by anything that maximizes that scalar. We are already an imaging seeker, so we inherit the imaging advantage — but the lesson is that **our discriminant must be spatial/structural, not photometric.** An 8-bit AGC CVBS sensor with no radiometry is *brightness-deceivable* in exactly the spin-scan sense; the sun glint off a wing, a hot ground feature crossing behind the target, or a brighter aspect of a second drone will pull a pure-brightness centroid the way a flare pulls a spin-scan reticle. The con-scan/rosette insight — *use spatial separation to reject a competing source that is bright but in the wrong place* — is precisely the job of our combined-cost anti-pull-off association and our covariance-sized gate.

### 1.2 The three classical imaging-tracker families, and why real seekers run them in combination

The open literature (the IDA EO/IR tutorial; the SPIE "Centroid tracker and aimpoint selection," Venkateswarlu & Sujata 1992; and a long line of US patents on dual-mode video trackers, e.g. US4849906, US4958224) converges on three primitive tracker types. Each is a different answer to "where is the target this frame," and each fails in a complementary regime — which is why fielded seekers *fuse* them.

**(a) Centroid / intensity trackers.** Within a track gate, threshold/segment candidate pixels and compute the first moment (intensity-weighted centroid):
  x_c = Σ I(x,y)·x / Σ I(x,y),  y_c = Σ I(x,y)·y / Σ I(x,y).
Cheap, sub-pixel, and the *de facto* aimpoint for a resolved target. The documented failure modes are exactly TASK #1's hazards: the centroid wanders as the target's *thermal* signature changes with aspect (motor/exhaust appearing and disappearing), and the SPIE/patent literature is explicit that **"as the range decreases the target image grows and finally overflows the FOV, and the centroid keeps changing"** — i.e. centroid breaks down at the very endgame where we need it most. Thresholding also couples the centroid to the AGC and the background, so a sky→horizon→ground transition (our stated background march) physically *moves* the reported centroid even if the target is rigid in the image.

**(b) Area-correlation trackers.** Store a reference *template* (a chip of the target image) and find, each frame, the shift (Δx,Δy) that maximizes a similarity metric over a search region — normalized cross-correlation (NCC) or, in hardware-cheap implementations, minimized sum-of-absolute-differences / MAD:
  (Δx,Δy) = argmin Σ |I(x+Δx, y+Δy) − T(x,y)|.
The decisive property, stated directly in the correlation-tracker literature: correlation **"can track internal target detail and maintain a stable terminal tracking point when the target image size exceeds the tracker field of view"**, and it outperforms centroid trackers "in low-SNR, high-clutter environments." Correlation does not need to segment the target from the background — it locks to *structure* — which is why it survives the sky-to-ground clutter transition that destroys a thresholded centroid. Its failure mode is the dual of centroid's: it has no intrinsic scale/rotation invariance, so as scale grows and aspect rotates the template *decorrelates*, and naïve template updating causes **drift / "walk-off"** as accumulated registration error and background bleed into the reference.

**(c) Edge / feature trackers.** Track a geometric feature — the leading edge along the velocity vector (US5872857 "biased centroid edge locator"), or, in modern guise, sparse keypoints. Robust to overall intensity changes, useful for picking a *specific* aimpoint (nose, leading edge) rather than the energy centroid, but fragile when the feature itself changes aspect.

The headline of the survey is that **production seekers do not pick one; they run a dual-mode tracker** (US4849906; US4958224 "forced correlation/mixed mode"). The documented architecture: centroid and correlation run together, *each continuously re-initializing the other* — "centroid tracking with the correlator constantly updated with a new reference and track centroid, or correlation tracking constantly updating the centroid to the target location." Correlation provides clutter-robustness and continuity; centroid provides the unbiased aimpoint and bounds correlation's drift. This mutual-reset architecture *is* the "don't lose the target" engineering.

**Transferable lesson.** Our stack is, today, essentially a **detection-plus-association centroid tracker** (top-hat / percentile-MAD / region-CFAR / MPCM / directional-median feeding a combined-cost associator and a CV-coast FSM). That is the family the literature says is *most fragile to exactly our endgame* (scale growth, aspect-dependent signature, background transition). The single highest-value transfer is to **add a lightweight area-correlation/template channel as a parallel observer and run the documented mutual-reset between it and the centroid** — correlation to hold through the clutter transition and the moment the UAV stops being a point, centroid to keep the aimpoint honest and bound correlation drift. On a Pi this is affordable: a MAD/NCC correlation over a small gate is a few thousand integer ops, well inside a 17 ms budget, especially if restricted to the IMM-predicted gate.

### 1.3 Template maintenance through scale and aspect — the FLIR-seeker discipline

Because a missile flies *down the boresight into* its target, the FLIR/missile-seeker community has a dedicated body of work on tracking under monotone, rapid scale growth (the ResearchGate "FLIR Target Tracking Based on Distance Updating"; SPIE 3086 "region template correlation"). The documented recipe: acquire a **region template** (not a point), then **update the template as the target magnifies**, with the explicit finding that region-template updating "produces significant reductions in drift rate compared to a conventional point correlation tracker." The danger is template corruption: the modern correlation-filter literature (scale-adaptive KCF, Monte-Carlo scale estimation; "Scene-Aware Adaptive Updating") is unanimous that **unconditional per-frame template updates cause drift**, and that the cure is a *gated* update — only fold the new appearance into the reference when the match confidence is high (peak-to-sidelobe ratio, cumulative-max-response criteria), and freeze the template when confidence drops (occlusion, a maneuver, a clutter crossing).

**Transferable lesson.** If we add a template channel, the update *policy* matters more than the matcher. Borrow the **confidence-gated, hysteretic template update**: update scale/appearance only on high-quality matches, freeze (and coast on the IMM) when match quality drops, and bound how far the template centroid may move per frame from the IMM prediction — this is the correlation analogue of our existing anti-pull-off cost and directly serves the "never lose lock" mandate. What does *not* transfer is heavyweight scale search (sequential-Monte-Carlo / multi-scale KCF pyramids) — too costly for the Pi and unnecessary because our **range/closing-speed are unobservable anyway**; we can let the gate size grow with the *measured* image-blob growth rather than estimating physical scale.

### 1.4 Aimpoint migration: hotspot → resolved silhouette → stable terminal point

The most TASK-#1-specific body of public art is **aimpoint migration as the target resolves**. The segmentation-for-terminal-aimpoint patents (EP0386231A1 / US4959714) state the problem precisely: early in flight the target is a point and the only available track point is the **hotspot** (the brightest pixel — for us, the LWIR motor/exhaust glow); but "the hotspot position does not necessarily coincide with a desirable aimpoint," which lives on the body silhouette. As the target resolves, the seeker must *migrate* the aimpoint from hotspot to a silhouette-derived point. Their disclosed mechanism is elegant and cheap: because the tracker keeps the target centered, **the target is nearly stationary frame-to-frame while the background moves**, so multi-frame differencing (MAX−MIN over ≥3 registered frames, threshold proportional to scene noise) segments the body silhouette without needing an absolute intensity threshold; the aimpoint then becomes the **silhouette centroid / first moment**. The newer aimpoint-selection literature (convex-hull and airplane-target methods) adds that for a *winged* target the silhouette centroid may still be suboptimal and a structure-aware point (wing-body junction, fuselage) is preferred — and warns that "centroid guidance is not optimal" once the target is resolved.

**Transferable lesson.** This is the missing stage in our pipeline and it is *directly* on the TASK-#1 critical path. Early: track the LWIR **hotspot** (we already detect it). Late: as the winged UAV grows past a few pixels, **migrate the aimpoint to a silhouette-derived point** using ego-motion-compensated frame differencing — and here is the gift: **we already have the exact registration primitive needed** (ORB+RANSAC homography MTI for ego-motion). Differencing the homography-registered frames inside the track gate yields the moving-target silhouette essentially for free, and its first moment gives a stable aimpoint that *does not jump* when the motor glow waxes/wanes with aspect. The transferable invariant is also a doctrine match: **the aimpoint is a geometric/structural quantity, never a brightness extremum**, mirroring our "learned/AI never writes centroid/LOS." What does *not* transfer is any aimpoint-selection scheme that needs target recognition/ATR to label "nose vs. wing root" — too much for a Pi and a default-deny doctrine; a silhouette-centroid with a small forward bias toward the closing velocity vector (the edge-tracker idea) is the cheap, robust substitute.

### 1.5 Counter-countermeasures and pull-off resistance — structural, not photometric

Even absent deliberate jamming, TASK #1 faces *natural* pull-off: sun glint, a second UAV, hot ground features, and aspect-dependent signature collapse. The IRCCM literature against deliberate flares is directly instructive because the discriminants are the same ones that beat *natural* distractors. AIM-9X's processor "isolates countermeasures from the target" using the same image it tracks on (NAVAIR). The named discriminants: **two-color / dual-band signal-ratio** (Python-5, IRIS-T two-color; the crossed-array two-color ICCM paper) — *not available to us*, we are single-band uncooled; **kinematic discrimination** — Intensity-Ratio-Change and **LOS-Rate-Change**, exploiting that a flare *decelerates and separates*, producing a LOS-rate transient and a trajectory inconsistent with target dynamics (IRIS-T's documented "centroid motion + angular acceleration" decoy rejection, <1% false-lock); and **spatial/structural** — the target is an extended, structured, persistent object while a decoy is a compact, transient, separating blob.

**Transferable lesson.** The two-color channel is the single most effective fielded IRCCM and **does not transfer** — we have one uncooled band and no radiometry; we must not pretend otherwise. Everything that transfers is **kinematic and structural**, and we already have the machinery: the **LOS-Rate-Change** test is implementable on our IMM's innovation/NIS stream (a separating distractor produces a NIS spike and a model-mismatch alarm — we already compute both); the **N-of-M trajectory-continuity** backstop is exactly the "persistent vs. transient" discriminant; and the combined-cost associator is the spatial-separation test. The gap is using them *as an explicit decoy/distractor discriminator that writes to lock-quality* (engage-permission), which our doctrine *permits* (learned/quality may write engage-permission) and which IRIS-T/AIM-9X show is the right home for it — never letting the discriminator yank the centroid, only gate the engage decision.

### 1.6 LOBL handover, strapdown LOS reconstruction, and the man-in-the-loop fallback

Two more system-level lessons. First, **Javelin** is the canonical LOBL IIR correlation tracker (64×64 LWIR MCT staring array, fire-and-forget): the literature is blunt that **"the tracker and its ability to maintain lock throughout flight emerged as the most critical aspect of the system"** — i.e., the entire program's risk concentrated in track-maintenance, exactly our TASK #1 framing — and that launch-transient survival required **rate-aided tracking** (US-H455), where the IMU/gyro rate feeds the tracker so a violent transient (our launch, or a hard target jink) doesn't break lock during the moment the image is unusable. Second, **strapdown** seekers (our configuration) cannot read LOS-rate off a gimbal; the open art (JHU APL "Principles of Homing Guidance"; the strapdown-seeker UKF papers) reconstructs it by **combining the image boresight-error measurement with integrated gyro rates and a body-to-inertial DCM** — a "virtual gimbal" / de-rotation, precisely our gyro de-rotation into [az,el,az_rate,el_rate]. Third, **Spike/Spike-NLOS** keep a man-in-the-loop EO datalink so a human can re-designate the aimpoint mid-flight — a fallback we *cannot* assume (autonomous, LOBL), which raises the bar on autonomous re-acquisition.

**Transferable lesson.** Javelin's **rate-aided tracking** is a direct, high-value borrow: feed gyro rate into the tracker/IMM gate so a hard UAV maneuver or our own transient *predictively slews the gate* instead of breaking lock — our IMM + CV-coast does the prediction, but explicitly *rate-aiding the search window* (not just the state) is the documented "don't lose it through the transient" trick. The strapdown LOS-reconstruction lesson confirms our architecture is correct (boresight-error + gyro de-rotation = virtual gimbal). Spike's lesson is *negative*: with no human fallback, our **autonomous REACQUIRE state must be as good as a human re-designation** — meaning the IMM coast prediction must hand the re-acquirer a tight, correctly-sized gate, and the FSM must prefer ABORT (a PASS) over a confident wrong re-lock (WRONG-HIT = HARD FAIL).

### Transferable to our seeker
- **Add a parallel area-correlation (MAD/NCC) channel and run the documented dual-mode mutual-reset against our centroid:** correlation holds structure through the sky→horizon→ground clutter transition and the point→resolved transition where thresholded centroids provably fail; centroid keeps the aimpoint unbiased and bounds correlation drift. Affordable in the 17 ms budget if confined to the IMM-predicted gate.
- **Implement hotspot→silhouette aimpoint migration using our existing ORB+RANSAC registration:** difference homography-registered frames inside the gate to segment the moving silhouette (EP0386231 MAX−MIN trick), and switch the aimpoint from the motor-glow hotspot to the silhouette first moment as the UAV grows — eliminating aimpoint jumps from aspect-dependent thermal signature.
- **Adopt confidence-gated, hysteretic template/scale update:** only update on high-quality matches (peak-to-sidelobe / cumulative-max criterion), freeze and IMM-coast on low confidence; let gate scale track *measured* blob growth, not an (unobservable) physical-range estimate.
- **Add rate-aided tracking (Javelin/US-H455):** feed gyro rate into the gate/search-window placement, not just the filter state, so a hard target jink or own-transient predictively slews the gate instead of breaking lock.
- **Promote kinematic+structural discrimination into an explicit distractor/decoy test that writes ONLY to lock-quality/engage-permission:** reuse the IMM NIS/innovation spike (LOS-Rate-Change analogue) and N-of-M continuity (persistent-vs-transient) to gate engagement — doctrine-legal, mirrors IRIS-T/AIM-9X IRCCM placement, never touches centroid/LOS.
- **Keep the aimpoint strictly geometric/structural, never a brightness extremum** — the reticle-deception lesson generalized: an 8-bit AGC CVBS sensor is brightness-deceivable exactly like a spin-scan reticle.

### What we may have missed
- **No structural/correlation track channel.** Our pipeline is detection+association+centroid — the literature's *most fragile* family for our exact endgame (scale growth, aspect change, background march). This is the largest gap.
- **No explicit aimpoint-migration stage.** We track a detection (effectively a hotspot) all the way in; we have no documented hotspot→silhouette-centroid transition, so the aimpoint will physically jump as the winged UAV resolves and its motor signature changes aspect — directly threatening a stable terminal aimpoint.
- **Template/scale-update drift policy is unspecified.** If we add correlation we must adopt the gated/hysteretic-update discipline up front; naïve per-frame updates are the documented #1 cause of walk-off.
- **Rate-aiding the gate (not just the state) is not called out.** Our IMM predicts state, but explicitly slewing the *search window* with gyro rate through transients is the documented Javelin launch-/maneuver-survival trick we don't yet name.
- **Two-color IRCCM is genuinely unavailable and should be acknowledged as a hard limit**, not worked around — our only robust countermeasure/distractor discriminants are kinematic and structural, so they must be made first-class rather than incidental.
- **Edge/forward-biased aimpoint for a winged target.** The convex-hull/airplane-aimpoint literature warns the silhouette *centroid* is suboptimal for a winged body; a small forward (velocity-vector) bias toward the fuselage/nose may give a better terminal aimpoint than pure first-moment — cheap to add once silhouette segmentation exists.

## 2. World Survey — Radar/Monopulse Angle Tracking & TWS (borrowable estimation)

A radar seeker and a passive LWIR strapdown seeker share almost nothing at the *sensing* layer — one is active, coherent, range-and-Doppler-resolved; the other is passive, incoherent, angle-only. But above the measurement layer the two converge: both reduce to the problem of holding a stable angular track on a maneuvering target while rejecting spurious returns, surviving signal dropouts, and not being "pulled off" by a competing signal. Radar fire-control engineering spent seventy years industrializing exactly this — gating, association, the track lifecycle, maneuver-adaptive filtering, and counter-deception track logic — and the resulting patterns are mature, quantified, and directly portable to a monocular imager. This chapter extracts the estimation and track-management machinery and maps each pattern onto our FT640/Pi stack.

### 2.1 What the angle measurement is — and why it is the borrowable part

A monopulse tracker forms two beams (or sum-Σ and difference-Δ patterns) simultaneously and computes the **monopulse ratio** k·Re(Δ/Σ), which is monotone in the off-boresight angle near boresight ([MDPI, *Performance Analysis of Amplitude-Comparison Monopulse DOA*](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/10/4/1246); [IEEE, *Angle Estimation in Amplitude Comparison Monopulse Systems*](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4103299/)). The decisive property is "monopulse" itself: the angle error is recovered from a *single pulse*, with no scan required, and Σ-normalization makes the error estimate **independent of target amplitude and range** ([Radar Systems – Tracking Radar, tutorialspoint](https://www.tutorialspoint.com/radar_systems/radar_systems_tracking_radar.htm)). That is exactly the structure our pipeline already has: the seeker's per-frame az/el centroid error is a monopulse-equivalent error signal, and dividing image moments by total target intensity is the optical analogue of Σ-normalization.

The contrast with the **older sequential methods is the load-bearing lesson.** Conical scan and sequential lobing recover angle from the *amplitude modulation of the return at the scan/lobe rate* — they need several looks over a scan cycle to form one error vector ([technobyte, *Angle tracking — conical scan, sequential lobing*](https://technobyte.org/angle-tracking-conical-scan-sequential-lobing-low-angle-tracking/)). This makes them catastrophically vulnerable to any amplitude fluctuation that is *not* the target's angular offset: target scintillation, AGC transients, or a repeater jammer injecting amplitude modulation at the lobe frequency ("inverse-gain" / angle-deception jamming) all corrupt the angle estimate ([US4183023, *Anti-jam device for a conical scan tracking radar*](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4183023); [tutorialspoint]). Monopulse defeats this precisely because the error is formed instantaneously from one look, so amplitude fluctuation between looks cannot masquerade as angle.

**Transferable lesson.** Our uncooled FT640 with 8-bit CVBS AGC is an amplitude-fluctuating sensor par excellence: AGC re-stretches contrast frame-to-frame, and the target's thermal signature is strongly aspect-dependent (motor/exhaust visibility changes head-on → beam). Any tracking scheme that infers position from *brightness changes across frames* is the optical equivalent of conescan and will be pulled around by AGC and signature flicker. The right architecture is the monopulse one: extract a *per-frame, intensity-normalized* geometric error (centroid / moment-based offset) and feed that single-look angle to the filter — never let frame-to-frame intensity changes leak into the position estimate. Our combined-cost association already moves in this direction; the explicit doctrine should be "position comes from this-frame geometry, normalized; intensity informs only confidence." That mirrors the radar discipline of putting AGC *ahead* of the angle discriminator so the discriminator sees a normalized signal ([ResearchGate, *Synthesis of automatic gain controllers for conical scan tracking radar*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3003145)).

### 2.2 The track filter: alpha-beta, Kalman, Singer, IMM

Radar trackers evolved their state estimator under exactly our pressure — a target that flies smoothly *most* of the time but maneuvers occasionally — and the lineage is instructive.

- **Alpha-beta (and α-β-γ)** are fixed-gain steady-state filters: position and velocity are corrected by constant gains α, β chosen from a single "tracking index." They are cheap and adequate for *straight-line* targets but degrade sharply against maneuver, where a Kalman filter with a proper motion model wins decisively ([Air Traffic Control Quarterly, *Tracking Algorithms for ATC*](https://doi.org/10.2514/atcq.3.1.19)).
- A **Kalman filter** with a constant-velocity model tuned so its process-noise covariance covers the 65–95% acceleration envelope works — until the target maneuvers, at which point the deterministic acceleration violates the white-noise assumption and the filter develops a **lag bias** in its state estimate ([PMC, *Improved IMM Based on Cubature KF*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4934231/)). This is the central failure mode of any single-model tracker and is exactly the bias that loses lock during a jink.
- The **Singer model** was the first to treat target acceleration as a *time-correlated (colored) random process* — a(k+1) decays toward zero with maneuver time-constant τ, α = 1/τ — instead of white noise ([MATLAB, *singer* model](https://www.mathworks.com/help/fusion/ref/singer.singer.html); [SPIE 1998, *Comparison of several maneuvering target tracking models*](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1998SPIE.3374...48M/abstract)). Its closed-form steady-state result is a clean scaling law: post-update RMS error depends on only **two dimensionless groups** — a maneuver index (target acceleration vs. measurement noise over the sample interval) and the product ατ. This is the radar analogue of our own "ten-tau" reasoning, and it says our filter tuning collapses to two knobs.
- **IMM (Interacting Multiple Model)** runs a bank of motion models in parallel (typically constant-velocity + one or more maneuver models), mixes their states each cycle through a Markov transition matrix, and weights them by their measurement likelihoods, switching responsibility automatically as the target's behavior changes ([PMC, *IMM Fifth-Degree Cubature KF*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5492340/)). It provably beats any single model in mixed maneuvering/non-maneuvering scenarios — which is precisely the winged-UAV regime: long smooth cruise legs punctuated by hard turns.

**Transferable lesson.** Our 4-state modified-polar IMM is the correct, mature choice and is already the radar best-practice. Two specific borrows sharpen it. **(1) Adopt the Singer colored-acceleration model as one of the IMM's maneuver members** (or as the maneuver-model process structure), parameterized by a τ matched to the winged UAV's turn dynamics — a fixed-wing's bank-to-turn acceleration is genuinely *autocorrelated*, not white, so Singer fits its physics better than a white-jerk model and gives a principled τ rather than a hand-tuned Q. **(2) Use the two-dimensionless-group scaling** to set process noise: tune to maneuver index and ατ, not to raw Q, so the gate and bandwidth track the engagement geometry. What does *not* transfer: radar filters typically run in Cartesian or range-augmented states; we are angle-only with unobservable range, so we keep modified-polar — but the IMM mixing, the Markov switching, and the Singer structure are all sensor-agnostic and port unchanged.

### 2.3 Gating and association — the anti-pull-off core

Once a track exists, every new frame produces candidate detections that must be matched to it. Radar formalizes this in two steps that our pipeline should mirror exactly:

- **Gating** is a screening test: only detections falling inside a validation region around the predicted measurement are eligible. The gate is sized by the *innovation covariance* S, i.e. a Mahalanobis / χ² ellipsoid, so it automatically widens when the filter is uncertain (after a coast) and tightens when confident ([MATLAB, *GNN multi-object tracker*](https://www.mathworks.com/help/fusion/ref/globalnearestneighbormultiobjecttracker.html); [MATLAB sensor-fusion tutorial](https://www.mathworks.com/videos/sensor-fusion-part-5-how-to-track-multiple-objects-at-once-1569411395828.html)). Gating both speeds assignment and, more importantly, *rejects clutter geometrically before it can corrupt the track*.
- **Association** then assigns within the gate. The empirically established rule: **nearest-neighbor (NN/GNN) is adequate only when clutter density is low** — above roughly **1.2 returns per gate on average, NN misassociation becomes prohibitive** and you must move to a probabilistic assignment (**JPDA**), which updates the track with a *probability-weighted combination of all gated returns* rather than committing to one ([atlantis-press, *Data Association Algorithm Overview*](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/16947.pdf); [MATLAB, *trackerJPDA*](https://www.mathworks.com/help/fusion/ref/trackerjpda-system-object.html)).

**Transferable lesson.** Our covariance-sized gate is the radar pattern done right and our combined-cost association is a GNN-class assignment. The unexploited borrow is **JPDA-style soft association for the cluttered endgame.** As the interceptor crosses the horizon into hot ground clutter, the number of competing warm blobs inside the gate will routinely exceed the 1.2-per-gate NN breakdown threshold. A hard winner-take-all centroid choice is exactly when pull-off happens. A JPDA-weighted update — blend gated candidates by likelihood, with the *predicted* target state dominating the weight — is the principled anti-pull-off mechanism and naturally biases the update toward kinematic continuity, the same bias that makes radar correlation trackers lock onto the right scatterer. This stays doctrine-legal: JPDA touches only the association/update math, never the guidance gain.

### 2.4 The track lifecycle — initiation, confirmation, deletion, coast

Radar trackers never treat "is there a track?" as binary. They run an explicit lifecycle with two competing, quantified logics:

- **History-based M-of-N:** a tentative track confirms after M hits in N looks (e.g. 3-of-5) and deletes after a set number of consecutive misses (e.g. 6) ([MATLAB, *Introduction to Track Logic*](https://www.mathworks.com/help/fusion/ug/introduction-to-track-logic.html)). Simple, robust at low false-alarm rate.
- **Score / SPRT-based:** each track carries a log-likelihood score incorporating detection probability, false-alarm density and new-target rate; a **Sequential Probability Ratio Test** confirms when the score crosses an upper threshold and rejects when it crosses a lower one, *delaying the decision* until evidence is sufficient ([Semantic Scholar, *SPRT-Based track confirmation and rejection*](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11805e8a74e4494a4700c437b5beacbadf953ff0); [MATLAB track logic]). The deletion rule is subtle and worth copying verbatim: a track is dropped when its score falls a fixed amount *below its own historical maximum* — not below an absolute floor — which prevents a once-strong track from being retained forever after the target is gone ([MATLAB track logic]). Score-based initiation specifically suppresses false tracks in heavy clutter (sea-clutter studies model clutter as K-distributed, true targets Poisson) ([Semantic Scholar, *score-based track initiation in sea clutter*](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27b75e8b9877eed8a3ce2b31d86e9dc9eabc79bb)).

**Transferable lesson.** Our LOCKED / PREDICTIVE_TRACK / REACQUIRE / HARD_LOST FSM with N-of-M confirmation *is* the radar lifecycle — confirming the architecture is sound. Two upgrades: **(1)** replace or augment the binary N-of-M with a **continuous lock-score updated by hit/miss likelihood**, and make HARD_LOST fire on *score-below-peak*, not just a miss counter — this gives a graceful, hysteretic drop that resists single-frame dropouts (occlusion by the target's own wing-flash, AGC blowout at horizon crossing) while still releasing a genuinely-gone target. This is exactly where our doctrine says the learned/AI layer is *allowed* to write: lock-quality and engage-permission, never centroid. The radar score is the natural carrier for that quality signal. **(2)** During PREDICTIVE_TRACK, coast on the IMM's CV model exactly as TWS radars coast through missed scans — we do this; the radar refinement is to *grow the gate by the propagated covariance during coast* so reacquisition is generous, then snap it back on re-lock.

### 2.5 Glint, multipath, and the extended-target endgame

Two radar failure modes map onto our terminal phase with eerie precision.

**Angle glint.** When a target becomes *resolved* (multiple scatterers within one resolution cell), the monopulse phase front wanders and the apparent angle can fall *outside the physical target*, with excursions of order the target extent — the estimate jitters or even points off-body ([IEEE/DTIC, *Track Performance Considerations for Monopulse Radars*](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA196232.pdf)). **Low-angle multipath** is the same disease: target-plus-ground-reflection acts as a glinting extended source, the tracker tries to null on the composite, and within ~one beamwidth of the horizon it "oscillates over about one beamwidth and frequently loses track altogether" ([US4005421, *Monopulse radar for improved low elevation tracking*](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4005421A/en); [IEEE, *Modified Monopulse for Low-Angle Multipath*](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4101487)). The mitigation that works is **EKF/Kalman smoothing of the angle through the glint** — predict position/velocity/acceleration of the angle and ride over the fluctuation rather than chasing each noisy sample ([MDPI, *Low-Angle Target via EKF Elevation Estimation*](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/13/19/3938)).

**Transferable lesson — this is our terminal phase exactly.** As we close, the winged UAV transitions point → resolved → fills-FOV; the centroid of a resolved, aspect-rotating, AGC-stretched thermal blob will glint over the airframe extent, and the background transitions cold-sky → horizon → hot-ground precisely when the target is near the horizon line — the optical low-angle-multipath case. Borrow the radar fixes: **(1)** trust the *filter prediction* over the raw centroid when glint/extent is detected — increase measurement noise R as the target's pixel extent grows (the optical analogue of beam-split), so the IMM smooths through glint instead of chasing it; this is already the right place for our ego.quality→R coupling. **(2)** Move from centroid to a **stable feature/correlation aimpoint** in the endgame: imaging-IR seekers explicitly abandon the centroid once the target resolves because the centroid overflows and wanders, and switch to a local feature / correlation lock or a convex-hull aimpoint ([SPIE, *Centroid tracker and aimpoint selection*](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/1697/1/Centroid-tracker-and-aimpoint-selection/10.1117/12.138205.short); [ResearchGate, *Feature-Based Tracking for IIR Anti-Ship Missiles*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258549005); [PMC, *Anti-Interference Aircraft-Tracking in IR Imagery*](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6470981)). A correlation tracker biased toward the nose/centroid is what holds a *consistent body point* through scale growth and aspect rotation.

### 2.6 ECCM track logic — the deception analogue

Radar's hardest-won track-management lessons come from **range/velocity-gate pull-off (RGPO/VGPO):** an adversary captures the tracking gate with a stronger false return, then walks the gate off the target ([Wikipedia, *Range-gate pull-off*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_gate_pull-off); [EMSOPEDIA, *RGPO*](https://www.emsopedia.org/entries/range-gate-pull-off-rangedeception/)). The defenses are pure track-management: **memory/inertial tracking with a narrow monitor gate** — coast on the predicted kinematics and watch a tight gate, because the deception return must accelerate *out of the kinematically-plausible region faster than a real target could*, exposing itself ([IEEE/IET, *Anti-vessel end-guidance radar ECCM against RGPO*](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/5367645)).

**Transferable lesson.** We have no jammer, but we have the *physical* equivalent: a hotter, brighter ground-clutter blob or a flare-like background source that captures a naive centroid gate and walks it off the cooler, smaller target — a "thermal pull-off." The radar countermeasure ports directly: **a tight kinematic monitor gate plus the rule that any candidate demanding implausible LOS-rate acceleration to grab the gate is rejected, with the filter coasting on prediction through the event.** This is the principled, doctrine-safe core of our anti-pull-off goal — and notably it writes only to association/lock-quality, never to the LOS-rate nuller's gain. It is also the right home for our NIS model-wrong alarm: an RGPO-like capture shows up as a sudden innovation spike, which is exactly the SPRT/NIS signal to *coast rather than chase*.

### Transferable to our seeker
- **Monopulse, not conescan, discipline:** derive the per-frame angle error from intensity-*normalized* geometry only; never let AGC or aspect-dependent brightness flicker leak into position. Σ-normalization = divide moments by total target intensity.
- **Singer colored-acceleration model in the IMM bank:** a fixed-wing's turns are autocorrelated, not white; a τ-parameterized Singer maneuver model fits the winged UAV's physics and reduces filter tuning to two dimensionless groups (maneuver index, ατ).
- **JPDA-style soft association for the cluttered endgame:** above ~1.2 competing blobs per gate, hard nearest-neighbor pull-off dominates; a likelihood-weighted update biased toward the predicted state is the anti-pull-off fix and stays guidance-legal.
- **Continuous SPRT/score lock-quality** replacing/augmenting binary N-of-M: delete on *score-below-its-own-peak*, not an absolute floor — graceful hysteresis through single-frame dropouts. This is the doctrine-sanctioned channel for the learned/engage-permission layer.
- **Covariance-grown gate during coast:** widen the validation gate by propagated covariance while in PREDICTIVE_TRACK, snap back on re-lock — the TWS missed-scan pattern.
- **Glint/multipath smoothing in the terminal phase:** raise measurement R with target pixel-extent and trust filter prediction over raw centroid through glint and horizon crossing; this is the optical low-angle-multipath case.
- **Centroid → correlation/feature aimpoint at resolve:** switch from centroid to a stable body-feature/correlation lock once the target fills the FOV, mirroring imaging-IR endgame practice.

### What we may have missed
- **A formal Singer τ for the target class:** we tune Q by hand; the radar literature gives a physics-anchored colored-acceleration model and a closed-form two-parameter steady-state error law we are not yet exploiting.
- **The 1.2-returns-per-gate NN breakdown threshold as an explicit mode-switch trigger:** instrument gate occupancy and switch GNN→JPDA-soft-update when the cluttered endgame crosses it, rather than running one association policy throughout.
- **Score-below-peak deletion logic:** our FSM appears to drop on miss-count/absolute criteria; the peak-relative SPRT deletion rule prevents both premature drops and zombie tracks and is strictly better.
- **A kinematic "monitor gate" anti-pull-off (RGPO analogue):** explicitly reject any gate-capturing candidate that demands implausible LOS-rate acceleration, coasting on prediction — a named, validated countermeasure to thermal pull-off we do not yet encode as such.
- **Tying NIS innovation spikes to a coast-don't-chase action:** we *raise* a model-wrong alarm but the radar doctrine is to *act* on it by inertial coasting, which closes the loop between detection and survival of the lock.
- **AGC-ahead-of-discriminator architecture:** verify our normalization truly precedes centroiding (radar puts AGC before the angle discriminator for a reason); if AGC transients reach the position estimate, we have a conescan-class vulnerability hiding in the FT640's 8-bit pipeline.

## 3. World Survey — EO/Visual Single-Object Tracking (the CV lineage)

A monocular strapdown LWIR seeker that must hold a single winged UAV from lock-on to closest-point-of-approach is, at the image-processing layer, a **single-object tracking (SOT)** problem with a brutal twist: the appearance model that lets you hold lock is the same model that, near impact, must survive scale growth from a sub-pixel point to a FOV-filling blob, aspect rotation, an aspect-dependent thermal signature, and a background transition from cold sky through horizon to hot-ground clutter — all inside ~17 ms/frame on a Pi 5. The CV SOT literature is precisely the discipline of *initializing an appearance model from one bounding box and propagating it through such transformations*. This section surveys that lineage, family by family, and asks of each: does it transfer to a cheap passive monocular uncooled-LWIR strapdown seeker, and at what cost?

### 3.1 The classical kernel: area correlation, mean-shift, and why they still matter

The oldest militarily-deployed EO/IRST tracker is **area / normalized cross-correlation (NCC)**: store a template patch, slide it over the next frame, pick the peak of the (mean-and-variance-normalized) correlation surface. It is exactly what early imaging Maverick and Paveway-class trackers did, and it is still the right *primitive* for a point-to-resolved target because it makes no appearance assumption beyond local photometric similarity. NCC's weaknesses are well known: O(N·M) cost without an FFT, no intrinsic scale/rotation handling, and template drift if you update every frame. Its decisive descendant is **MOSSE** (Bolme et al., CVPR 2010), which trains a *Minimum Output Sum of Squared Error* correlation filter directly in the Fourier domain so that detection is one element-wise multiply and an inverse FFT — running at **669 FPS** in the original paper ([Bolme 2010, ORNL/CVPR](https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/visual-object-tracking-using-adaptive-correlation-filters/)). Two MOSSE ideas are permanently transferable: (a) **PSR — peak-to-sidelobe ratio** — as a per-frame confidence/occlusion signal (peak minus sidelobe-mean over sidelobe-std; a drop flags occlusion or drift), and (b) "pause and resume" — freeze the filter when PSR collapses rather than learning garbage ([Bolme 2010](https://github.com/amoudgl/mosse-tracker)).

**Mean-shift / CAMShift** (gradient ascent on a histogram back-projection toward the mode of a similarity density) is the other classical pillar ([NUS CS4243 notes](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~cs4243/lecture/meanshift.pdf)). It is cheap and elegant but built on a **color/intensity histogram** appearance model with **static bandwidth** (mean-shift) or a crude moment-based scale (CAMShift). For an 8-bit AGC LWIR image where the target is a few hot pixels on a near-uniform sky, a histogram has almost no discriminative content, and CAMShift's scale-from-zeroth-moment will be dominated by AGC-driven background swings. **Lesson: mean-shift/CAMShift does not transfer** — the appearance statistic it relies on is the one thing an uncooled, non-radiometric, auto-gained LWIR frame doesn't supply. NCC/MOSSE *do* transfer, and in fact a MOSSE-style filter is the single best fit to our compute budget.

### 3.2 Discriminative correlation filters: KCF, DSST, CSR-DCF, ECO — the workhorse era

The 2014–2017 DCF wave turned correlation tracking into a *discriminative* learner by exploiting circulant matrices: all cyclic shifts of the patch are trained on simultaneously in the Fourier domain, making a ridge-regression appearance model nearly free. **KCF** (Henriques et al., 2015) adds a kernel trick and HOG features and runs at **~650 FPS** ([KCF, arXiv 1404.7584](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.7584)). KCF alone is **fixed-scale** — fatal for a closing engagement where scale grows monotonically. **DSST** bolts on a separate 1-D scale-space correlation filter (a pyramid of patch scales) and still runs at **~106 FPS** ([KCF-DSST](https://github.com/liliumao/KCF-DSST)); this is the canonical cheap **scale-adaptive** recipe and is directly relevant to our point→resolved→fills-FOV scale problem. **CSR-DCF / CSRT** (Lukežič, CVPR 2017) adds **channel-and-spatial reliability** — a foreground/background spatial mask that suppresses the DCF *boundary effect* and lets the filter learn a non-rectangular target — at **~12 FPS** with much better accuracy ([CSR-DCF, CVPR 2017](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Lukezic_Discriminative_Correlation_Filter_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf)). **ECO** (Danelljan, CVPR 2017) factorizes the filter bank (512→64 basis filters), compresses the sample set, and updates conservatively every N frames — its hand-crafted-feature variant runs **60 Hz on a single CPU at 65% AUC on OTB-2015** ([ECO, CVPR 2017](https://www.cvl.isy.liu.se/research/objrec/visualtracking/ecotrack/index.html)).

The **UAV-tracking sub-literature is the most directly transferable body of work we have**, because it solves *our* deployment constraint — DCF on a power/compute-starved airborne platform. The survey of Correlation Filters for UAV aerial tracking ([arXiv 2010.06255](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.06255)) and the **BACF / ARCF** line are the key reads. **BACF** (background-aware CF) crops *real* negative samples from a large search region instead of cyclic-shift phantoms, attacking the boundary effect at its root. **ARCF** (Aberrance Repressed CF, ICCV 2019) adds a regularizer that *penalizes sudden changes in the response map between frames* — explicitly suppressing the response "aberrance" caused by background transitions and partial occlusion, and it is noted as outstanding on **small-object** aerial scenes ([ARCF, ICCV 2019](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Huang_Learning_Aberrance_Repressed_Correlation_Filters_for_Real-Time_UAV_Tracking_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf)). ARCF's temporal-smoothness-of-response prior is conceptually identical to what our IMM/NIS gate enforces in *state* space — but ARCF enforces it in *image* space, which we currently do not.

**Transferable DCF lessons:** the DSST decoupled scale filter; CSR-DCF's spatial reliability mask (a target/background prior, which a top-hat/CFAR foreground map already half-gives us); ARCF's response-aberrance penalty as a background-transition immunizer; and the **response-map confidence metrics**. Two confidence statistics matter for our doctrine: **PSR**, and **APCE** (Average Peak-to-Correlation Energy), defined as APCE = |F_max − F_min|² / mean Σ(F_w,h − F_min)², which quantifies how *peaked vs. fluttery* the response surface is and is the standard gate for "only update the model when confident" ([APCE / high-confidence update, Wang 2017 lineage](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11042-019-7216-1)). These map cleanly onto our **lock-quality / engage-permission** channel without ever touching the centroid — exactly our doctrine invariant.

**What does *not* transfer from DCF:** HOG/Color-Names feature stacks (designed for textured visible imagery; an LWIR blob has little oriented-gradient content until it resolves), and the per-frame template update that causes drift on a maneuvering, aspect-rotating target.

### 3.3 Siamese trackers: learned similarity, fixed template

**SiamFC** (2016) reframed tracking as a learned cross-correlation: a shared CNN embeds a template and a search region, and a single correlation layer produces a similarity heatmap; it won the **VOT-17 real-time challenge** ([SiamFC family](https://medium.com/alegion/single-object-tracking-using-the-siamese-family-of-trackers-part-1-siamfc-43e6aabfe55a)). **SiamRPN / SiamRPN++ / SiamMask** added a region-proposal head for joint box regression (giving **aspect-ratio**, not just scale), enabled deep ResNet backbones via spatial-aware sampling, and added a segmentation branch ([SiamRPN++, CVPR 2019](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/papers/Li_SiamRPN_Evolution_of_Siamese_Visual_Tracking_With_Very_Deep_Networks_CVPR_2019_paper.pdf)). The conceptual gift of the Siamese line is **template-vs-search learned matching** that is far more robust to appearance change than a single linear filter — and the **fixed first-frame template** (no online update) is *itself* a drift-control strategy, which aligns with our LOBL (lock-on-before-launch) reality where the lock template is acquired up front.

The problem is compute. A ResNet-50 SiamRPN++ forward pass is hundreds of MFLOPs and will not fit 17 ms on a Pi 5 CPU. The relevant transfer is the **efficient-Siamese** branch: **LightTrack** (NAS-searched lightweight backbone/head), **NanoTrack** (SiamBAN+LightTrack-derived, **>200 FPS on an Apple M1 CPU** via NCNN, explicitly built for embedded/mobile), and **FEAR-XS** (dual-template, **>200 FPS on iPhone-11**, 4.3× faster and 2.4× smaller than LightTrack) ([NanoTrack](https://github.com/HonglinChu/NanoTrack), [FEAR, ECCV 2022](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136820625.pdf)). FEAR's **dual-template** idea — keep the immutable lock template *and* a slowly-updated dynamic template, fused — is the single most relevant occlusion/drift-control mechanism from this family: it lets you absorb the aspect-rotation and scale-growth of a closing engagement without letting one bad frame poison the immutable reference. On a Pi 5 (ARM-A76, no usable GPU, NEON only), an INT8-quantized Nano/FEAR-class network *might* fit in budget alongside our existing 17 ms pipeline, but it is the riskiest item to schedule and would consume most of the frame.

**Transferable Siamese lessons:** fixed-reference matching (matches LOBL); explicit box-regression head for *aspect-ratio* estimation (we currently track angles, not target extent); FEAR's dual-template drift control. **What doesn't transfer:** anything ResNet-class, and the training-data assumption — these nets are trained overwhelmingly on **visible-band** video; their priors transfer poorly to 8-14 µm AGC imagery without TIR-specific retraining (see §3.5).

### 3.4 Transformer trackers: the accuracy ceiling, the wrong compute class

The current SOTA — **TransT, STARK, MixFormer, OSTrack, SeqTrack** — replaces hand-designed cross-correlation with attention. The decisive architectural shift is **one-stream** tracking (OSTrack, MixFormer, SimTrack): template and search tokens are concatenated and processed jointly so feature extraction and template-search fusion happen together, with **candidate-elimination** modules pruning background tokens for speed ([Transformer SOT survey, arXiv 2302.11867](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.11867)). **STARK** added a learned dynamic-template-update head; **SeqTrack/ARTrack** predict the box autoregressively. Even the *efficient* variants are out of class for us: **MixFormerV2-B runs 165 FPS on a desktop GPU** ([MixFormerV2, arXiv 2305.15896](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.15896)) — that number does not survive transplant to a Pi CPU. The transferable *idea*, not the model, is **attention-style adaptive template-search fusion** and STARK's **confidence-gated** template update — both concepts we can approximate cheaply (e.g., a learned scalar that *only* writes to lock-quality). **What doesn't transfer:** the architecture itself. Transformers are the right answer on a gimbal with an embedded GPU/NPU, the wrong answer on a bare Pi 5.

### 3.5 Re-detection, long-term tracking, and the thermal benchmarks

Our FSM already has REACQUIRE/HARD_LOST states; the long-term-tracking literature is exactly the formalization of that. **GlobalTrack** builds a target-specific Faster-R-CNN that searches the *entire frame* with no motion prior — the canonical full-image re-detector for "target absent" ([Long-term VT repo](https://github.com/wangdongdut/Long-term-Visual-Tracking)). **LTMU** is the more transferable design: a short-term tracker plus a learned **Meta-Updater** that fuses geometric, appearance, and confidence cues to decide *whether the current state is reliable enough to update*, and a SiamRPN re-detector that fires when it isn't — winning VOT-LT with **F=0.690 vs 0.629** for SiamRPN++ ([LTMU / Meta-Updater](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/340374587_High-Performance_Long-Term_Tracking_with_Meta-Updater)). The LTMU pattern — *short-term tracker + confidence verifier + bounded re-detector* — is essentially our LOCKED/PREDICTIVE_TRACK/REACQUIRE FSM, and the **Meta-Updater is the principled version of our lock-quality channel**. The doctrinally critical transfer: re-detection must be **bounded** (search the covariance-predicted region, not the whole frame), or a full-image GlobalTrack-style re-detect will blow the 17 ms wall and risk locking the wrong object — a WRONG-HIT, our hard fail.

The **TIR benchmarks** are the reality check on every learned method above. **LSOTB-TIR** (1,400 sequences, 600K frames, 47 categories) defines 12 challenge attributes including the ones that define our endgame: **thermal crossover** (target and background reach the same apparent temperature — the LWIR analog of camouflage), **camera shake**, and **target intensity change** ([LSOTB-TIR](https://github.com/QiaoLiuHit/LSOTB-TIR)). **PTB-TIR** (60 pedestrian sequences) isolates scale variation, background clutter, fast motion ([PTB-TIR, arXiv 1801.05944](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.05944)). **VOT-TIR** scores by EAO/Accuracy/Robustness. The consistent finding across this literature (and the reason a dedicated TIR-tracking field exists) is that **visible-trained appearance models degrade on TIR** — low texture, polarity inversions, AGC-driven contrast swings, and thermal crossover all break priors learned on RGB. This is *exactly our regime* and is the strongest argument for a **physics-first detection front-end** (our top-hat/CFAR/MPCM stack) feeding a **lightweight, TIR-aware** appearance/association layer, rather than betting the lock on a heavy learned tracker.

### 3.6 Synthesis — the best track-maintenance design realizable in 17 ms on a Pi

The budget verdict is sharp. Transformer and ResNet-Siamese trackers are out (desktop-GPU class). The realizable design is a **layered tracker**: a physics-based detector for the small-target/sky regime (what we have), a **MOSSE/DSST-class correlation filter** that takes over the moment the target resolves to a trackable patch (giving cheap translation + a decoupled scale filter), **PSR/APCE response-confidence** wired *only* to lock-quality/engage-permission, a **FEAR-style dual-template** (immutable LOBL reference + slowly-updated dynamic template) for drift control through aspect rotation, an **ARCF-style response-aberrance penalty** to immunize against the sky→horizon→ground background transition, and an **LTMU-style bounded re-detector** confined to the IMM covariance gate. Crucially, all appearance machinery feeds *measurements and confidence* into the existing IMM/LOS-rate-nuller — never the gain — preserving every doctrine invariant.

### Transferable to our seeker
- **MOSSE/DSST core**: an FFT correlation filter with a *separate 1-D scale filter* is the only learned-tracking family that fits 17 ms on a Pi and directly handles the monotonic scale growth of a closing engagement ([Bolme 2010](https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/visual-object-tracking-using-adaptive-correlation-filters/); [DSST](https://github.com/liliumao/KCF-DSST)).
- **PSR + APCE as the lock-quality signal**: response-map peakedness is a cheap, principled occlusion/drift/confidence metric that writes only to engage-permission, never to the centroid ([APCE](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11042-019-7216-1)).
- **FEAR dual-template**: keep the immutable lock-on template and a slowly-updated dynamic template, fused — absorbs aspect rotation and signature change without one bad frame corrupting the reference ([FEAR, ECCV 2022](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136820625.pdf)).
- **ARCF response-aberrance penalty**: penalize abrupt frame-to-frame response-map change to survive the cold-sky→horizon→hot-ground background transition — image-space analog of our NIS state-space guard ([ARCF, ICCV 2019](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Huang_Learning_Aberrance_Repressed_Correlation_Filters_for_Real-Time_UAV_Tracking_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf)).
- **LTMU short-term + meta-updater + bounded re-detector**: the principled form of our LOCKED/REACQUIRE FSM; confine re-detection to the IMM covariance gate so it never costs the full frame or locks a decoy ([LTMU](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/340374587_High-Performance_Long-Term_Tracking_with_Meta-Updater)).
- **CSR-DCF spatial-reliability mask**: reuse the top-hat/CFAR foreground map as a target/background mask to suppress DCF boundary effects on a small, non-rectangular hot blob ([CSR-DCF, CVPR 2017](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Lukezic_Discriminative_Correlation_Filter_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf)).

### What we may have missed
- **No image-space response-confidence metric.** We gate in *state* space (NIS, covariance gate) but have no PSR/APCE-style *appearance*-confidence on the lock itself — the cheapest, most-proven CV health signal, and one our doctrine could legally consume on the lock-quality channel.
- **No decoupled scale/aspect estimator.** Our angular IMM tracks [az,el,rates] but estimates neither target *extent* nor *aspect ratio*; a DSST scale filter or a Siamese box head would give an explicit growing-scale and head-on→beam aspect cue that aids aimpoint stability as the target fills the FOV.
- **No dual/dynamic template.** We have CV-coast and an association cost but no explicit immutable-plus-adaptive appearance pair; this is the field's standard drift-control answer for exactly the aspect-rotation problem TASK #1 poses.
- **No explicit response-aberrance / background-transition immunizer in image space.** ARCF/BACF show the sky→ground clutter transition is best fought where it happens — in the correlation response map — not only downstream in the state filter.
- **No TIR-benchmark-driven failure taxonomy.** LSOTB-TIR's "thermal crossover" and "target intensity change" attributes are named, reproducible endgame failure modes; we should be regression-testing the seeker against TIR sequences exhibiting them, not only synthetic geometry ([LSOTB-TIR](https://github.com/QiaoLiuHit/LSOTB-TIR)).
- **Under-exploited foreground prior.** We compute strong CFAR/MPCM foreground maps but don't yet feed them as a CSR-DCF-style spatial reliability mask into any matched-filter/correlation stage — a near-free accuracy gain already paid for upstream.

## 4. World Survey — Counter-UAS & Drone-Interceptor Systems

The counter-UAS (C-UAS) world has, in roughly five years, gone from "shoot the drone with a bigger missile" to a dense ecosystem of purpose-built kinetic interceptors, each of which has had to solve some version of TASK #1: get a small, low-contrast, maneuvering air target into custody and *keep* it there through a closing geometry that destroys the very image features the tracker locked onto. This section surveys the fielded and disclosed systems by how they detect, hand off, track, and close — and pulls, for each, the lesson that does or does not transfer to a passive monocular uncooled-LWIR strapdown seeker on a Raspberry Pi 5.

### 4.1 The two architectural families

Fielded interceptors split cleanly into two families, distinguished by *where the tracking intelligence lives*.

**Family A — Off-board cued, RF/radar-seeker terminal.** Raytheon's **Coyote Block 2** is the canonical example. It is a turbine-powered interceptor (≈345–370 mph, 10–15 km reach) carrying a **radio-frequency (radar) seeker**, cued and mid-course-corrected by the ground-based **Ku-band KuRFS** radar inside the LIDS/FS-LIDS kill chain ([RTX Coyote](https://www.rtx.com/raytheon/what-we-do/integrated-air-and-missile-defense/coyote); [Army Recognition](https://www.armyrecognition.com/news/army-news/2025/pentagon-awards-raytheon-5-04b-army-contract-for-coyote-counter-uas-and-kurfs-radars)). The architecture is *radar-centric*: KuRFS holds the track and exploits **micro-Doppler** (rotor/prop modulation) for classification; the EO/IR cameras in the FS-LIDS suite are primarily for operator confirmation/ID, not for closing the loop ([defense-update FS-LIDS](https://defense-update.com/20250526_fs-lids.html)). The interceptor's own seeker takes over only in the final basket.

**Family B — On-board vision/IR terminal homing.** This is where the world has moved for the *small, cheap, agile* threat. **Anduril Roadrunner-M** (twin-turbojet, thrust-vectored, high-G, reusable) and **MARSS Interceptor-MR** (reusable hit-to-kill ram, <$50k, IR terminal) both follow the same pattern: an off-board sensor net (radar + EO/IR, fused in a C2 layer) flies the interceptor to within visual range, then **an onboard infrared camera takes custody for the terminal phase** ([Anduril Roadrunner](https://www.anduril.com/roadrunner); [MARSS NiDAR](https://marss.com/products/nidar-cuas/); [cuashub MARSS](https://cuashub.com/en/content/marss-completes-testing-of-counter-drone-interceptor/)). MARSS is explicit: the interceptor "receives regular updates on the target position until it's within visual range of the infrared camera," after which the IR seeker holds it. **Fortem DroneHunter F700** is the soft-kill cousin — radar-cued (onboard **TrueView R20/R40**), AI-classified, net-capture — but the same custody logic applies: TrueView "detects, tracks, and locks onto its target," then the interceptor prosecutes autonomously ([Fortem F700](https://fortemtech.com/products/dronehunter-f700/); [Army-Technology](https://www.army-technology.com/projects/dronehunter-counter-unmanned-aerial-system-c-uas/)).

**Lesson for us.** Our interceptor is structurally a *Family-B terminal seeker with no Family-A front end*. We are LOBL (lock-on-before-launch) precisely because we have no KuRFS to cue us and no datalink to feed mid-course updates. The transferable insight is that *every* fielded Family-B system treats the IR terminal phase as the hard part and budgets a separate, robust subsystem for it — which is exactly what our lock FSM and IMM are. What does *not* transfer is their luxury of an off-board track to fall back on: when MARSS or Roadrunner loses IR custody for a moment, the radar track is still alive and can re-cue. We have only the image. That single fact justifies our most conservative design choices (PREDICTIVE_TRACK coast, default-deny abort).

### 4.2 The acquisition-to-track chain and the handoff problem

The dominant C-UAS doctrine is **slew-to-cue**: a volume-search radar (or RF/acoustic net) detects at range and passes a 3-D cue (az, el, range) to an EO/IR gimbal, which snaps to that bearing; "advanced AI algorithms within the camera system then take over, locking onto the drone's pixels to provide automated, persistent optical tracking" ([Drone-Warfare multi-sensor fusion](https://drone-warfare.com/counter-uas/multi-sensor-fusion/); [Slew-to-Cue EO/IR network, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/330200888)). Fusion doctrine demands **corroboration from ≥2 independent sensors** before escalating, and frames the core question as *track custody*: "can the system keep the target in custody long enough to respond?" ([Drone-Warfare](https://drone-warfare.com/counter-uas/multi-sensor-fusion/)).

The physics behind the handoff is the reason it is hard. Radar offers near range-independent detection of a small UAS out to kilometers and gives *range*, which an angle-only tracker never gets. EO/IR offers fine **angular resolution** but a small target subtends only a few pixels until close. A microbolometer's IFOV sets the floor: our FT640 at ~48.7°×38.6° over a 640×512 array gives ≈1.33 mrad/pixel; a 2 m-span winged UAV first becomes a *resolved* (multi-pixel, shape-bearing) object only inside roughly 1–1.5 km, and is a sub-pixel-to-few-pixel **point source** beyond that. So the acquisition chain everyone uses — radar finds it as a Doppler/range blip, EO/IR confirms it as a point, then *grows* it into a resolved object as range closes — is dictated by sensor physics, not preference.

**Lesson for us.** We collapse the entire slew-to-cue chain into one passive sensor, so we inherit *all three regimes in one engagement*: point-source (CFAR/MPCM/top-hat detection), transition (resolved blob, association ambiguity), and FOV-filling (the target's own structure becomes clutter). Family-A/B systems hand the regime change off to *different sensors*; we must hand it off between *different algorithms inside the same pipeline*. That is precisely the seam our detector→blob→association→IMM stack spans, and it is where track-loss risk concentrates. The transferable practice is **explicit custody accounting**: fielded fusion engines never assume the track survived a regime change — they re-corroborate. Our analog is the IMM **NIS model-wrong alarm** and the FSM's REACQUIRE state. **What we may have missed:** the ≥2-corroboration doctrine has no passive-monocular analog *spatially*, but it does *temporally* — N-of-M trajectory continuity (which we built) is the right idea; we could push it further with a second *independent feature channel* (e.g. motion/MTI track vs. intensity-blob track) that must agree before LOCKED, mimicking dual-sensor corroboration within one camera.

### 4.3 Holding a small, maneuvering target — the tracking science

The open IR-tracking literature targets exactly our failure modes. Three threads matter.

**Interacting Multiple Model (IMM) filtering.** IMM runs a bank of motion models (e.g. constant-velocity, constant-acceleration, coordinated-turn) in parallel, weighting each by its likelihood against the latest measurement and mixing the estimates through a Markov model-transition matrix. It is the standard answer to *maneuvering* targets because it switches models faster than a single tuned Kalman filter and degrades gracefully when the target jinks ([IMM cubature KF, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5492340/); [IMM for TBM tracking](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3007678)). Recent work (**UIMM-Tracker**, 2025) adds *uncertainty detection* that dynamically modulates the Markov transition probabilities so the filter adapts its own agility to the observed maneuver state ([UIMM-Tracker](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025RemS...17.2052H/abstract)). This is *exactly* our 4-state modified-polar IMM with a NIS-driven model-wrong alarm.

**Scale and aspect change during approach.** The most directly relevant finding: in IR seeker geometry "both target and missile are highly maneuverable, with strong ego-motion and scale changes dramatically during the approaching process, making it more difficult to maintain robust track" ([Anti-interference IR aircraft tracking, PMC6470981](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6470981/)). The literature's countermeasures are **scale-adaptive correlation/template tracking** (resize the matched template as the target grows), **ego-motion compensation** (de-rotate the image with onboard attitude before correlation — our gyro de-rotation), and **anti-interference re-acquisition** logic that distinguishes a target lost to occlusion/flare from one lost to mis-association.

**Track-before-detect (TBD) and pipeline filters** integrate energy over multiple frames *before* declaring a detection, recovering sub-pixel point targets buried in clutter ([SSD + pipeline filter, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1051200420302943); [Labeled-RFS TBD, PMC4721749](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4721749/)). This is the long-range acquisition regime where the winged UAV is still a point against cold sky.

**Lesson for us.** Our IMM, gyro de-rotation, and N-of-M continuity are squarely on the documented best-practice path. The **transferable gap** is *scale-adaptive appearance*: the literature is emphatic that the dominant terminal track-loss mode is scale change, and the fix is a tracker whose template/gate grows with the target. Our covariance-sized gate adapts to *kinematic* uncertainty but not to the target's *angular extent* — as the UAV fills the FOV, a centroid that was stable on a blob can walk across a resolved, aspect-rotating shape (head-on motor hotspot → beam-aspect wing/fuselage), pulling the aimpoint. That is the single most important external lesson for TASK #1.

### 4.4 The FPV drone-vs-drone revolution and last-mile autonomy

The Ukraine/Russia FPV-interceptor practice is the closest real-world analog to our exact hardware class, and it has converged hard on **"last-mile / terminal autonomy."** The operator flies the interceptor to within ~0.5–1 km; then an onboard module *"independently locks onto and tracks the target"* for the final approach, surviving EW jamming and link loss ([thedefender.media](https://thedefender.media/en/2026/05/terminal-guidance-interceptor-drones/); [United24 P1-SUN](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/ukraine-unveils-ai-powered-p1-sun-long-drone-that-spots-shaheds-faster-than-human-operators-19925)). Specifics that matter to us:

- **The Fourth Law TFL-1/TFL-2**: machine-vision terminal guidance taking control in the final ~500 m; the anti-Shahed variant runs a **thermal camera (Odd Systems Kurbas-640a)** and explicitly **"places a green contour around the drone and maintains stable real-time tracking"** of static, moving, and *camouflaged* targets — and TFL-2 is disclosed to run on a **Raspberry Pi 5**, our exact compute ([The Fourth Law, Ukraine's Arms Monitor](https://ukrainesarmsmonitor.substack.com/p/the-fourth-law-autonomy-for-every)). Reported field hit rate ≈80%, raising FPV success from ~20% to ~80% when added ([United24 Fourth Law module](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/new-ai-guided-drone-system-helps-ukraine-bypass-russian-jamming-14057)).
- **SkyFall P1-SUN**: AI terminal guidance that detects and locks aerial targets to ~1 km on low-resolution thermal imagers, using neural nets "trained specifically for early target detection" ([United24](https://united24media.com/war-in-ukraine/ukraine-unveils-ai-powered-p1-sun-long-drone-that-spots-shaheds-faster-than-human-operators-19925)).
- **Merops Surveyor**: machine-vision + thermal/radar autonomous ram that "lock[s] onto and ram[s] targets even when radio and GPS links are jammed" ([dronelife](https://dronelife.com/2026/05/21/perennial-autonomy-pentagon-contract/)).

**Lesson for us.** This family validates our entire premise: a cheap, passive, monocular thermal seeker on a Pi *can* hold lock on a maneuvering air target through terminal closure — it is being done at scale in combat. The transferable engineering choices are (a) **terminal autonomy that is robust to link loss by design** (we go further — we have *no* link), and (b) the **"green contour" pattern** — a continuously re-fit bounding region whose size tracks the target's growth, which is the scale-adaptive answer Section 4.3 demanded.

But two of their choices **do not transfer, and we must consciously reject them.** First, these modules are overwhelmingly **learned detectors/trackers that write directly to the aimpoint** (the neural net *is* the centroid). Our doctrine forbids this — learned/AI writes ONLY to lock-quality/engage-permission, never to centroid/LOS/tracker — for a default-deny, WRONG-HIT-is-hard-fail reason their attritable economics let them ignore. Their 80% hit rate *is acceptable to them*; an 80% correct-centroid rate is not acceptable to us. Second, many use **GPS/inertial cruise-mode coast** through jamming; our coast is the IMM **CV prediction in angle space**, not a position cruise — appropriate because range is unobservable.

### 4.5 Guidance-law convergence

Across families, the terminal law is some form of **LOS-rate nulling**. Classical proportional navigation commands lateral acceleration $a_c = N\,V_c\,\dot\lambda$ — proportional to closing velocity $V_c$ and LOS rate $\dot\lambda$. Radar seekers (Coyote) get $V_c$ directly and use full range-scaled PN. **A passive angle-only seeker cannot measure $V_c$ or range**, so range-scaled PN is unavailable — which is precisely why our design uses a **$\dot\lambda$-nuller** (drive LOS rate to zero, no range/closing-speed scaling) and why our doctrine declares *range never scales guidance gain*. The Ukrainian vision modules, lacking range too, implement an equivalent image-plane LOS-centering behavior (keep the contour at a fixed aimpoint), which is a $\dot\lambda$-nuller in pixel space. This is a case where the *passive* constraint forces the *right simple* answer, and the world's passive interceptors have converged on it independently.

### Transferable to our seeker
- **Scale-adaptive appearance/gate is the #1 terminal lesson.** The IR-seeker literature names dramatic scale change as the dominant terminal track-loss mode; the Ukrainian "green-contour" modules answer it with a continuously re-fit, size-tracking region. Add an *angular-extent-adaptive* component to our blob/gate so the centroid stays anchored as the winged UAV grows from point → resolved → FOV-filling and rotates head-on → beam.
- **Aspect-dependent thermal signature must be modeled, not assumed.** Every fielded IR seeker contends with the motor/exhaust hotspot disappearing off-axis. Our centroid should be biased toward a *geometrically stable* aimpoint (e.g. body centroid) rather than the brightest pixel, which migrates with aspect.
- **Custody re-corroboration after regime changes.** Borrow the fusion doctrine of never trusting a track across a sensor/regime transition: gate the sky→horizon→ground-clutter background transition and the point→resolved transition as explicit "re-confirm lock" events in the FSM, not silent continuations.
- **Terminal autonomy robust to information loss is the proven pattern** — combat-validated on our exact Pi-5/uncooled-thermal class (TFL-2, P1-SUN). Our IMM-CV coast through brief loss is the angle-space version; keep it, and tune the coast horizon against the documented ~0.5–1 km terminal-takeover window.
- **IMM with self-modulating agility (UIMM-style) is current best practice** and we already run it; the actionable upgrade is tying maneuver-state estimate to the model-transition matrix, not just to a NIS alarm.
- **$\dot\lambda$-nulling without range scaling is the correct, independently-converged passive law** — Coyote needs radar for PN; we and the Ukrainian vision modules do not. Validates our doctrine invariant.

### What we may have missed
- **Dual-channel intra-sensor corroboration.** Fielded fusion requires two independent sensors to agree before lock; we could approximate this *inside one camera* by requiring our intensity-blob track and our MTI/motion track to agree before declaring LOCKED — a temporal/feature analog of the ≥2-sensor rule we don't yet enforce.
- **Track-before-detect (TBD) energy integration** for the long-range point-source regime. Pipeline/RFS-TBD filters recover sub-pixel targets against cold sky *before* a hard detection; our CFAR/MPCM stack is single-frame-thresholded and may miss the winged UAV at maximum range where it is dimmest.
- **Aspect/scale as an explicit measured state, not a nuisance.** No part of our stack currently *estimates* target angular extent or aspect angle; the IR-seeker literature treats scale as a tracked quantity. Estimating it would let the gate, centroid bias, and even the IMM measurement-noise R adapt to the closure phase.
- **Anti-flare / decoy-discrimination logic.** Fielded IR seekers carry explicit anti-interference re-acquisition that distinguishes a target lost to a flare/hotspot from one lost to mis-association. Against a *cooperative* threat this matters; even against a non-cooperative winged UAV, a hot ground source in the FOV-filling phase is a functional decoy our association cost may not reject.
- **Quantified custody budgeting.** The fusion community reasons in explicit kill-chain probabilities (e.g. 99% detect × 95% track × 95% ID × 95% defeat). We have no published per-stage custody-probability budget for our pipeline across the three image regimes — building one would expose which regime transition is the weakest link in TASK #1.

## 5. Deep Problem — Holding Lock Through Closing Geometry

Task #1 is not "detect a drone." It is to **never let go** of one specific winged UAV from lock-on to closest-point-of-approach, across an engagement in which the target's image is a moving target in every sense: its scale spans three or more orders of magnitude in solid angle, its aspect rotates, its thermal signature breathes with that aspect, and the background slides from cold sky through the horizon line into hot ground clutter — all while the target jinks and our own strapdown body slews. This is the *terminal homing autotracker* problem, and the open literature is explicit that the hard part is exactly ours: "the auto-tracker must select an appropriate aimpoint as the target grows from a point source at long range to a high-resolution image filling a large percentage of the field-of-view at short range" ([IEEE, *Target tracking and aimpoint selection in homing stage*](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/112783/); [US Patent 4,959,714, segmentation for terminal aimpoint on moving objects](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4959714)). Below I walk the engagement stage by stage, name the algorithm that owns each stage, give the governing relations and the failure mode, and extract the transfer to our FT640/Pi-5 strapdown nuller.

### 5.1 The scale axis: one detector cannot span the whole engagement

The dominant nonstationarity is **angular scale**. The target's apparent diameter in pixels grows as θ ≈ (L·cosψ)/R · (N_px/FOV), where L is span, ψ aspect, R range, and our instantaneous FOV is ~48.7° over the array width. A 2–3 m winged UAV that is a sub-pixel point at a few km becomes a 2–5 px blob, then a resolved shape of tens of pixels, then *overfills* the frame in the last fraction of a second. No single detector is valid across that span, and the breakpoints are sharp.

**Stage 1 — unresolved point source.** Here matched-filter / morphological small-target detection is optimal because the target's spatial signature is the point-spread function. Our stack lives here: white top-hat + percentile/MAD + region-adaptive CFAR + MPCM local contrast + directional max-median. This is exactly the canonical ISTD toolchain — top-hat morphology with multi-directional structuring elements to measure target-vs-surround difference, CFAR as the segmentation thresholder, and patch-contrast saliency ([modified top-hat transforms, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S004579061000056X); [improved top-hat + ADMM, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9571038/); [awesome-infrared-small-targets survey](https://github.com/Tianfang-Zhang/awesome-infrared-small-targets)). The SNR-defining quantity is contrast against local clutter, and CFAR holds detections at a fixed false-alarm rate by setting the threshold factor from the local background statistic.

**The breakdown — and our own code already names it.** A white top-hat is a *band-pass* tuned to objects smaller than the structuring element; it **suppresses the interior of an extended target** and returns only its rim. The instant the UAV resolves past the SE size, the top-hat's reported `area_px` *collapses* even as the real object grows — a catastrophic, silent failure for any size- or energy-gated logic. Our detector encodes precisely this knowledge: `detect.py` computes a separate `area_extended_px` cue for the top-K salient blobs, with the in-code rationale "the white top-hat suppresses the interior of a LARGE/extended target, so `area_px`" is not the true footprint. That is the right instinct, but in the current stack it is *diagnostic only* — the system has the cue but does not yet **switch detector regimes** on it. This is the single most important transition in the engagement and it deserves to be a first-class state, not a logged number.

**Stage 2 — resolved blob.** Once the target is a connected region of N≳10–20 px, the optimal estimator is no longer a point-detector; it is **region segmentation + correlation/appearance tracking**. The seeker literature is unambiguous that the autotracker must segment the image, rank-and-select the best candidate, and *gate* it ([US Patent 4,959,714](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4959714)). Modern CV's answer is the **scale-adaptive correlation filter** (KCF/DSST family): a discriminative template correlated each frame, with an explicit *scale pyramid* so the gate grows with the target ([Scale-Adaptive KCF, Hindawi](https://www.hindawi.com/journals/mpe/2017/1605959/); [Multi-Template Scale-Adaptive KCF, ICCV-W 2015](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_iccv_2015_workshops/w14/papers/Bibi_Multi-Template_Scale-Adaptive_ICCV_2015_paper.pdf)). The transferable mechanism is the *coupling of the gate/template size to an estimated scale*, not the specific filter.

**Stage 3 — fills / overfills FOV.** When the target subtends a large fraction of the frame, even segmentation fails: there is no "background" to subtract, the target may be clipped by the FOV, and edges leave the array. The end-game must fall back to **edge/feature-region tracking on a chosen sub-feature** of the target rather than the whole-body centroid. This is the regime where strapdown nullers earn or lose their miss distance.

### 5.2 Aimpoint vs. centroid: the divergence that creates miss distance

While unresolved, centroid = hotspot = aimpoint; there is nothing to choose. Once resolved, the three **diverge**, and the choice is a guidance decision with a metric cost. The classic seeker error here is that an amplitude/energy-centroid tracker does not reliably home on a stable body point: as the image enlarges, an amplitude-modulated offset estimate grows its error (the historical reason seekers moved from AM to FM offset sensing — [Infrared homing, Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Infrared_homing)). Two coupled effects bite our passive LWIR seeker specifically:

- **Aspect-dependent hotspot migration.** The thermal centroid is driven by the hottest aspect-visible feature — motor/exhaust/leading-edge aero-heating. As aspect rotates head-on → beam, that hot region *moves relative to the body* (e.g., from a nose glow to a side-aspect motor/wing-root). An 8-bit AGC CVBS sensor like the FT640 will further pull the apparent centroid toward whatever saturates the AGC. So the *energy* aimpoint physically walks across the airframe through the engagement even with zero tracker error.
- **Aimpoint wander / scintillation.** Glint and signature scintillation are the IR analog of radar glint — "an apparent displacement of the target from its mean position," the same phenomenon as amplitude scintillation ([Scintillation (radar), Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scintillation_(radar))). This injects a *correlated, non-Gaussian* line-of-sight noise that does not average out and that grows in angular terms as 1/R near the end-game.

The governing consequence for us: our guidance is a **λ̇ (LOS-rate) nuller**, and LOS rate is the *time-derivative* of an angle measured to the aimpoint. Aimpoint walk and glint are therefore *rate* disturbances, amplified by differentiation, exactly when R is smallest and the 1.0–1.4 s "ten-tau" terminal wall leaves no time to recover. The literature's remedy is **aimpoint bias** — deliberately offsetting the commanded aimpoint from the raw centroid toward a stable, intercept-relevant body point ([US Patent H400, *Aimpoint bias for terminal homing*](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/H400); [dynamic energy-centroid locator, US 4,600,305](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4600305)). For a hit-to-kill closure, the right move once the target resolves is to *stop nulling the rate to the energy centroid* and instead null to a geometrically stable point (e.g., the leading-edge/nose along the velocity vector), with the offset estimated from the resolved shape. This is doctrine-compatible: the bias writes the *aimpoint*, which is a perception output, not a guidance gain — range still never scales the loop.

### 5.3 Appearance update vs. drift: the stability–plasticity dilemma

A resolved-stage template *must* adapt — the appearance is changing by construction (scale, aspect, signature). But every online update risks learning the wrong thing: occlude or partially clip the target and a naïvely-updated template absorbs background/decoy pixels and **drifts**, which in KCF "will lead to tracking drift or failure in subsequent frames" ([Scene-Aware Adaptive Updating, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5713650/)). This is the **stability–plasticity dilemma** named directly in that literature. The mature answers are all *conditional update*:

- **Confidence-gated learning rate.** Update the template fast when the per-frame match/PSR (peak-to-sidelobe ratio) is high, freeze it when low — adaptive learning rate plus occlusion detection ([Effective Visual Tracking, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5375719/); [improved KCF with occlusion handling, Hindawi](https://www.hindawi.com/journals/mpe/2018/6931020/)).
- **Anchored long-term + plastic short-term templates.** Keep the *lock-on* appearance as an immutable anchor and run a fast-adapting working template; demand agreement before committing. This is the design principle behind complementary-learner trackers ([Staple/complementary learners, NJU](https://mcg.nju.edu.cn/publication/2018/mmm18_lxz.pdf)).
- **Scene-aware update.** Classify the scene (sky / horizon / ground clutter) and pick the update policy accordingly ([Scene-Aware Adaptive Updating, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5713650/)). For us the scene class is *available almost for free* from the same region map our graduated CFAR already uses (k_horizon < k_sky < k_ground) — sky → aggressive update, horizon/ground → conservative.

Our current stack has **no online appearance model at all** — it tracks a CFAR blob centroid and coasts on constant velocity. That is robust by virtue of having nothing to corrupt, but it forfeits the resolved-stage discrimination that prevents pull-off and that keeps a stable aimpoint when the blob becomes a shape.

### 5.4 Anti-pull-off: holding the *committed* target against a hotter intruder

Pull-off is the failure where the tracker migrates from the true target onto a brighter, closer, or decoy source. Against a winged UAV the realistic pull-off attractors are: the horizon/sun-glint band, a hotter background hot-spot as we cross into ground clutter, or a deliberate decoy/flare. The seeker community's countermeasure is twofold and well documented: (1) **kinematic/track-history gating** — the adaptive track gate (ATG) detects decoy onset and re-acquires the *original* track rather than the brightest object ([NATO STO, *IR Countermeasure Techniques Against Imaging Seekers*](https://www.sto.nato.int/document/infrared-countermeasure-techniques-against-imaging-seekers/); [CCM for IIR seeker, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/311609492_Counter_countermeasure_method_for_missiles_imaging_infrared_seeker)); and (2) **morphological/spectral discrimination** — an imaging seeker "can distinguish between a localized, point source (a flare) and the distributed, complex radiation pattern of an aircraft," and two-color seekers use band-ratio to reject flares ([two-color CCM, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/243483847_Two-color_infrared_counter-countermeasure_based_on_the_signal_ratio_between_two_detection_bands_for_a_crossed-array_tracker)).

We are single-band uncooled, so band-ratio does **not** transfer. What transfers strongly is **kinematic continuity as the primary identity test**: a true target's image moves consistently with the IMM-predicted angular state; a decoy/glint separates or appears with inconsistent motion. Our stack already has the right organs for this — combined-cost anti-pull-off association, ORB+RANSAC homography MTI with N-of-M trajectory continuity, the multi-track manager, and IMM with an NIS "model-wrong" alarm and a covariance-sized gate. The decisive doctrine point: pull-off resistance must live in *association and gating*, and the AI/learned layer may only veto via lock-quality/engage-permission — it must never be allowed to re-point the centroid onto a "better-looking" object.

### 5.5 Coast, same-track re-acquisition, occlusion, and the field-of-regard wall

**Brief loss → coast.** When detection drops for a few frames (FFC freeze, momentary clutter merge, AGC transient), the right behavior is to *predict, not search* — propagate the angular state and keep guiding. This is exactly our FSM: LOCKED → PREDICTIVE_TRACK with a CV coast predictor on the last two observations, confidence decaying per coast frame (`track.py`). The long-term-tracking literature formalizes the same prediction/detection split ([TLD, *Long-Term Tracking Through Failure Cases*](https://cvssp.org/Personal/KarelLebeda/papers/VOT2013.pdf); [3 R's of long-term tracking: Re-detection, Recovery, Reliability](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12273)).

**Longer loss → re-acquire the SAME track.** Post-commit, re-detection must reattach to the *same* identity, never spawn a fresh one — a hard doctrine invariant here (WRONG-HIT is a HARD FAIL). The mechanism is an *expanding, covariance-driven search window centered on the prediction*, with re-association inside the gate, which is our REACQUIRE state with its gate-radius multiplier and reacquire budget before HARD_LOST. The key reliability metric from the literature is a **presence/confidence score** that gates re-detection and prevents premature reattachment to clutter ([Reliable Re-Detection for Long-Term Tracking](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323822506_Reliable_Re-Detection_for_Long-Term_Tracking); [performance methodology for long-term tracking](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.08675)).

**Occlusion.** True occlusion (cloud, terrain masking near the horizon) is just a *bounded coast* — but the coast budget must be set by the *predicted* time-out-of-view, not a fixed frame count, because at high closure the geometry behind an occluder changes fast. The literature's occlusion-detection-then-freeze pattern (freeze template, widen gate, demand confident re-detect) maps onto suspending template update during PREDICTIVE_TRACK/REACQUIRE.

**Field-of-regard exhaustion — the strapdown wall.** This is our unique, non-negotiable limit. We are **strapdown**: no gimbal, so the seeker FOV *is* the field of regard, and the achievable LOS-rate-nulling slew is capped by the airframe's ≤0.84 g lateral authority. A late, hard target jink near CPA can drive the required LOS rate beyond what 0.84 g can null inside the ~1.0–1.4 s ten-tau wall, walking the target toward the FOV edge until it leaves the array — an irrecoverable loss with no gimbal to chase it. Gimbaled seekers buy themselves field-of-regard margin precisely to survive this; we cannot. The only mitigations are (a) **predictive centering** — lead the body-pointing using the IMM angular-rate state so the target stays nearer frame-center, buying FOV margin before the jink; (b) **earliest-possible detector regime switch** so we never lose the resolved target to a collapsing top-hat at short range; and (c) honest acknowledgment that beyond a critical jink-vs-closure ratio the engagement is geometrically un-winnable and the correct output is ABORT (a PASS), never a guess.

### 5.6 The staged solution: re-parameterizing the target model across the engagement

The robust seeker does not run one tracker; it runs a **scheduled sequence of target models keyed to estimated angular scale**, with hysteresis at each boundary to prevent regime chatter, and a single persistent track identity carried through all of them:

1. **Point regime** (sub-pixel → few px): matched-filter/CFAR detection, centroid = aimpoint, energy-gated. *(Our current strength.)*
2. **Transition trigger**: `area_extended_px` rising / top-hat `area_px` collapsing → promote out of point regime. *(We have the cue; we must act on it.)*
3. **Resolved regime** (tens of px): segmentation + scale-adaptive appearance template, confidence-gated update, scene-aware (sky/horizon/ground) learning rate, **aimpoint bias** off the energy centroid to a stable body point feeding the λ̇ nuller.
4. **Fill/overfill regime**: drop whole-body centroid; track a chosen sub-feature/edge; freeze plasticity; hold aimpoint geometrically.
5. **Throughout**: IMM angular state + covariance gate as the identity backbone; FSM coast/REACQUIRE for losses; lock-quality score gating engage-permission; ABORT when geometry or FOV margin is exhausted.

### Transferable to our seeker

- **Make the scale transition a state, not a log line.** Promote `area_extended_px`-rising / top-hat-`area_px`-collapse into an explicit detector-regime switch (point → resolved) with hysteresis. This is the single highest-value missing transition; a collapsing top-hat at short range is a silent lock-loss waiting to happen ([modified top-hat band-pass behavior](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S004579061000056X)).
- **Adopt aimpoint bias for the resolved stage.** Once resolved, stop nulling λ̇ to the AGC energy centroid; bias the aimpoint to a stable body point (nose/leading edge along the closure vector). Bias is a perception output → doctrine-clean ([US H400](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4959714), [aimpoint bias patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/H400)).
- **Reuse the CFAR region map as a scene classifier** to set any future template-update learning rate (sky = plastic, horizon/ground = stable), inheriting scene-aware updating for free ([Scene-Aware Adaptive Updating](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5713650/)).
- **Add a presence/confidence score to govern REACQUIRE reattachment**, not just a frame-count budget — the long-term-tracking "reliability" metric prevents reattaching to clutter post-commit ([Reliable Re-Detection](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323822506_Reliable_Re-Detection_for_Long-Term_Tracking)).
- **Predictive frame-centering from the IMM rate state** to bank FOV margin before a terminal jink — the only strapdown substitute for gimbal field-of-regard.
- **Keep anti-pull-off purely kinematic** (combined-cost association + trajectory continuity + NIS gate); single-band can't band-ratio decoys, so motion consistency is our flare/glint test ([NATO STO IRCM](https://www.sto.nato.int/document/infrared-countermeasure-techniques-against-imaging-seekers/)).

### What we may have missed

- **No online appearance model in the resolved stage.** We coast a CFAR centroid; we have nothing that discriminates the held target from a same-kinematics-but-different-shape intruder once both resolve. A scale-adaptive, confidence-gated template (DSST/KCF-style PSR-gated) would add resolved-stage identity ([Scale-Adaptive KCF](https://www.hindawi.com/journals/mpe/2017/1605959/)).
- **Aimpoint-walk is unmodeled.** The energy centroid physically migrates with aspect and AGC; we currently treat the centroid as ground truth, injecting correlated λ̇ noise at exactly the worst moment. Glint/scintillation is non-Gaussian and does not average out ([Scintillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scintillation_(radar))).
- **Stability–plasticity is unaddressed because we don't adapt at all** — fine today, but it caps our resolved-stage and fill-stage performance; the moment we add a template we inherit the drift problem and need confidence-gated/anchored update ([stability–plasticity, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5713650/)).
- **Coast budget is frame-count, not geometry-driven.** At high closure the time-behind-occluder and time-to-FOV-edge scale with the engagement, not with a fixed frame count; budgets should be set from predicted out-of-view time.
- **No explicit fill/overfill (Stage-3) handler.** Whole-body centroid tracking has no defined behavior when the target clips the FOV near CPA — precisely the last second that sets miss distance; a sub-feature/edge aimpoint policy is undefined in the current stack.

## 6. Deep Problem — Maneuver, Background Transition & Aimpoint Stability

The first five seconds of an engagement are a detection problem; the last second is an estimation-under-stress problem. Once the seeker has declared LOCK, the science of TASK #1 becomes the science of *not losing what you already have* while three things change at once: the target's **kinematics** (it jinks), the target's **appearance** (it grows from a point to a resolved, flexing, glinting object), and the **background** behind it (cold sky → horizon → hot ground clutter). The hard part is that these are not three independent failures the stack can handle one at a time — they are correlated and they peak together in the terminal second, exactly when the time-to-go budget is too short to recover from a dropped track. This chapter treats the joint problem: maneuver-adaptive estimation, association and track memory through a clutter-crossing, and aimpoint stability in the endgame, and asks what a passive monocular uncooled-LWIR strapdown seeker on a Pi should borrow from the real terminal-seeker literature.

### 6.1 The joint association problem: kinematics and appearance changing together

Classical data association assumes one of the two descriptors is stable. Gating on kinematics (the IMM gate, sized by innovation covariance) assumes the target's *image* is recognizable frame-to-frame; appearance-template association assumes the target's *motion* is predictable. In TASK #1 both assumptions break in the same window. As the interceptor closes, the target's projected scale grows roughly as `s ∝ 1/R` and its *rate of growth* `ṡ/s = −Ṙ/R = 1/τ` where `τ = R/|Ṙ|` is time-to-go — so in the last `~10τ` the apparent size, the centroid-to-extremity offset, and the per-pixel intensity gradient all change fastest precisely as the kinematic uncertainty from a jink is also largest. A correlation tracker tuned on the lock-on template is matching against an object that no longer looks like its template; a kinematics-only tracker is gating on a prediction that a maneuver has just invalidated.

The defensible architecture, which our stack already partly implements as the **combined-cost anti-pull-off association**, is to keep the two cues but never let appearance drive the geometry. The lesson from multiple-hypothesis tracking (MHT) literature for infrared surveillance (Blackman; the IEEE *Multiple-hypothesis track confirmation for IR surveillance* line of work) is that the *association decision* should be deferred and probabilistic, but the *state estimate that guidance consumes* must come only from the kinematic filter. The combined cost should be a gate-and-rank, not a fused state: appearance similarity and a maneuver-aware kinematic Mahalanobis distance jointly *score* candidate measurements, but the winning measurement updates the IMM, and the IMM alone produces the LOS estimate. This is the doctrine invariant — learned/appearance signals write to lock-quality, never to centroid/LOS — expressed at the association layer. It is also why a single hard nearest-neighbor association is dangerous in the crossing: the right answer is to carry the ambiguity briefly (deferred decision) and let trajectory continuity resolve it, which is what the N-of-M continuity backstop and CV-coast in PREDICTIVE_TRACK are for.

### 6.2 Maneuver-adaptive estimation: CV / Singer / coordinated-turn in the IMM

A winged UAV is not a quad. Its maneuvers are *banked, coordinated turns* at roughly constant speed, not the near-holonomic acceleration steps of a multirotor. This matters for model selection. The three canonical models in the maneuvering-target-tracking (MTT) literature, and how they map onto our 4-state modified-polar angular filter `[az, el, aż, eḟ]`:

- **Constant-velocity (CV / nearly-constant-velocity).** White-noise acceleration; the quiescent model. In angular state this is constant LOS-rate. It is the correct baseline because between maneuvers a winged target's *angular* track is smooth, and CV gives the lowest-noise estimate when nothing is happening. This is our coast model.
- **Singer model.** Acceleration as a zero-mean, exponentially-correlated (first-order Markov) random process with correlation time `τ_m` and variance `σ_a²`; the discrete process-noise covariance is the classic Singer `Q(τ_m, σ_a, T)`. Singer (1970) and Berg's coordinated-turn-augmented variant (1983) are the standard references. Singer is the right "maneuver-on" model for a *target whose maneuver onset you cannot predict* — it widens the gate and inflates `Q` smoothly rather than committing to a turn direction. Characteristic numbers: `τ_m` of order 1–5 s for an aircraft-class target; for our ~0.84 g target the acceleration std is `σ_a ≈ 0.84·9.81 ≈ 8 m/s²`.
- **Coordinated-turn (CT), unknown turn-rate `ω`.** The physically *correct* model for a banking winged UAV. In Cartesian the nearly-constant-turn transition is the well-known nonlinear map with `sin(ωT)/ω` and `(1−cos ωT)/ω` terms on the velocity components and `ω` carried as an augmented state, requiring EKF/UKF linearization (Roth/Gustafsson, DIVA-portal *EKF/UKF Maneuvering Target Tracking using Coordinated Turn Models*). The CT model captures the *correlated, sustained* lateral acceleration of a turn, which Singer's white-ish jerk does not, so it holds the track tighter through a long bank with less lag.

The IMM runs these in parallel, mixes their estimates each step by the mode-transition Markov matrix, and reweights by each model's measurement likelihood `Λ_j = N(ν_j; 0, S_j)` — the Gaussian of that model's innovation `ν_j` under its innovation covariance `S_j`. The mode probability update `μ_j ∝ Λ_j Σ_i π_ij μ_i` is the entire adaptivity: when a coordinated turn begins, the CV innovation blows up, its `Λ` collapses, and probability mass flows to the CT/Singer mode within a few frames — *no explicit maneuver decision is needed*. The **NIS model-wrong alarm** our stack already runs is the scalar `ε = νᵀ S⁻¹ ν` compared to a `χ²` threshold (e.g. `χ²₂,₀.₉₉ ≈ 9.2` for a 2-DOF angular innovation); a sustained NIS exceedance is the divergence/maneuver detector that should *widen the gate before* the mode probabilities have fully switched. The transferable subtlety: the IMM's value here is not better steady-state accuracy — it is **automatic gate widening at maneuver onset**, which is exactly what prevents the post-jink gate from excluding the true measurement and dropping the track.

**What transfers, what doesn't.** The full Cartesian CT model needs range to convert turn-rate to angular rate — and we have no range. The transferable form is an *angular* IMM: CV (constant LOS-rate) ⊕ a "maneuver" mode that is simply CV with inflated `Q` (a Singer-like white-jerk in `[az̈, eë]`), plus optionally an angular-acceleration mode. A literal coordinated-turn-in-angle model is weakly observable on a strapdown monocular seeker because aspect and bank are confounded with range; better to spend the model budget on a well-tuned Singer-`Q` maneuver mode and lean on fast gate adaptation than on a CT model whose `ω` we cannot observe cleanly.

### 6.3 Track memory through a clutter-crossing

The background transition is where tracks die. The MHT/track-before-detect (TBD) literature exists precisely for the case where the target's measurement momentarily merges with, or is masked by, clutter — the horizon line, a hot cloud edge, a ground hot-spot the target overflies. The relevant published results: MM-PHD and labeled-RFS TBD for "infrared maneuvering dim multi-target" show that **deferring the detection decision and integrating likelihood over a few frames** lets a track survive a gap that a frame-by-frame detector would break (ScienceDirect, *Track-before-detect for IR maneuvering dim multi-target via MM-PHD*; *Labeled RFS-Based TBD in the IR FPA*). Q-IMM-MHT work specifically targets "small and weak multiple targets under dynamic interference" — the crossing case where trajectories overlap on the focal plane and naive association swaps identities.

For our single-target seeker the transferable mechanism is *not* full MHT — that is too heavy for a 17 ms/frame Pi budget and we deliberately run a single-target lock FSM. The transferable mechanism is the **coast-and-reacquire policy with a covariance-sized gate**, which is a degenerate, computationally trivial MHT: when the measurement vanishes into clutter, PREDICTIVE_TRACK coasts on the CV model, the covariance `P` grows by the process noise each frame, the gate `g = √(χ² · S)` grows with it, and REACQUIRE accepts a re-detection inside that growing gate. The N-of-M trajectory-continuity test is the deferred-decision confirmation that prevents the coast from locking onto a clutter spike that happens to fall in the gate. Two design numbers govern survival: the **maximum coast horizon** (how many frames `P` can grow before the gate is so large it admits clutter — for a maneuvering target this is short, of order the ratio of maneuver-induced position uncertainty to clutter spacing) and the **HARD_LOST timeout**. The honest failure mode to call out: a *coordinated maneuver executed inside the clutter-crossing* defeats CV coast, because the prediction the gate is centered on is wrong by the maneuver and the true measurement re-emerges outside the gate. This is the single most dangerous joint event in TASK #1.

### 6.4 The background classifier feeds lock-quality, not the tracker

The region-adaptive IRST literature is directly transferable and validates our existing design. Kim & co. (*Small Infrared Target Detection by Region-Adaptive Clutter Rejection*, Sensors 2014, PMC4168490) segment the scene into **sky / horizon / sea(ground)** by horizon fitting (RANSAC + SVD on the horizon line) and apply *different* clutter rejection per region: a hysteresis-CFAR with **region-dependent thresholds `k_horizon < k_sky < k_sea`**, a directional 1-D median background-removal filter on the horizon band, an AdaBoost spatial-attribute classifier (eight features: std-dev, ranked-fill-ratio, second moment, area, size-ratio, rotational size variation, frequency energy, mean distance) to reject cloud clutter in the sky, and a temporal-consistency filter against sea/sun-glint. Reported: ~88.8% detection at 8.7% false alarm for the sky classifier, ~97.7% glint rejection by temporal consistency, overall false alarms cut by 2.5–9.4× at 0.1–0.8% detection-rate cost.

Our stack already mirrors this with **graduated region-classified CFAR `k_horizon < k_sky < k_ground`**, MPCM local contrast, and directional max-median suppression — the survey confirms the ordering of thresholds and the per-region split is the right structure. The doctrine-critical point, and the one the literature does *not* always honor, is **where the classifier's output is allowed to act**. In our system the background classifier writes only to **lock-quality / engage-permission**, never to the centroid or LOS. This is correct and should be defended: a scene classifier that down-weighted or moved the centroid when it "believes" the target is in clutter would inject a background-correlated bias into the aimpoint — precisely the kind of error that produces a confident wrong-hit (a HARD FAIL under our doctrine). The classifier's job is to tell the FSM *"detections are less trustworthy here, raise the continuity bar and lower engage-permission,"* not to edit the geometry. The transferable rule: **scene understanding modulates trust and gating; it never modulates the estimate guidance consumes.**

### 6.5 Aimpoint stability in the endgame

This is the terminal-second problem the real seeker literature is most explicit about. Sources of aimpoint wander, in rough order of endgame importance for a passive imaging seeker:

- **Glint / angle scintillation.** As the target resolves, the apparent thermal centroid hops between dominant hot features (motor, exhaust plume, leading-edge aero-heating) as aspect rotates head-on → beam. Radar glint theory (Howard; Borden) gives the scaling that transfers directly to a thermal imager: the apparent-aimpoint angular wander has standard deviation of order `σ_glint ≈ k · L / R` where `L` is the target's physical span and `R` the range — so **glint error grows as range closes**, the opposite of receiver noise, and *dominates in the last fraction of a second*. Critically its distribution is **heavy-tailed (non-Gaussian, Student-t/Laplacian-like)**, with occasional excursions far outside the centroid — a Gaussian `R` in the filter under-weights these and lets a glint spike pull the aimpoint. The transferable countermeasure: a **robust innovation** (Huber/clipped update, or NIS-gated rejection of single-frame jumps inconsistent with the kinematic state) so a one-frame glint excursion cannot move the commanded LOS. This is the imaging analog of radar frequency-agility glint averaging.
- **Target flexing / aspect change.** A winged UAV's projected silhouette and thermal hot-spot move *relative to its center of mass* as it banks. The centroid of the thermal blob is therefore not a fixed body point — chasing it injects a maneuver-correlated aimpoint oscillation. The terminal-seeker answer (segmentation-aimpoint patents, e.g. US 4,959,714; JSOW-C / IIR-seeker practice) is **aimpoint selection from a segmented silhouette plus temporal smoothing of the aimpoint *offset***: pick a stable body reference (e.g. the leading hot-spot or the geometric centroid of the segmented planform) and low-pass the offset between track-point and aimpoint, so the commanded point tracks the body, not the flickering hot pixel.
- **λ̇ measurement noise into a high-gain loop.** Our guidance is a LOS-rate (`λ̇`) nuller. In the endgame, `λ̇` noise is the direct driver of miss. The homing-guidance literature (Palumbo & Blauwkamp, *Basic Principles of Homing Guidance*, JHU/APL Tech Digest) frames the unavoidable tension: the homing loop must have enough bandwidth to follow a real target maneuver but low enough bandwidth to reject glint/noise, and these fight. They also formalize the **radome/boresight-error analog** as an *effective navigation ratio* `Ñ(r) = N / (1 + r·N·V_c/v_M)` where a slope error `r` in the angle-measurement-vs-look-angle relation degrades the loop — for us the analog is any look-angle-dependent bias in the strapdown angle estimate (AGC shading, gyro de-rotation error), which similarly de-tunes the nuller.

**Smooth without lagging a real maneuver.** The core endgame trade is: filter `λ̇` hard enough to kill glint, and you add lag that lets a genuine terminal jink open the miss; filter lightly, and glint walks the aimpoint. The principled resolutions, all transferable:

1. **Let the IMM/NIS, not a fixed low-pass, set the smoothing.** When NIS is quiescent, smooth `λ̇` aggressively (long effective time constant); when NIS alarms (real maneuver), shorten the time constant / widen `Q` so the loop follows. This is adaptive bandwidth keyed to the *innovation*, which is the only honest discriminator between "noise" and "maneuver."
2. **Robust/clipped innovation** so glint tails cannot pass, while consistent multi-frame motion (a maneuver) does.
3. **Predictive-gate sizing by `S = HPHᵀ + R`**, not a fixed pixel radius — the gate is already covariance-sized in our IMM, which is exactly right: it is wide after a maneuver (high `P`) and tight when coasting (low `P`).
4. **Accept the `10τ` floor.** Below `~1.0–1.4 s` time-to-go (our "ten-tau" wall), the loop can no longer correct a new maneuver — the physics says commit the aimpoint and stop chasing late innovations, because chasing glint inside the floor *adds* miss. Real seekers freeze or heavily damp the aimpoint in the final fraction of a second for exactly this reason.

### Transferable to our seeker
- **Adaptive `λ̇` smoothing keyed to NIS**, not a fixed low-pass: smooth hard when innovations are quiescent (kills glint), shorten the time constant the instant the NIS model-wrong alarm fires (follows a real jink) — directly resolves the smooth-vs-lag trade without a new sensor.
- **Robust (Huber/clipped or NIS-gated) innovation update** so a single-frame glint or aspect-flip excursion — which is heavy-tailed, not Gaussian — cannot pull the commanded LOS; consistent multi-frame motion still passes.
- **Aimpoint = smoothed offset from a stable segmented body reference** (leading hot-spot / planform centroid), low-passed separately from the track point, so flexing and motor-aspect hot-spot migration don't oscillate the hit-point as the target resolves.
- **Tune the IMM maneuver mode as an angular Singer-`Q`** sized to the 0.84 g / `τ_m≈1–3 s` target, and treat the IMM's real payoff as *automatic gate widening at maneuver onset*, not steady-state accuracy.
- **Keep the background classifier strictly on lock-quality/engage-permission** (it already is) — confirm no code path lets scene class touch centroid/LOS; this is the wrong-hit firewall.
- **Freeze/heavily-damp the aimpoint inside the 10-τ wall** as an explicit terminal state: below ~1.0–1.4 s, late innovations can only add miss, so stop chasing them.

### What we may have missed
- **No explicit endgame robust-loss on the innovation.** A χ²/Mahalanobis *gate* rejects whole measurements, but a Huber-style *down-weighting* of in-gate-but-tail innovations is the standard glint defense and is not obviously in the stack.
- **No deferred-decision / 2-best track memory through a crossing.** Our coast is single-hypothesis CV; a one-step "two surviving hypotheses through the clutter band, resolved by N-of-M continuity" would cheaply approximate MHT for the single most dangerous joint event — a *maneuver hidden inside a clutter-crossing*, which CV coast cannot survive.
- **CV-coast prediction is maneuver-blind during occlusion.** Coasting on the *last IMM mixed estimate* (carrying a maneuver-mode velocity/turn bias) rather than pure CV would re-center the reacquire gate better when the target jinks *while* masked by clutter.
- **Aimpoint-offset dynamics from aspect rotation appear unmodeled.** The head-on→beam motor/exhaust hot-spot migration is a *predictable, aspect-correlated* bias, not random noise; a small aspect-aware offset model could pre-empt the centroid hop instead of filtering it after the fact.
- **No explicit glint/scale-coupled `R` inflation.** Measurement noise `R` on the angle should be *inflated as scale grows* (glint ∝ L/R rises into the endgame); a fixed `R` over-trusts the centroid exactly when glint peaks.
- **Bandwidth-vs-NETD margin near `10τ` may be unbudgeted.** The Palumbo loop-bandwidth-vs-noise trade implies a hard limit on how fast the nuller can both follow a terminal jink and reject 50 mK NETD jitter; an explicit endgame noise/bandwidth budget would tell us whether the 1.0–1.4 s wall is set by physics or is conservative.

## 7. Deep Problem — Multi-Modal Fusion to Defeat Track-Loss (worth it on SWaP?)

The persistence problem in TASK #1 is not, at root, a detection problem — our cascade (top-hat + percentile/MAD + region-adaptive CFAR + MPCM + directional max-median) already pulls a winged UAV out of cold sky and off the horizon. It is a *continuity-under-transformation* problem: the same physical target presents as a sub-pixel point against cold sky, then a resolved cruciform against horizon clutter, then a signature that rotates head-on→beam while the motor/exhaust hot-spot waxes and wanes with aspect, all while the background slides from −40 °C sky through a thermally cluttered horizon to hot ground. A single uncooled LWIR channel must survive every one of those transitions on one statistical description of "target." Fusion is the hypothesis that a *second physical channel*, failing on *different* transitions, can keep the track alive across the union of the gaps. The question this chapter answers is which second channel, if any, buys enough persistence to justify the SWaP, latency, and — critically — the doctrinal risk of letting another sensor near the LOS, on a Pi-class strapdown interceptor.

### 7.1 The decorrelation argument — the only reason fusion is worth anything

The entire scientific case for fusion reduces to one inequality. If two modalities lose the track independently, with per-frame loss probabilities $p_1$ and $p_2$, the fused track survives unless both fail simultaneously: $P_{\text{fused-loss}} = p_1 p_2$ *only if the failures are statistically independent*. Two LWIR channels with the same NETD looking at the same thermal-crossover event are not independent — $\rho \to 1$ and fusion buys nothing. The persistence dividend is real only to the degree the *failure modes decorrelate*. So the correct way to rank fusion candidates is not by raw accuracy but by the **failure-mode orthogonality** against our known LWIR loss events:

- **Thermal crossover / aspect null** — target signature drops below CFAR threshold (head-on motor occluded, ΔT→0 against a sun-warmed horizon). An *event camera fails identically* (no intensity change → no events when target and seeker are momentarily co-moving), but *RGB texture* and *radar/ToF return* are fully decorrelated — they don't care about ΔT.
- **Background transition (sky→horizon→ground clutter)** — our hardest loss mode; CFAR region statistics whipsaw. *RGB* decorrelates partially (structure persists, but ground clutter is also textured); *event camera decorrelates strongly* if the target's image motion differs from the background's optical flow (it does, because the target maneuvers and we de-rotate ego-motion); *radar/ToF* decorrelates completely (clutter rejection is in range/Doppler, an orthogonal axis).
- **Scale explosion + motion blur in the last ~1 s** — target goes point→resolved→fills-FOV at high LOS rate; our 17 ms/frame cadence under-samples the angular slew and the bolometer's ~10–15 ms thermal time constant smears it. This is where an **event camera's microsecond temporal resolution and motion-blur immunity** is genuinely decorrelated and most valuable.

This is the same logic that drives every multimode missile seeker in the open literature: Kongsberg's NSM combines imaging-IR and a passive RF/MMW channel specifically so radar jamming and IR decoys are *orthogonal* attacks ([Kongsberg NSM dual-mode seeker, public](https://d3isj3hu8683a4.cloudfront.net/military/offense/missiles-technology/missile-terminal-guidance-overview/)); dual-band IR seekers fuse two IR bands and two FOVs to "enrich the information difference between target and background" precisely because single-band-single-FOV loses lock on aspect/background transitions ([System Design of an Infrared Dual-band Seeker, IOP](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1757-899X/711/1/012092/pdf)). The transferable lesson: **fusion is bought for decorrelation, not accuracy** — and the modality that decorrelates against *our specific worst loss mode (the last-second high-LOS-rate background transition)* wins, even if it is a worse sensor in isolation.

### 7.2 The estimation framework — measurement-level vs track-level, and where our IMM sits

Two architectures exist, and the choice is forced by SWaP and by our doctrine, not by accuracy.

**Centralized / measurement-level fusion** feeds raw measurements from every sensor into one filter. It is provably optimal in MMSE terms when all sensors are healthy and cross-correlations are tracked ([centralized IMM/Kalman fusion, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331080915)). For us that means feeding an event-derived LOS measurement *and* an IR centroid into the *same* IMM update step. It is the most accurate and the most fragile: a single corrupted sensor (an event-camera flood under ego-motion) poisons the shared innovation, and it demands tight time-sync between asynchronous (event) and framed (IR) streams.

**Federated / track-level fusion** runs a local filter per modality and fuses the *tracks* (state estimates + covariances), classically with the federated Kalman filter's information-sharing and covariance-upper-bound to keep the locals decorrelated ([federated vs centralized fusion, IEEE AESS track-to-track](https://ieee-aess.org/presentation/introduction-track-track-fusion-and-distributed-kalman-filter)). It is sub-optimal when everything is healthy but **fault-tolerant**: a faulty local track can be detected by its own NIS/innovation and *gated out* before it contaminates the global state — distributed/weighted-robust Kalman filters are documented to out-survive the centralized filter under sensor faults ([multi-sensor fusion fault tolerance, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0005109804000287)).

For our interceptor the decision is made by the doctrine, not the math. **The invariant that learned/AI and any new sensor may write ONLY to lock-quality/engage-permission and NEVER to centroid/LOS/tracker forbids a naive centralized fuser** that lets a radar or RGB measurement steer the LOS directly. The architecture that respects this is **track-level / federated, with the IR-derived modified-polar IMM as the master LOS authority**, and the other modalities entering as:

1. **Independent local tracks whose agreement raises lock-quality** (RGB or event track confirms the IR track → confidence up; disagreement → REACQUIRE, never silent hand-off). This is fail-closed and invariant-safe by construction.
2. **A gated observability injection** (the ranger), discussed in §7.5, allowed to touch the *range* state only behind a fail-closed endgame gate — and *even then* range never scales guidance gain (our lambda-dot nuller is range-free by design; range is for the engage/fuze decision only).

This is the cleanest mapping of the published fusion taxonomy onto our constraints: **measurement-level for sensors we trust to be the LOS authority (IR only); track-level/federated for everything else, with consensus → lock-quality, disagreement → fail-closed.** Our existing IMM (with NIS model-wrong alarm and covariance-sized gate) is *already* the local IR filter; fusion adds sibling locals and a consensus rule, it does not replace the master.

### 7.3 IR + Event camera (DVS) — the highest persistence-per-cost candidate

The event camera is the modality most aligned with our worst failure mode. A DVS pixel fires asynchronously on log-intensity change with ~µs latency and effective temporal resolution equivalent to >10 kHz framing, with intrinsic HDR and **motion-blur immunity** ([DVS high-speed tracking survey](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/dynamic-vision-sensors-dvs)). For lambda-dot guidance this is decisive: our LOS-rate estimate is differentiated from a 17 ms-cadence angular track, so $\dot\lambda$ noise scales as $\sigma_\theta/\Delta t$ and *blows up* exactly in the last second when $\dot\lambda$ matters most and the bolometer smears the target. An event stream yields $\dot\lambda$ from the *event flow itself* — motion is the native measurement — giving a clean, low-latency LOS-rate and natural motion segmentation of a maneuvering target against clutter. The military precedent is exact and recent: **DARPA FENCE** put event-based readout *in the IR band* (our band), and Raytheon's public demo tracked aircraft and "resolved rapid motion that conventional infrared cameras often miss" at **<1.5 W with sensor-level sparsity** ([Raytheon FENCE MWIR, Military Aerospace](https://www.militaryaerospace.com/sensors/article/55371155/); [DARPA FENCE program page](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/fast-event-based-neuromorphic-camera-and-electronics)) — 100× less data, 100× lower latency, 100× lower power than a framing FPA in sparse scenes.

**What transfers:** the *algorithmic idea* — derive LOS-rate and motion segmentation from temporal contrast, not from differencing framed centroids — and the architectural role (event track as a federated local that sharpens $\dot\lambda$ and votes on lock-quality).

**What does not transfer, honestly:** (1) FENCE is an *IR* event FPA and is not a buyable part — commercial DVS (Prophesee/iniVation) are **silicon visible-band**, so a bolt-on event camera is an *RGB-band* sensor that goes blind at night, defeating the whole passive-LWIR premise. (2) **Ego-motion floods.** A strapdown DVS on a maneuvering interceptor is the documented worst case: "whenever the platform shifts, the DVS pixels pick up reflectance changes and produce output even when the scene is static," occluding the salient target ([ego-motion problem, arXiv 2303.14114](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.14114)). Event rates run $10^4$–$10^6$ ev/s and saturate the bus under ego-motion ([DVS noise/bandwidth, Tangram Vision](https://www.tangramvision.com/blog/sensoria-obscura-event-cameras-part-i)); our Pi 5 USB/CSI budget and 17 ms loop cannot absorb a flood. We *do* have a mitigant the literature lacks — **gyro de-rotation** already in our stack — which lets us subtract predicted ego-flow and keep only target-relative events (object-motion-sensitivity filtering). **Verdict: highest persistence-per-cost *only if* an LWIR-band event sensor existed; with a visible DVS it is a daytime-only sharpener and a SWaP/bandwidth risk we likely cannot afford on a Pi 5.** The honest near-term action is to *borrow the algorithm* — a temporal-contrast / time-surface stage on the existing IR frames to estimate $\dot\lambda$ between samples — not the hardware.

### 7.4 IR + RGB — cheap, decorrelates on clutter, dies at night

A visible camera is the cheapest co-bore-sighted second channel (a second Pi camera, grams, <1 W). RGB contributes **structure and texture** for ID and for clutter rejection during the horizon/ground transition where IR CFAR statistics break — multispectral IR+RGB fusion is the standard counter-UAS answer to thermal crossover ([IR/RGB UAV fusion DEPFusion, arXiv 2509.07327](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.07327); [Counter-UAS multi-sensor fusion, drone-warfare.com](https://drone-warfare.com/counter-uas/multi-sensor-fusion/)). **What transfers:** an RGB local track that votes on lock-quality and disambiguates target-vs-clutter when IR is confused — strictly behind the invariant (RGB raises/lowers confidence, never moves the LOS). **What does not:** RGB is useless at night/low-light (its decorrelation collapses to zero exactly when the mission is hardest), it needs co-registration and parallax handling, and against a small low-contrast winged UAV at range it adds little the IR doesn't already have. **Verdict: cheap and SWaP-trivial, but its persistence dividend is conditional on daylight; acceptable only as a confidence-voter, never a LOS source. Lower priority than the event-*algorithm*.**

### 7.5 IR + tiny radar / single-point ToF-LIDAR — the only modality that buys *observability*

Everything above sharpens or confirms an *angular* track. Radar and ToF/LIDAR are categorically different: they make **range and closing-rate observable**, which our passive monocular geometry fundamentally cannot ([range/closing-velocity unobservable in passive bearings-only; cf. active vs passive terminal guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_guidance)). Multimode seekers add an active end-game channel for exactly this — "active optical terminal homing provides the needed end-game centroid tracking and range when integrated with a passive seeker" ([AOTH patent, USPTO 4383663](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4383663)). A single-point ToF/LIDAR ($R=\tfrac{1}{2}c\tau$) like a TFmini-class module is grams and watts and gives clean range inside ~10–12 m; tiny FMCW radar gives range+Doppler through obscurants and decorrelates from *every* optical failure mode.

**But this is where our doctrine is loudest.** Range must NEVER scale guidance gain (the lambda-dot nuller is deliberately range-free), and a wrong-hit is a HARD FAIL. So a ranger is admissible *only* as a fail-closed **endgame gate**: it does not enter the LOS solution at all; it gates *engage-permission / fuzing* in the last ~1–1.4 s "ten-tau" wall, where range/closing-rate decides the hit-point/CPA decision and nothing else. This matches how proximity fuzing is partitioned in real interceptors — the ranger fires the decision, it does not steer the missile ([proximity fuzing, Excelitas](https://www.excelitas.com/product/proximity-fuzing); [dual-mode APKWS-II proximity-fuzed C-UAS, 2026](https://www.armyrecognition.com/news/aerospace-news/2026/u-s-air-force-approves-145m-dual-mode-apkws-ii-air-to-air-rocket-to-counter-drone-swarms)). **What transfers:** range observability *strictly behind the gate*, fail-closed (no return → no engage-permission, default-deny — an abort is a PASS). **What does not:** active emission risks counter-detection; LIDAR range is too short (~10 m) to help acquisition and only serves the terminal CPA decision; radar adds the most SWaP/cost/integration of any option. **Verdict: the only modality that adds a new state our physics denies us — but its value is confined to the endgame engage/fuze decision, and it must be architecturally walled off from the LOS by the fail-closed gate. Highest *unique-capability* value, narrowest scope.**

### 7.6 IMU-aided everything — already partly ours, nearly free

The IMU is not a target sensor but it is the cheapest persistence multiplier: gyro de-rotation (we have it) converts body-frame image motion to inertial LOS so the IMM tracks *target* motion, not airframe motion; it is also what makes a strapdown event camera survivable (§7.3) and what lets the CV-coast in our REACQUIRE state predict where the target re-emerges after an occlusion. **What transfers / what we may extend:** tighter IMU-to-IMM coupling (feed-forward angular acceleration into the model set, not just de-rotation) buys coast accuracy through blackout at essentially zero SWaP. This is the highest persistence-per-gram lever on the board and it is *already in our stack* — the lesson is to lean on it harder before adding any new sensor.

### 7.7 Ranking — persistence-gained-per-cost on a Pi-class interceptor

1. **IMU-aided coupling (own the modality we have).** Near-zero SWaP, decorrelates ego-motion from target motion, extends coast. Do first.
2. **Event-derived LOS-rate as an *algorithm* on existing IR frames** (time-surface/temporal-contrast $\dot\lambda$). Captures the FENCE lesson — clean low-latency $\dot\lambda$, blur immunity — with zero new hardware and no bus-flood risk. The hardware DVS is *not* recommended on a Pi 5 until an LWIR-band event FPA is buyable.
3. **Single-point ToF/LIDAR behind a fail-closed endgame gate.** Adds the one thing physics denies us (range/closing-rate) for the CPA/engage decision only; cheap, walled off from the LOS by doctrine. Add when the terminal-fuze decision needs hardening.
4. **RGB confidence-voter.** SWaP-trivial but daylight-only and adds little to a small-target IR track; lock-quality vote only.
5. **Tiny FMCW radar.** Best decorrelation and through-obscurant range, but highest cost/SWaP/integration and emission risk; reserve for a heavier variant, never the Pi-class baseline.

### Transferable to our seeker
- **Adopt the FENCE event-camera *algorithm*, not the hardware**: add a temporal-contrast / time-surface stage on the existing FT640 frames to estimate inter-frame LOS-rate, attacking the $\dot\lambda \propto \sigma_\theta/\Delta t$ blow-up in the last second without a new sensor or bus-flood risk ([Raytheon FENCE](https://www.militaryaerospace.com/sensors/article/55371155/)).
- **Architect all fusion as federated/track-level with the IR-IMM as sole LOS authority**: sibling RGB/event/IMU locals vote on lock-quality, agreement raises confidence, disagreement forces REACQUIRE — never a silent hand-off ([track-to-track fusion, IEEE AESS](https://ieee-aess.org/presentation/introduction-track-track-fusion-and-distributed-kalman-filter)).
- **Tighten IMU→IMM coupling** (feed-forward angular acceleration into the model set) to extend CV-coast through blackout/occlusion at near-zero SWaP — the best persistence-per-gram lever, already in-stack.
- **Admit a single-point ToF/LIDAR only as a fail-closed endgame engage/fuze gate**: range never enters the LOS or scales gain, no-return → no engage-permission (default-deny, abort-is-a-PASS), mirroring active-optical terminal homing partitioning ([AOTH, USPTO](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4383663)).
- **Use gyro de-rotation as the enabling layer for any motion-based modality** — it is what makes a strapdown event/optical-flow channel survivable against the documented ego-motion flood ([ego-motion problem, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.14114)).
- **Rank every fusion candidate by failure-mode orthogonality to our specific worst loss mode** (last-second high-LOS-rate background transition), not by standalone accuracy — the decorrelation inequality $P_{\text{loss}}=p_1p_2$ is the only payoff.

### What we may have missed
- **The LOS-rate-from-motion idea itself** — our $\dot\lambda$ comes from differentiating a 17 ms framed track; we have not exploited *temporal-contrast/optical-flow* as a direct, blur-immune $\dot\lambda$ measurement even on the IR frames we already have. This is the single biggest unforced lever.
- **An explicit cross-modality consensus/disagreement rule wired to lock-quality** — our FSM has LOCKED/PREDICTIVE/REACQUIRE/HARD_LOST but no notion of "two independent channels disagree → distrust the lock," which is the fail-closed safety the decorrelation argument is supposed to buy.
- **Range observability for the CPA/aimpoint decision** — we have correctly kept guidance range-free, but we may be leaving the *terminal engage/fuze timing* under-determined; a gated single-point ranger is the doctrine-clean way to harden the hit-point decision without touching the LOS.
- **IMU feed-forward of angular acceleration into the IMM model set** (not just de-rotation) for blackout coast — a near-free persistence gain we likely under-use.
- **Honest bus/compute budgeting for any asynchronous sensor** — the DVS bandwidth-saturation and BA-noise literature warns that a naive event stream can exceed a Pi 5's I/O under ego-motion ([DVS bandwidth/noise](https://www.tangramvision.com/blog/sensoria-obscura-event-cameras-part-i)); our trade studies should treat I/O saturation as a first-class failure mode, not an afterthought.

## 8. Novel & Ideal — How to Do Track-to-Intercept Better Than Anyone

### 8.0 The thesis

Our current stack is a *good frame-based seeker*: it detects, associates, locks, and coasts on 30 Hz CVBS frames with a clever cascade of clutter filters and an IMM angular tracker. The deep limitation is structural, not parametric. We are doing **synchronous, frame-quantized, intensity-domain inference on an AGC-corrupted 8-bit stream**, and then asking an IMM to recover smooth λ̇ from that. The image we feed it is the single hardest thing in Task #1: across the last ~1.4 s "ten-tau" wall, scale grows by an order of magnitude, aspect rotates head-on→beam, the apparent thermal signature swings with motor/exhaust aspect, and the background slides cold-sky→horizon→hot-clutter. Every one of those is an *intensity-domain* perturbation that an intensity-domain tracker must fight. The "ideal" architecture inverts the problem: **sense and guide on the quantities that are invariant to all four of those nuisances — motion (λ̇) and looming (τ) — and treat appearance only as a slow, self-corrected prior, never as the guidance signal.** Below I build that pipeline end-to-end, justify each block with physics, and show it is *more* doctrine-compatible than what we have, not less.

### 8.1 Event-based (neuromorphic) sensing — the single highest-leverage change

A Dynamic Vision Sensor (DVS / ATIS / Prophesee IMX636 class) does not output frames. Each pixel independently fires a timestamped event when its **log-intensity** crosses a threshold: e_k = (x, y, t, p), with t at microsecond resolution. Three properties map almost perfectly onto Task #1:

1. **Log-intensity differencing is its own AGC-invariance and its own background-subtraction.** The DVS responds to *contrast change*, ∂(log I)/∂t > C. A static cold-sky or static hot-ground patch produces *no events* — the background literally does not exist in the data stream until it moves relative to the sensor. Our entire B1 cascade (region-CFAR, MPCM, directional max-median, top-hat) is machinery for *suppressing static structured background in an intensity frame*. A DVS suppresses it in silicon, for free, and the log operation makes it invariant to the multiplicative AGC gain that corrupts our 8-bit CVBS — the exact nuisance that makes our percentile/MAD thresholds drift as the scene transitions. This is the most important sentence in the chapter: **the four hardest nuisances in Task #1 (AGC, background transition, signature change, scale growth) are precisely the things a log-contrast motion sensor is blind to.**

2. **Microsecond latency feeds λ̇ directly.** Open literature on neuromorphic obstacle avoidance reports end-to-end sense-to-angle latencies of ~0.25 ms on neuromorphic hardware and ~3.5 ms full-loop for high-speed obstacle reaction (ColibriUAV, UZH RPG event-vision work; DARPA FENCE program explicitly cites "smarter missile guidance" and elimination of motion blur as goals — [interestingengineering](https://interestingengineering.com/military/infrared-camera-real-time-tracking-threats), [ColibriUAV arXiv 2305.18371](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18371), [UZH RPG event vision](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/research_dvs.html)). Our 17 ms frame pipeline is a *λ̇ quantizer*: we estimate an angular rate by differencing centroids 17 ms apart, so our λ̇ noise floor is set by (centroid jitter)/(17 ms). On the terminal wall where λ̇ is changing fastest and matters most, that latency *is* the miss-distance budget. λ̇ from a continuous event stream is differentiated against a microsecond clock — the rate signal is fundamentally cleaner and phase-lead-free, which directly attacks the dominant terminal miss-distance term.

3. **Motion blur immunity at closure.** As the target fills the FOV and angular rates spike, a frame camera smears; a DVS sharpens (more events, still individually sharp). This is exactly backwards from our failure mode.

**What does NOT transfer / the honest cost.** A DVS is a *different sensor* — this is a hardware bet, not a software change, and it breaks LOBL-on-the-Foxeer. DVS gives no absolute radiometry and no DC appearance, so target *recognition* (is this the winged UAV vs. a bird vs. a thermal of a road) is weaker — you must keep a low-rate intensity channel for ID and engage-permission (an ATIS or a hybrid frame+event sensor, as in ColibriUAV's dual-stream design). Uncooled-LWIR DVS specifically is immature and the slow thermal time-constant of a microbolometer fights the DVS's speed advantage (the pixel can't change temperature in microseconds). **Transferable lesson, deployable now without new hardware:** emulate the DVS's invariance in software — run our tracker on a **temporal log-difference / "synthetic events" channel** (per-pixel log-intensity change between de-rotated frames) in parallel with the intensity channel, and feed λ̇ preferentially from the motion channel. That captures the AGC-invariance and background-subtraction benefits on the FT640 we already own. This is the cheapest high-value experiment in the whole study.

### 8.2 Bio-inspired guidance — falcons already validated our doctrine; dragonflies extend it

Two results from the open biology literature are directly load-bearing.

**Falcons run proportional navigation.** Brighton, Thomas & Taylor (PNAS 2017) GPS-tracked peregrines intercepting towed targets and showed terminal trajectories are *quantitatively* fit by the PN guidance law of missiles, but at a **low navigation constant, median N ≈ 2.6**, below the textbook 3–5 ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1714532114); gyrfalcon follow-up finds the same law at even lower gain — [PubMed 33536303](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33536303/)). The transferable lesson is subtle and reassuring: a 350-million-year-evolved interceptor uses *commanded turn rate ∝ λ̇*, with the constant tuned **low for a low-speed, rate-limited airframe** — which is exactly our regime (≤0.84 g, strapdown, modest closing speed). Our λ̇-nuller is in the right family. The refinement biology suggests: the effective gain should be *airframe-matched and conservative*, and falcons add a feed-forward "lock-on" phase before the PN terminal phase — mirroring our planned A1 ACQUIRE-then-PN state. Nature votes for the architecture we already chose.

**Dragonflies add internal forward models and motion camouflage.** Dragonflies intercept by holding the prey on a **constant bearing** (motion camouflage — they null λ̇ so completely that they appear stationary to the prey), and crucially they do it using an **internal predictive model of both the prey's motion and the reafferent effect of their own body movement** ([Mizutani/Chahl motion camouflage in dragonflies, Nature 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12789327/); [dragonfly interception heuristics](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315774902)). Two transfers: (a) constant-bearing/λ̇-null *is* motion camouflage, so our guidance law has an emergent counter-detection benefit, free; (b) the dragonfly's "model of the effect of my own movement on the visual field" is exactly **gyro de-rotation done right** — they don't just subtract ego-motion, they *predict* it forward. That motivates §8.4.

**Looming / τ guidance for the terminal closure.** Lee's tau theory: τ = (apparent size)/(rate of expansion) = θ/θ̇ is a *direct optical estimate of time-to-contact requiring no range*. Animals brake and dock by holding **τ̇ = constant**; at the critical value **τ̇ = 0.5 the body decelerates to arrive at contact with zero velocity**, and τ̇ < 0.5 guarantees a hard stop before contact (Lee; Extended Tau Theory for robot control — [PSU ICRA17](https://www.me.psu.edu/cheng/Documents/C%20(14)%20ICRA17_Zhao.pdf); TauPilot 4D tau-interception on rotorcraft — [tau guidance survey](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334991299)). For us, range is unobservable but **τ is observable from monocular looming** (rate of blob-area growth). This is a genuinely new control variable our stack does not use: τ from looming gives us a **passive, range-free terminal-phase clock** to schedule the "ten-tau" wall, trigger terminal aimpoint commit, and arm engage-permission — *physically* instead of by a fixed 1.0–1.4 s timer. τ-based timing is exactly the right tool because it self-scales: a faster closure expands the blob faster, shrinking τ, advancing the commit automatically.

### 8.3 Anticipatory / predictive lock — forward-model the *image*, not just the state

This is the "nobody-does-this-well" core. Our IMM forward-models the **angular state** [az, el, aż, el̇]. It does *not* forward-model the **appearance**. So every frame the tracker is *surprised* by the new scale, aspect, and signature, and the association/template stage has to re-discover the target from scratch under a changed image. That surprise is the proximate cause of pull-off and lock loss on the terminal wall.

The ideal pipeline carries a **generative image predictor** alongside the state filter: a forward model G(template_t, ΔZ) that *pre-warps* the expected target appearance for frame t+1 given the predicted closure. The physics needed is cheap and known:

- **Scale:** apparent angular size θ ∝ L/R, and although R is unobservable, **θ̇/θ = −Ṙ/R = −1/τ** is observable from looming. So the predictor scales the template by exp(Δt/τ) *without ever knowing range* — the τ from §8.2 directly drives the scale forward-model. This is the elegant coupling: looming both times the terminal phase **and** predicts the scale warp.
- **Aspect/rotation:** the IMM already estimates the LOS and the target's cross-LOS velocity; that yields a predicted aspect-rotation rate to apply an affine warp to the template.
- **Signature:** maintain a small **aspect-indexed appearance dictionary** (head-on / quarter / beam thermal templates) and interpolate by predicted aspect, so the motor/exhaust hot-spot appearing at beam aspect is *expected*, not a distractor.

The win is that the matched filter / association at t+1 runs against an image the tracker *predicted*, so residuals stay small and the gate stays tight exactly when the raw appearance is changing fastest. This is the appearance-domain analog of what the IMM does in state space, and it is the single biggest robustness lever for "never lose it from view." A learned version (a tiny conditional warp-net) is possible but a **physics-parameterized affine+scale warp driven by τ and IMM aspect needs no training data and is fully interpretable** — the preferred first implementation.

### 8.4 Self-supervised online adaptation — bounded, distractor-aware, firewalled

The target model *must* adapt during a LOBL track (the appearance genuinely changes), but naive online update is how trackers drift and get pulled onto clutter. The mature open-literature answer is **high-confidence, distractor-aware, conservative update**: only update the template when lock confidence is high *and* no competing distractor is inside the gate (KeepTrack's self-supervised distractor association; high-confidence update gating; distractor-aware response maps — [KeepTrack/cascaded regression arXiv 2006.10336](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.10336), [distractor-aware CF survey](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/s10489-021-02694-8)). The self-supervision signal is free and physical: **the IMM's own prediction is the label.** If the appearance match and the kinematic prediction agree (low NIS, tight residual), that frame is a trustworthy self-label to fold into the template; if they disagree, *freeze* the template and coast on CV — which is exactly our PREDICTIVE_TRACK behavior. So online adaptation slots into our existing FSM as "update only in LOCKED with NIS-in-gate and single candidate," never in REACQUIRE.

**Doctrine firewall.** This stays strictly legal under our invariants because adaptation writes only to the **appearance template used for re-detection and lock-quality**, never to the centroid, LOS, or λ̇ command. The learned/SSL component can lower or raise *lock-quality and engage-permission*; it can never move the aimpoint. A pulled-off or corrupted template degrades to low lock-quality → REACQUIRE/ABORT (a PASS), never to a confident wrong aimpoint (a HARD FAIL). The architecture is default-deny by construction.

### 8.5 Lock-persistence as predict-then-reacquire-the-SAME-track

Reframe every loss not as "find a target" but as "**reacquire track ID 0 inside its own covariance ellipsoid**." On loss, the IMM keeps propagating; its covariance P grows; project P into the image plane to get a **covariance-bounded search region** (a 3σ ellipse), and run the re-detector *only inside it* with the §8.3-predicted template. This is what our LOCKED→PREDICTIVE_TRACK→REACQUIRE FSM with CV coast and covariance-sized gate already gestures at — the upgrade is making the search region **explicitly the propagated P-ellipse**, search-pruned by it, and re-associating to the *same* track rather than spawning a new one. N-of-M trajectory continuity (which we have) is the acceptance test for the reacquired detection. Result: a clean, bounded, single-hypothesis reacquire that cannot wander onto a distractor outside the ellipse, and that degrades gracefully (ellipse grows → eventually HARD_LOST → ABORT/PASS).

### 8.6 The firewalled learned re-detector

A small CNN/SNN re-detector (an SNN is the natural match if we go event-based — DVS+SNN tracking at sub-mW, [PMC12526984](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12526984/)) is permitted **only** to score candidates inside the §8.5 ellipse for *lock-quality / engage-permission*. It never proposes a centroid that bypasses the deterministic detector; it only re-ranks deterministic candidates and gates permission. This is the doctrine-maximal use of learning: high recall on "is the lost target back," zero authority over where we point.

### 8.7 The ideal end-to-end pipeline

`Hybrid sensor (low-rate LWIR-intensity frame for ID + log-contrast/event motion channel)` → `gyro-predicted ego-motion de-rotation (dragonfly reafference model)` → `motion-channel detector (event clustering / synthetic-event CFAR; inherently AGC- and background-invariant)` → `covariance-gated association to single track` → `IMM angular filter [az,el,aż,el̇] (unchanged)` → `λ̇-nuller guidance at low, airframe-matched N (falcon-validated)` with `τ-from-looming scheduling the terminal commit and the scale forward-model` → `appearance forward-model G pre-warps template by τ-scale + IMM-aspect (anticipatory lock)` → `self-supervised, distractor-aware, NIS-gated template update` → `firewalled learned re-detector scoring lock-quality/engage-permission only` → `predict-then-reacquire-SAME-track on any loss inside the propagated P-ellipse`.

Why it is superior: the **guidance signal (λ̇) and the terminal clock (τ) are both invariant to the four nuisances** that dominate Task #1, the **appearance is predicted forward** so the tracker is never surprised, **adaptation is self-labeled by the filter** so it can't silently corrupt, and **every learned/adaptive element is firewalled to permission/quality**, so the worst failure is a PASS. It is faster, more invariant, and *more* default-deny than the current stack simultaneously.

### Transferable to our seeker
- **Build a software "synthetic-event" motion channel now** (per-pixel log-intensity temporal difference on de-rotated frames) and feed λ̇ preferentially from it — captures DVS-style AGC- and background-invariance on the FT640 we already own, no new hardware.
- **Add τ = area/(area-rate) from blob looming as the passive terminal-phase clock** to schedule the ten-tau commit and arm engage-permission physically (τ̇ ≈ 0.5 reference), replacing the fixed 1.0–1.4 s timer.
- **Couple τ into an anticipatory appearance forward-model**: pre-warp the template by exp(Δt/τ) for scale and by IMM-estimated aspect rate, so association runs against a *predicted* image — directly attacks pull-off on the terminal wall.
- **Adopt falcon-style low, airframe-matched navigation gain** for the λ̇-nuller (biology converges on N≈2.6 for low-g interceptors) and keep the explicit ACQUIRE-before-PN phase (A1).
- **Make online template update high-confidence + distractor-aware + NIS-gated**, self-labeled by IMM agreement, active only in LOCKED with a single in-gate candidate — frozen otherwise.
- **Make reacquire explicitly the propagated P-ellipse search for the SAME track ID**, with the learned re-detector firewalled to lock-quality/engage-permission only.

### What we may have missed
- **We track appearance reactively, never predictively** — no forward-model of scale/aspect/signature; this is the proximate cause of terminal pull-off and the biggest untapped robustness lever.
- **We have no looming/τ channel at all** — we leave a fully passive, range-free time-to-contact observable on the table while relying on a fixed terminal timer.
- **We operate purely in the intensity domain** and fight AGC/background with filters, when a log-contrast motion domain makes those nuisances *disappear* rather than be suppressed.
- **Our de-rotation subtracts ego-motion but does not predict it forward** (dragonfly reafference); predictive de-rotation tightens residuals before the measurement, not after.
- **No self-supervised online adaptation** of the target model during LOBL — the appearance genuinely changes across closure and a frozen template is a slow-leaking lock-quality drain.
- **Event-based/SNN sensing is unevaluated** as a hardware path; even if uncooled-LWIR DVS is immature, a hybrid frame+event front end is the highest-leverage long-term architecture change and should be on the roadmap as a tracked experiment.
